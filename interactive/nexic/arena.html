What is measured

The scores are Bradley–Terry strengths fitted to public votes on animal matchups, African Lion = 100. A bout's odds are the vote share Sone side / (Sone side + Sthe other). The untrained man, the spear's premium and the way groups add up are read from those votes. The anatomy correction multiplies a dinosaur's crowd score by the paper's factor.

What is judgment

Skill, armour, the weapons other than spear and club, and how much of a duellist's skill carries over against an animal are estimates with wide bands, set in step 8 of the pipeline. Tick the uncertainty box and each bout draws its odds from those bands.

How a bout is fought

The winner is drawn first, with exactly the model's share, so the long-run tally is the Bradley–Terry share by construction. The fight you watch is then drawn from a fight model until it ends that way. In that model a hit takes (attacker's strength ÷ victim's strength)0.4 of the victim's staying power, a third between equals, so a T. rex downs a man in one bite and a man needs dozens of thrusts. Who lands each exchange is solved so that one against one reproduces the share, and one tilt per side does the same for groups. A side is beaten when half its strength is down. The 0.4 and the third are staging choices; they shape the fight and not the odds.

Mixed sides, three sides

Different kinds on one side add as (Σ s1/k)k, which gives nk for n alike. With three sides each wins in proportion to its strength, the multi-way form of Bradley–Terry; the votes are all one against one, so that is an extension. When everyone on the sand is human, the odds are real odds from step 8's duel ratings and no longer vote shares.
