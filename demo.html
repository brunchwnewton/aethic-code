How it works

Signatures, classes, pruning.

Each detector watches some subset of the slits, firing if the particle takes any of them. A slit’s signature is the set of detectors that fire when only it is open; two slits with identical signatures are permanently indistinguishable and form one equivalence class. The powerset of the slits is every candidate agreeing superposition, and the postulates sort it into exactly three bins.

P2 (the blank rule) removes any subset that is a proper part of a single class — it asserts a distinction no possible record could support, so it over-specifies and dies. P3 (the pruning rule) removes any subset spanning two or more classes — a future record can tell those members apart, exposing the maintained coherence as contradictory: checkmate. What survives is the empty set and any subset that exactly matches a full equivalence class. Move a detector and the signatures redraw, re-sorting the entire powerset — the quantum–classical boundary as live re-classification. The preferred basis falls out for free: it is the detector-induced partition itself.
