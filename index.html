On the 2026 papers

Note that my most recent 2026 papers are AI written, but to be clear the core ontological ideas themselves date to 2021, 2022, and 2023. The reason I ended up using AI is because the experiment in 2024 of just writing it all in my voice sort of failed, so now I'm trying to optimize for reception clarity rather than perfect human touch. I hope to cycle back to that though after the reception period!

Also I'm happy to answer questions as they come (although I've tried to state that all explicitly in my verbatim footnotes, saying things like "I didn't think of that" whenever it happens) about what the AI did indeed contribute, because there certainly is stuff there. For example, the cautious monotony proof was Claude's, but I defined the ontology AND the algebra AND I stated it uniquely prior to Claude touching anything, AND then on top of that I went over several dozen entries with Claude where I corrected its misreadings and clarified misunderstandings as it went (for example it would ask 'do you mean A or B', and most times sure enough I would say 'neither, it's actually C'). So yeah inductive reasoning is the thing Claude can NOT do by a long shot, and also even when I give it all the outputs of that it still has mostly error toward capturing it and carrying out the inference correctly, that is until it reaches the fixed point of good inference after I clarify enough times—sort of like giving it a mini literature that it would have trained on to get that inferential context if this were a mainstream paper, but in my case the vacuum in place of a literature sort of highlights what it looks like on its own, and what that is is a state embodying conformity rather than the polar opposite mentality required for inductive reasoning or even low error. So the general rule for what it produced is deduction off of stuff I already stated in the abstract, so the deductive contents of the papers are split between me and the AI, while the inductive contents are all me.

Even recently by the way, I don't think in a million years it could have thought of the 'Nexus structure invalidated terms surviving, and we reopen those back to invalidity to bake weights in'—instead I realized that by unifying the toy example of backtracking-along-your-own-centric-unfolding with weightless-reopening, and voila! So yes that was me, and it had to be me instead of the AI because the idea has even a fragment of creativity inside of it. For an example of what Claude DID do, it'd say explicitly it was stuck on how to address the open problem—emphasis on I—told it about pertaining to allowing reopening to be compatible with restoring Aethic weights as it does in the centric unfolding-backtracking special case. It then would give me options that were purely patches, like I don't remember the particularities of it but it's always something like "what if we just write a new index stating that a parent Aethus is in a particular line" or garbage like that—so yes there's a very explicit art form to NOT just throwing in a patch, and I would say most humans have trouble with that while AI has absolute trouble with that. I've never once seen AI make the non-patch metaphorical chess move, which in a word is why I but not it could produce the framework down to every fine detail. As for why it took me until 2026 to think of that particular idea, for that you can let me have it lol—I think it's because I overwrote my 2023 intuition (present in the videos as "Aethic concatenation", if you want to fact check me on whether I had it then!) about Aethic addition when I wrote the 2024 papers, and so I conflated the LCA-based union with the overlay-based addition!

How to read the historical archive (September 2026). The materials below divide by date into two strata, and the division is worth keeping. The 2023 manuscripts and the earliest code are the framework before literature exposure: the ontology complete, the vocabulary improvised, no engagement with the counterfactual-semantics or reconstruction literatures. The 2026 papers are the same framework after that exposure and after its formalization — the long paper’s §13.11, “For the Historian,” lays out the three dated instruments and what changed between them (the apparatus, and one input) and what did not (the postulates). The Marked Paper on this page carries a table mapping the 2023 wording onto the principles as they are now named, so the correspondence can be checked phrase by phrase.

Algorithm

Python — bare-bones invalidation

The core classification algorithm in plain Python with no dependencies. Computes detector signatures, equivalence classes, and labels each powerset element Valid, P2, or P3. The full source is under “View source”. What it implements: the Powerset Invalidation Theorem of Quantum Mechanics from Records for a finite slit set under a supplied detector configuration, with each detector's signature taken as given (OR logic over the slits it watches). Assumptions it takes as input: that the supplied signatures are the complete record structure of the configuration and persist under refinement, which the theorem requires and the program cannot check. Conventions: the empty subset is returned as Valid by convention; a subset spanning two signature classes is P3 (consistency-pruned); a subset inside one class but short of it is P2 (over-specified); a full class is Valid. Expected output: a label for every element of the powerset, 2n entries for n slits. Limitation: it implements a finite classification under supplied record assumptions; it does not establish those assumptions for any actual laboratory, and it computes no weights, which the kernel supplies separately.

Python source · aethic_invalidation.py + 
from itertools import chain, combinations

def powerset(iterable):
    s = list(iterable)
    return chain.from_iterable(combinations(s, r) for r in range(len(s) + 1))

def classify(n_slits, detectors):
    """
    n_slits   : number of slits (indices 0..n_slits-1)
    detectors : list of sets; each set is the slits one detector watches (OR logic)
    Returns {subset: label} for every element of the powerset of slits,
    where label is "VALID", "P2" (too narrow), or "P3" (too broad).
    """
    # 1. Each slit's signature = which detectors fire when only it is open.
    signature = {
        s: tuple(i for i, d in enumerate(detectors) if s in d)
        for s in range(n_slits)
    }

    # 2. Group slits by identical signature into equivalence classes.
    classes = {}
    for s in range(n_slits):
        classes.setdefault(signature[s], set()).add(s)

    def class_of(s):
        return signature[s]

    # 3. Classify every subset of the powerset.
    result = {}
    for subset in powerset(range(n_slits)):
        subset = frozenset(subset)
        if not subset:
            result[subset] = "VALID"                      # empty set
            continue
        spanned = {class_of(s) for s in subset}
        if len(spanned) > 1:
            result[subset] = "P3"                         # spans classes: checkmate
        else:
            full = classes[next(iter(spanned))]
            result[subset] = "VALID" if subset == full else "P2"
    return result

if __name__ == "__main__":
    # Double slit, one detector on slit 0: {A} and {B} valid, {A,B} checkmated.
    for subset, label in classify(2, [{0}]).items():
        shown = "".join(chr(65 + s) for s in sorted(subset)) or "Ø"
        print(f"{shown:<6} {label}")
The core algorithm, as pseudocode +

Given n slits and a set of detectors (each fires for some subset of slits): 1. For each slit, compute its detector signature: sig(s) = which detectors fire when only s is open 2. Group slits by identical signature into equivalence classes. Slits in the same class are observationally indistinguishable. 3. For each subset S of the powerset (each candidate superposition): if S = ∅ → VALID if S = one full class → VALID (no contradiction reachable) if S ⊂ one class (proper) → P2 (specificity no observer can access) if S spans multiple classes → P3 (a future observer could find contradiction)

Historical · the priority instrument

The Original Aethic Paper (May 2023)

My original Aethic reasoning paper (May 20, 2023), included here to close the gap as best I can about what it looks like in practice when you have really deep structural intuitions but only normal ability for pedagogy-in-writing. Aethic reasoning was actually complete by May 8, 2023, when I came up with the two-general constraint to capture the self-similarity in an Oliver's Aethus — the full richness was already swimming inside my head, fully matured, but I could not for the life of me find the words to explain it.

The curse, and how it broke +

The issue was not that I didn't understand the formalism, because from my perspective it was totally captured — the problem was that I couldn't see it from the reader's perspective, down to every last little confusion and nuance like the unpacking of the union principle. That unpacking is not about me or the principle — it's about forming the mapping between a standard human worldview and the principle, but either is a variable in that function. If I were explaining this to some alien race, then simply put it would not be broken down the way it was, because they would have come to the conversation with a different set of initial assumptions.

So the curse of it was that I A) had no one to talk to about what was going on that understood abstract math, because I couldn't get a single professor to engage with me, and B) genuinely lacked the raw pedagogical skill to convey the punch (and yes I say pedagogical rather than formalistic, because when you're in that deep formalistic is a subclass of pedagogical). The miracle of 2024 then was two things: A) I met Dr. Fokoué and he single-handedly saved me from devastation by engaging, and B) something just clicked at the exact right time in my brain, weirdly, like on the timescale of a month, where suddenly I felt like I could decompose mathematical structures into prose better — almost like a kind of intangible precocious maturation that was late to the party or something. The result is that as I read the paper back, I feel this guttural sense of relief in seeing how far I came, because the level to which I was screwed in the moment (due to the pedagogical inability) is hard to overstate.

On the meeting with Dr. Fokoué +

Also, interestingly, no exposition in the paper, direct or abstract, came from the meeting itself with Dr. Fokoué. What was important about the meeting is that it gave me an entry point to latch onto toward the explanation. I actually met Dr. Fokoué in February I think it was of 2024, and I had been trying several times during conversations with him to explain even one Aethic concept, which finally worked spontaneously on a day in September. I had been writing about 120 pages of the paper in parallel with this, mind you, (due to that sudden formalization ability kicking in), so what happened during the meeting was that I got the definitional hierarchy across to him precisely, (by explaining it with the n-Aethus concept), as well as the superposition-from-blanks concept in the basic sense (ie no mixed decompositions), the extrusion principle table which has Copenhagen as the diagonal (which he found stunning, because it directly connected back to an unrelated observation he made earlier that day about how old paradigms tend to look like subspaces of new ones), and a little bit about probability as weighted Aethae, which you can imagine really piqued his interests, as a statistician! I then got a second concept across to him two weeks later, being the mixed-decomposition premise (which handles edge cases of superpositions): and what's especially interesting is that using my vocabulary at the time of "present versus absent", he had extrapolated a different dependency-graph for how Aethic blanks worked than what I had, so this difference in orientation was itself deeply informative for me to invent the decomposable-vocab. I then did a full rewrite of those two sections in the paper, since as we know being able to transmit the abstraction directly instead of a handful of concrete projections is absolute gold for the framework's existential fate. So, I managed to convey to him maybe 10% of the framework's own direct contents, and really none of the connectives between the contents (which are by weight most of it anyway), but that was more than enough for me to extrapolate the entire structure of the paper around it. That is, the first 1% of that kind of learner-locating context is more crucial than the next 70% combined kind of thing! I think, in fairness, that the fact I was able to pull off this extrapolation itself stems from my same cognitive-expansion that allowed me to extrapolate even the framework itself from the double-slit experiment plus macroscopic world as two inductive boundary conditions of sorts, so once again it's just a sheer miracle that I was there with the chops to do this kind of feat when I needed to. Like, epistemically it was bordering on humanly impossible, but had dropped down several orders of magnitude and so now was at least achievable.

The Marked Paper annotates this manuscript line by line — each phrase linked to the principle it was already reaching for — to show the whole framework was present in 2023, just unable to be named. A study in linguistic masking, and a quiet argument for listening harder to the next person in that position.

The correspondence archive preserves this work's full contact with the institutional record, 2023–2026: the DKIM-signed arXiv moderation exchange of May 2023 — the account confirmed May 8, the day the framework was completed, and the decline that asked "Do you have a conventional publication record?" — through the Foundations of Physics and Synthese decisions (the editor's seventeen-reviewers line, signed and verbatim), the Quantum gate-cascade exchange, the preprint declines, and the endorsement chain Dr. Fokoué and an RIT physics colleague answered. Originals, manifest, checksums.

Note · a documented convergence

Koide Formula Parametrization Rediscovery

A short paper illustrating the "principle of twos" — the hypothesis that the Born rule's squared amplitudes, chiral decomposition, and the lepton mass spectrum share a common two-factor structure — by deriving a trigonometric parametrization of the Koide formula with cyclic 120° phase shifts.

After posting, I learned that the core parametrization (mass scale + phase angle, cyclic eigenvector structure, δ ≈ 2/9) was previously obtained by Carl Brannen in 2006 via a circulant-matrix approach! The present paper arrived at the same result independently. It is retained here to keep a record of that convergence, but there is no use having it on PhilArchive still. The sweet irony, anyway, is that I sort of April Foolsed myself here!

Why I searched after timestamping +

Ok as for why I actually posted first, then searched, it's just because in the scenario where I was actually first, I wanted to make sure I had that timestamp before letting any LLM catch a sight of the idea. I have enough equations and scraps of equations floating around at the moment that my policy is to universally keep all of them out of sight of LLMs until I get a timestamp. Since I'm working in isolation, I probably miscalibrated how easy it was to discover this, because I didn't anticipate there was a formalistic path in addition to my own ontological path. So my bad there! But yeah also it was just a better safe than sorry thing, because I'm a little paranoid about somebody trying to scoop me or something if the LLM logs the equation and they manage to publicize it before I can get my timestamp!
