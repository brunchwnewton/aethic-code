The **canonical space** $E$ is the axiom; the middle space is *derived* from it by quotienting a circle; and the middle space then has **two** four-dimensional readouts, which earlier versions ran together — one where matter and light are localized, one where gravity acts.

> $$\mathbb{C}^\times\ \longrightarrow\ SL(2,\mathbb{C})\ \longrightarrow\ Q^2_{\rm aff}\cong SL(2,\mathbb{C})/\mathbb{C}^\times$$
> whose compact real form is the ordinary Hopf fibration $U(1)\to SU(2)\simeq S^3\to S^2$. **So the Hopf character is no longer decoration. It is the compact real form of the complex spin geometry belonging to an isotropic shell**, and it was the right guess for the wrong reason in every version that assumed it.

> $$\boxed{\ \text{spin geometry fixes the fiber's topology;}\qquad\text{gravity fixes only its metric length.}\ }$$
> These are different kinds of information. The spin structure says what the fiber **is** — $U(1)\hookrightarrow SU(2)\simeq S^3\to S^2$, the **unit Hopf class** — and Law I says how large that already-existing circle is at each base point, $L_H = \mathcal{F}[g_G]$. This is not a loss of content but the removal of an inconsistency — and it takes two free parameters with it.

# PART 0 — VOCABULARY AND THE HIERARCHY

## 0.0 The leading intuitions [D]

*The theory grew from a handful of intuitions. The formal parts sometimes carry an intuition further than its first statement, sometimes find a different form for it, and sometimes retire it. This section states each one plainly, then says what the mathematics made of it and where.*

**1. Light resolves a representative; gravity resolves a class.** The seed. A point of space can appear twice to matter and light — as itself and as its twin — while gravity sees only the pair. *Made into:* two bases, the representational one where matter and light live and the gravity base that sums over each pair (Law I); and, in its cleanest form, the twin theorem of §IV.1, where one gravitational source on the quotient simply *is* a same-sign pair on the cover. *Status:* the architecture.

**2. The other side of the quotient is dark.** The twin carries its source's gravity but none of its matter, and so none of its light — a stand-in for dark matter. *Made into:* the barren twin, dark because it is barren, not because it is remote (§V.3), with its electromagnetic barrenness bounded by lunar ranging (§X.13). *Status:* established within the architecture, its assumptions stated.

**3. Change the shells, not the tangent spaces.** Rather than add structure to spacetime's tangent spaces, let the shells around each source carry an extra circle — the first gesture toward the Hopf fiber — and let a rotating hole be a source tilted against that grain. *Made into:* the complex spin lift of Part I, where the shell's circle turns out to *be* the spinor's phase (§I.6), and the complex displacement of Part VI, which captures Kerr's complex structure though not, alone, all of Kerr. *Status:* verified for the phase; partial for Kerr.

**4. Time as what is not quotiented.** Time might be the one direction of the deeper structure that is never quotiented away. *Made into:* three attempted routes, none successful. *Status:* open; time is still an input (§XI.2).

**5. A galaxy fills one hemisphere of a small closed universe.** A galaxy occupies one hemisphere of a closed three-sphere and its dark twin the other, and an exponential falloff of mass from one point makes the rotation curve flatten — flat curves as a closure effect rather than a halo. *Made into:* the galactic arena, one galaxy with no cosmological slot (the note on scale), and the plateau $v^2 = 2.994\,GM_{\rm grav}/\ell$ (§IV.8). *Status:* the plateau is verified; what supplies the arena's closure background, and how light sees past its distance cap, are the two open structural problems (§XI.2).

**6. Charge as a signed imbalance.** Reissner–Nordström might be the small-charge face of a projective effect: two chiral pieces whose balance is Schwarzschild and whose full imbalance is an extremal hole. *Made into:* the factor-weight picture of §X.0 — mass the sum of two weights, charge their signed difference, gravity blind to the sign. *Status:* exact as the algebra of Reissner–Nordström; the readout hypothesis is open.

**7. Other galaxies through apparent scale.** A galaxy's apparent size may differ from its actual size, and the mismatch may be what produces Tully–Fisher's square root. *Made into:* a detached wing, scoped but deliberately not attached (Part XII). *Status:* exploratory.

**8. The observed event is rigid downstairs; its canonical realizations are flexible upstairs.** Two canonical alternatives remain one observed event when their positions project to exactly the same point and their directions agree to within the resolution of whatever physical channel could record a difference — a camera being only an example of such a channel. *Made into:* Part XIII's canonical optics — two agreement rules, a readout map, and a connection whose holonomy separates the canonical endpoints of one observed event. *Status:* definitions and constraints; the key finding is that only phase-type holonomy survives inside standard quantum mechanics (§XIII.3).

**9. One thing can appear twice.** One underlying point may show up at two places in an output space, each appearance keeping a different part of a common structure — like a logo with different parts quotiented away, read as two logos. Nobody would have noticed, because each appearance keeps something the other lacks. *Made into:* rendering as a correspondence, and agreeing structure as what both appearances retain (§XIII.4); the seed is its first instance. *Status:* definitions.

**10. Fields are what failed to unpack.** A *centrade* can be packed and unpacked: fully unpacked in the Kerr direction it has no angular momentum, in the Reissner–Nordström direction no charge. Unpackings conflict, "whack-a-mole", so there is a budget. *Made into:* admissible unpackings as a non-product set, and fields as residual obstructions, with standard precedents (§XIII.5). *Status:* conjecture, with a verified miniature in the Hopf-sign budget of §V.4.

**11. Our spacetime is one rendering among many.** Each global unpacking scheme outputs its own visual spacetime; ours is privileged by the sheer number of microstates and the relational complexity it supports, not by the laws. A hexagonal sheet of richly linked nodes, each carrying an infinite single-file tower, would hide the towers from its inhabitants. *Made into:* a renderer, multiplicity, and relational complexity as the criterion of visibility (§XIII.6). *Status:* conjecture; what stays invariant across renderings is open.

**12. The galactic plane is a fixed set, and spirals are rendered.** The stress-energy upstairs may be roughly isotropic, with the galactic plane the fixed set of an involution; and a star at a turning point of its orbit might be rendered on the opposite side, making rosettes look like spirals. *Made into:* the involution fixing a great two-sphere, and the two readings of the orbital flip, computed (§XIII.7). *Status:* the fixed set is standard; neither simple flip makes spirals from a symmetric disk, though a handedness flip freezes each rosette into a stationary spiral segment.

**A method, not an intuition: stay conservative.** Where a choice is not forced, keep the smaller assumption — an ordinary one-form photon, a twin empty of matter, an isotropic galaxy profile — until the evidence decides.

## 0.4 What is axiomatic

The primitive data are $\big(E,\ \hat g,\ U(1)_H,\ \mathcal{A}_H,\ \rho_E,\ \widehat{\mathcal{U} }_{\rm mer}\big)$ — **all living on $E$**: the canonical space, its metric, the fiber action, its connection, the real-analytic structure needed to complexify shells, and an upstairs meridian datum.

That last is a rank-one structure $\widehat{\mathcal{U} }_{\rm mer}\subset TE$, regular away from the preimage of a controlled singular set, which is (i) **$U(1)_H$-invariant**, $(R_\theta)_*\widehat{\mathcal{U} }_{\rm mer} = \widehat{\mathcal{U} }_{\rm mer}$, and (ii) **transverse to the fibers**, $\widehat{\mathcal{U} }_{\rm mer}\cap\ker d\pi_H = 0$. Invariance makes its image independent of the point chosen on each fiber; transversality keeps that image rank one. So it is projectable, and the middle space's meridian structure is *defined* as the image:

$$\mathcal{U}_{\rm mer} = d\pi_H\big(\widehat{\mathcal{U} }_{\rm mer}\big)$$

Everything else — the middle space, its meridian structure, both bases, the seed — then genuinely **descends**:

$$E\ \Longrightarrow\ (\mathcal{M},\ \mathcal{U}_{\rm mer})\ \Longrightarrow\ B_{\rm grav}$$

**The theory is top-down**, functorially and not just rhetorically. *(A previous revision listed $\mathcal{U}_{\rm mer}$ itself among the primitives while calling it "the meridian structure of the quotient" — one layer too low, since the quotient is not primitive.)*

**The real structure $\rho_E$, defined [S/T].** $E$ is real-analytic, so it sits inside a complexification $E_\mathbb{C}$, carrying an **antiholomorphic involution**

$$\rho_E:E_\mathbb{C}\to E_\mathbb{C},\qquad\rho_E^2 = 1,\qquad E = \operatorname{Fix}(\rho_E)$$

For reality to descend through the tower, $\rho_E$ must satisfy two compatibilities. **With the fiber:** writing $\mathbb{C}^\times$ for the complexified fiber group, $\rho_E(\lambda\cdot x) = \bar\lambda^{-1}\cdot\rho_E(x)$. The map $\lambda\mapsto\bar\lambda^{-1}$ fixes exactly $|\lambda| = 1$ **[V]**, so the compact $U(1)_H$ — and nothing larger — preserves $E$: the physical real form of §I.4, stated as a condition rather than observed. **With the meridian datum:** $d\rho_E$ preserves the complexification of $\widehat{\mathcal{U} }_{\rm mer}$, so its real part is what descends. *Whether $E_\mathbb{C}$ is primitive — the complex-first reading — or merely the complexification of a real-analytic $E$ is a choice this version need not make locally, where the two agree. It matters globally: a real-analytic manifold has a complexification only as a germ, so the complex-first reading makes the global-$E$ problem (§XI.2, item 1) a problem about constructing a global $E_\mathbb{C}$* **[O]**.

*What the upstairs datum carries [T].* Lifts of a given line field downstairs are not unique: two lifts differ by a $U(1)_H$-invariant vertical component, which projects to zero. The lift horizontal with respect to $\mathcal{A}_H$ is canonical, and choosing it adds nothing beyond the connection already present.

# PART I — THE COMPLEX SHELL AND ITS SPIN LIFT

*This part supplies the fiber. It is the version's substantive addition, and it is the reason the Hopf structure is no longer an assumption.*

## I.1 The complex worldline and its normal space [T/S]

Let $\Gamma_\mathbb{C} = \{\gamma(\tau)\}$ be the analytic complex worldline of a source, with non-null tangent, $g_\mathbb{C}(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)\neq0$. At each $\gamma(\tau)$ the **complex normal space** is

$$N_\gamma = \{\xi : g_\mathbb{C}(\xi,\dot\gamma) = 0\}$$

complex three-dimensional — the complexification of the ordinary spatial rest space about a real timelike worldline.

## I.2 A complex shell is an affine quadric [T]

A shell of nonzero complex squared radius $R^2$ is

$$\Sigma^\mathbb{C}_{\gamma,R} = \{\xi\in N_\gamma : g_\mathbb{C}(\xi,\xi) = R^2\}$$

Choosing an orthonormal complex frame and rescaling,

$$\Sigma^\mathbb{C}_{\gamma,R}\ \cong\ Q^2_{\rm aff} = \{(z_1,z_2,z_3)\in\mathbb{C}^3 : z_1^2+z_2^2+z_3^2 = 1\}$$

**a complex surface, not a real 2-sphere.** Its symmetry group is the complex rotation group $SO(3,\mathbb{C})$, whose spin double cover is $Spin(3,\mathbb{C})\cong SL(2,\mathbb{C})$.

## I.3 The complex Hopf lift [T/V]

$SO(3,\mathbb{C})$ acts **transitively** on the affine quadric — any non-null vector of given norm rotates to any other — with stabilizer the complex torus $SO(2,\mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{C}^\times$. Lifting to the double cover:

$$\boxed{\ \mathbb{C}^\times\ \hookrightarrow\ SL(2,\mathbb{C})\ \twoheadrightarrow\ Q^2_{\rm aff}\cong SL(2,\mathbb{C})/\mathbb{C}^\times\ }$$

Checked by dimension: $\dim_\mathbb{R}SL(2,\mathbb{C}) - \dim_\mathbb{R}\mathbb{C}^\times = 6-2 = 4 = \dim_\mathbb{R}Q^2_{\rm aff}$ **[V]**.

**This is the complex Hopf shell.** The old Hopfian character survives complexification not by pretending the complex shell is still a 2-sphere, but by complexifying the *homogeneous-space relation itself*.

## I.4 The physical real form is exactly the Hopf fibration [T/V]

For a timelike source on the Lorentzian real slice, the normal rest space is ordinary Euclidean three-space, and each piece passes to its compact real form:

| complex | real | check |
|:--|:--|:--|
| $Q^2_{\rm aff}$ | $S^2$ | $z_1^2+z_2^2+z_3^2 = 1$ with $z$ real *is* the unit 2-sphere |
| $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ | $SU(2)\simeq S^3$ | maximal compact subgroup |
| $\mathbb{C}^\times$ | $U(1)$ | maximal compact subgroup |

$$\boxed{\ U(1)\ \hookrightarrow\ SU(2)\simeq S^3\ \twoheadrightarrow\ S^2\ }$$

with $3-1 = 2 = \dim S^2$ **[V]**. So the earlier versions' assertion — *every spherical shell lifts to a Hopf 3-sphere* — is **recovered as the physical real form** of the complex statement, rather than imposed before complexification.

## I.5 Why this is isotropic [T/D]

The shell is defined by the invariant condition $g_\mathbb{C}(\xi,\xi) = R^2$ alone. The rotation group acts transitively on it, so no angular direction is preferred; and the lift $SL(2,\mathbb{C})\to SL(2,\mathbb{C})/\mathbb{C}^\times$ is likewise homogeneous. **The fiber is not attached by choosing a longitude. It is the stabilizer of the spin-group action.** On the real form, $SU(2)\to SU(2)/U(1)\simeq S^2$ is equally isotropic under ordinary rotations — which is the precise sense in which the magnetic/isotropic motivation of the original picture is preserved.

## I.6 The shell fiber *is* the spinor phase [T/V]

*Noting that the same group appears twice would be a coincidence-of-names argument. The identification can be made explicit, and it is an equality of **actions**, not of group labels.*

$SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ is already the spin group of four-dimensional Lorentzian two-spinor geometry — the group under which Newman–Penrose spinors transform. The question is whether the $U(1)$ that the shell quotients is the *same* circle as the spinor's phase. It is.

**The construction.** Identify a normalized spinor $\psi = (z_1,z_2)^{\mathsf T}\in S^3\subset\mathbb{C}^2$ with the group element

$$U(\psi) = \begin{pmatrix} z_1 & -\bar z_2\\ z_2 & \bar z_1\end{pmatrix}\ \in\ SU(2)$$

which is unitary with unit determinant precisely because $|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2 = 1$ **[V]**. Now act on the right by the diagonal stabilizer

$$h_\theta = \begin{pmatrix} e^{i\theta} & 0\\ 0 & e^{-i\theta}\end{pmatrix}$$

The first column of $U(\psi)h_\theta$ is $e^{i\theta}\psi$ **[V]**.

> **Given the timelike direction $u$ that defines the physical rest-space shell and its $SU(2)$ stabilizer, the shell's $U(1)$ stabilizer acts on a normalized spinor as ordinary spinor phase.** Not the same group twice — *the same action*.

**Relative to what [T/V].** The qualifier is not decorative. $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ does not preserve $|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2$: the boost $\operatorname{diag}(e^{\eta/2},e^{-\eta/2})$ takes the unit spinor $\psi = (0,1)$ to norm $e^{-\eta/2}$ — $0.670$ at $\eta = 0.8$ — while $SU(2)$ keeps every unit spinor at $1$ **[V]**. (For unit $\psi$ the boosted norm ranges over $[e^{-\eta/2},e^{\eta/2}]$.) The positive-definite spinor norm is $u_{AA'}\psi^A\bar\psi^{A'}$ and needs a timelike $u$; $SU(2)$ is exactly its stabilizer. So "normalized," "$S^3\subset\mathbb{C}^2$" and "phase" are all defined relative to $u$ — **the same $u$ whose stabilizer is the diagonal $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ of §XI.3.** Stated this way §I.6 and §XI.3 are one fact seen twice, rather than an observer-independent claim sitting beside an observer-dependent one.

**And the Hopf map is the quotient by exactly that action [V].** With $n(\psi) = \big(2\,\mathrm{Re}(z_1\bar z_2),\,2\,\mathrm{Im}(z_1\bar z_2),\,|z_1|^2-|z_2|^2\big)$, one has $n(e^{i\theta}\psi) = n(\psi)$ identically — verified. So the shell $S^2$ is the space of spinors modulo phase, and the fiber is the phase.

**The complex version is the same statement [V].** $h_\lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\lambda^{-1})\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ scales the first column by $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^\times$ — verified. So the complex stabilizer is complex phase-and-scale on the spinor, and the compact real form recovers pure phase.

**This is the "not bolted on" claim, discharged.** Every earlier version had to *posit* a circle. Here the circle is the spinor phase of a group the theory already contains, acting in the way it already acts.

## I.7 What the complex shell is **not** [T]

At a point of complexified four-dimensional spacetime, the projectivized complex null cone is a different object — a projective quadric built from two independent chiral spinors, natural for **null directions**. The shell used here represents **fixed nonzero radius in the complexified rest space of a non-null worldline**. These must not be conflated:

$$\text{null-direction quadric}\ \neq\ \text{fixed-radius worldline shell}$$

The affine quadric is preferred because its three pieces complexify *together* and its real limits are exactly the three wanted (§I.4). The projective version does not have the $S^2$ limit for a source shell.

# PART II — THE CANONICAL SPACE AND THE TWO BASES

## II.1 Law 0 [S/O]

> ### ◆ LAW 0 — THE CANONICAL SPACE
> There exists a canonical space $E$ such that: (i) its quotient by the compact fiber action is the middle space, $\mathcal{M} = E/U(1)_H$; (ii) near a source worldline, its analytic continuation restricted to every nonzero-radius complex shell is the homogeneous bundle $\mathbb{C}^\times\to SL(2,\mathbb{C})\to Q^2_{\rm aff}$; (iii) on the physical rest-space shell this reduces to $U(1)\to SU(2)\to S^2$; (iv) the fiber carries a connection $\mathcal{A}_H$; (v) the metric $\hat g$ gives the compact fiber a length $L_H$.

**Two obstructions, and the second is the real problem.**

**(a) The cohomological one [V].** Condition (iii) cannot hold over an *unpunctured* static middle space: $H^2(S^4) = 0$, so every circle bundle over $\mathbb{R}_t\times S^4$ is trivial, and a trivial bundle restricts to $S^2\times S^1$ on each shell — **not** $S^3$. The Hopf lift needs a nontrivial class on the shell, which needs the secondary meridians removed. What the argument requires is precisely the **cohomological** statement, and no more: by Alexander duality,

$$H^2\big(S^4\setminus S^1;\mathbb{Z}\big)\ \cong\ \tilde H_1(S^1;\mathbb{Z})\ \cong\ \mathbb{Z}$$

which permits a nontrivial circle bundle **[T]**. More is true, though not needed: smooth circles in $S^4$ are **unknotted** — homotopic embeddings of an $n$-manifold in an $m$-manifold are isotopic once $m\geq2n+2$, here $4\geq4$, and $\pi_1(S^4) = 0$ makes every circle homotopic to every other. Since $S^4 = (S^1\times D^3)\cup_{S^1\times S^2}(D^2\times S^2)$, the complement of a tubular neighbourhood is $D^2\times S^2\simeq S^2$ **[T]**. *A previous revision of this section claimed smooth 1-knots in $S^4$ exist and treated the homotopy statement as an embedding debt. That was wrong and is retired (§XI.1); knotting in $S^4$ begins with embedded 2-spheres.* Alexander duality nonetheless remains all the argument uses.

**With $N$ secondaries the removed set is a graph [T].** In the static pencil every meridian passes through both poles, so $\Gamma$ has two vertices and $2N$ arcs, and

$$H^2\big(S^4\setminus\Gamma;\mathbb{Z}\big)\cong\tilde H_1(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2N-1}$$

— one integer per arc, less one relation. That relation forces the Hopf classes at all crossings to sum to zero — a pairing for a single meridian, one global constraint for several (§V.4). **Law 0 must therefore be stated over $\mathcal{M}$ minus the secondary meridians**; the architecture inherits that surgery rather than escaping it.

**(b) The global one, which is now the main construction problem [O].** Part I builds its shell on the **normal space** $N_\gamma$ — a space of *vectors*, not of spacetime points. Passing from normal vectors to nearby points of the middle space uses the exponential map, and that identification is **local**: curvature, cut loci and caustics obstruct it globally. So even granting (a), it does not follow that **one** global six-dimensional $E$ restricts to the unit Hopf bundle around **every** source worldline simultaneously.

> **That is the version's central open construction**, and it is a sharper problem than the ones it replaces. The local spin geometry of Part I is settled mathematics; whether it glues is not.

## II.2 The middle space is derived [D]

$$\mathcal{M} = E/U(1)_H$$

Nothing in this version may treat $\mathcal{M}$ as more fundamental than $E$. The quotient discards the spin phase while retaining the meridian-completed localization geometry. **This is the first loss of information in the tower.**

## II.3 The representational base is a bisection, not a section [S/O]

$B_{\rm rep}$ is an embedded Lorentzian hypersurface $\iota:B_{\rm rep}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}$ where matter is localized, Maxwell fields live, detectors receive light, and Newman–Janis is interpreted.

In the old static picture, the equatorial $S^3$ met each meridian at **two** points, $\hat n$ and $-\hat n$. The general statement is

$$L_b\cap B_{\rm rep} = \{x,\ \tau x\},\qquad \tau^2 = 1,\qquad q(x) = q(\tau x) = b$$

so $B_{\rm rep}$ is a **bisection**: two points per gravity fiber, not one. **And the second point may be barren.** This is where the seed lives, and stating it as a bisection rather than a section is what makes the seed a consequence instead of a postulate.

## II.4 The gravity base is a quotient [S]

$$B_{\rm grav} = \mathcal{M}/U_{\rm mer}$$

Gravity does not ask which representative in $q^{-1}(b)$ holds the matter. It asks only for the reduced source assigned to the class.

# PART III — THE LAWS

> ### ◆ LAW I — GRAVITY
> $$G_{\mu\nu}[g_G] + \Lambda g^G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\,T^{(g)}_{\mu\nu}\qquad\text{on } B_{\rm grav},\qquad \Lambda = 1/\ell^2\ \ \text{(arena constant)}$$
> with the source obtained by meridian reduction of resolved matter. Writing $r := q\circ\iota : B_{\rm rep}\to B_{\rm grav}$, which away from the singular set is a local diffeomorphism on each branch of the bisection, the reduction is
> $$T^{(g)}_b = \sum_{x\in r^{-1}(b)}\big((dr_x)^{-1}\big)^*T_x$$
> up to whatever density factor an action supplies. **The map $dr$ is what makes the sum well-typed**: tensors at $x$ and $\tau x$ live in different tangent spaces and cannot be added until the quotient supplies the identification. The shorthand $T^{(g)}([x]) = T(x)+T(\tau x)$ is this expression with that step suppressed. The same solution fixes the canonical fiber length (§III.2). Law I introduces **no additional scalar degree of freedom** — no dilaton, no radion, nothing with a $1/r$ coupling. **But recovery of the observed PPN parameters does not follow from that alone here**, because photons and detectors live on $B_{\rm rep}$ while Law I solves on $B_{\rm grav}$: it additionally requires the induced metric on $B_{\rm rep}$ to reproduce general relativity's optical metric, and the map $g_G\to g_{\rm rep}$ has not been derived **[O]**.

> ### ◆ LAW II — THE MERIDIANS
> The middle space carries a one-dimensional **meridian structure** $U_{\rm mer}$, regular as a foliation away from controlled singular sets. (In the static limit it is a *pencil*: distinct meridians meet at the poles, so it is not a foliation of all of $S^4$ — §VIII.1.) The representational base resolves its representatives; gravity quotients it. In the static spherical limit it must reduce to the great circles of $S^4$ through both poles, with quotient $\mathbb{RP}^3$. The general equation selecting the meridian structure is **open** **[O]**.

> ### ◆ STRUCTURE III — THE CANONICAL CONNECTION
> *Numbered III for continuity with earlier versions, but **not a law**: there is no field equation here, and calling it one would misdescribe the document's own architecture.*
> The canonical fiber carries a connection $\mathcal{A}_H$ with curvature $F_H = d\mathcal{A}_H$. Its **topological type is fixed by the spin geometry**: every relevant linking shell carries a unit class, $|c_1| = 1$, with total space $S^3$. The **sign** is induced globally — by the chosen orientation, the embedding, and the restriction map — and is neither chosen shell by shell nor set by whether matter is present; in particular, a single meridian's two crossings carry *opposite* induced signs, while with several meridians only the total over all crossings is forced to vanish (§V.4). It is independent of the source's mass and of any parameter. The reference connection is the canonical Hopf connection, and deformations away from it leave $c_1$ untouched, since $\int_{S^2}d\alpha = 0$. In an adapted metric they appear as **two local connection cross-components**, whose gauge-invariant content is fixed only after quotienting the $U(1)$ gauge freedom $A\mapsto A+d\lambda$ (§III.3). **Nothing in this version sources them, and no matter-coupling law is asserted** **[O]**.

> ### ◆ LAW IV — ELECTROMAGNETISM
> Maxwell on $B_{\rm rep}$, with $\epsilon = \mu_{\rm EM} = 1$. Light resolves meridian representatives and does **not** perform the gravity quotient. The field lives downstairs, so its pullback to $E$ is **$U(1)_H$-invariant** — it does not couple to the fiber. (Earlier drafts called it a "zero mode," which implies a Kaluza–Klein expansion this version does not perform; see §XI.1.)

## III.2 Law I locks the fiber length [S]

In the conformal/static sector, with $g_{\rm spatial} = \Omega^2g_{\rm ref}$ and $\Omega = \phi^2$:

$$L_H = \Omega\,L_H^{\rm ref},\qquad\text{so to first order}\qquad \frac{\delta L_H}{L_H} = -\Phi$$

> **To first order, the fiber-length increase tracks the Newtonian potential**, and space stretches as much as time slows. The qualifier is not decorative: the relation parts company with Schwarzschild at $O(m^2/r^2)$, as the scope box below shows.

> **Gravity does not create the fiber — the spin geometry supplies it (Part I). Gravity determines its metric size.**

**The direction, stated carefully [D/O].** The defensible relation is **one-way**:

$$g_G\ \longmapsto\ L_H = \mathcal{F}[g_G]$$

Law I solves the base geometry; the canonical metric is then constrained so that its fiber length records part of that solution.

> **A two-way relation would be much stronger, and is not claimed.** $g_G$ is a metric; $L_H$ is a single scalar function. One scalar cannot in general reconstruct four-dimensional geometry — this document itself separately carries a lapse $N$, a conformal factor $\phi$, and a meridian scale $\kappa$ (§IV.5). A statement $L_H\Leftrightarrow g_G$ would hold only in a restricted one-function sector, and no such sector has been isolated here **[O]**.

**And the future emergence claim is a different one.** If an action is later exhibited in which varying the *full* canonical metric yields Einstein's equation downstairs, what that would establish is

$$\hat g\ \Longrightarrow\ g_G$$

**not** $L_H\Rightarrow g_G$. The six-dimensional metric carries far more than its fiber length, and conflating the two would claim emergence from a scalar that only emergence from the whole metric could support. That distinction is likely to matter later.

*Scope.* This is a first-order statement. Isotropic Schwarzschild has $N = (1-m/2r)/(1+m/2r)$ while $N = \Omega^{-1}$ would require $(1+m/2r)^{-2}$; expanding, $1-m/r+m^2/2r^2$ against $1-m/r+3m^2/4r^2$ — they agree to first order and part at $O(m^2/r^2)$, with $N\Omega = 1-m^2/4r^2$ **[V]**. Imposing $N\Omega = 1$ exactly gives the Majumdar–Papapetrou form and hence extremal Reissner–Nordström, $Q = M$ — every body an extremal black hole, **excluded** **[V/T]** as a *universal* condition. As a *characterization* it is exact: $N\Omega = 1$ holds precisely for extremal holes (§X.2). Deriving the locking from an action, rather than imposing it, is open **[O]**.

## III.3 The metric-component decomposition, on the physical real shell [V/S]

A metric on the three-manifold $S^3$ has $3\cdot4/2 = 6$ components. Adapted to the circle bundle $S^3\to S^2$ they decompose as below. **This is a block count of metric components, not of physical propagating degrees of freedom** — gauge and diffeomorphism freedom would have to be removed for that, and is not:

| block | count | content |
|:--|--:|:--|
| shell 2-metric | 3 | the base geometry, from Law I |
| fiber length $L_H$ | 1 | the gravitational fiber scale, from Law I |
| connection cross-components | 2 | **open** — and *local components*, not gauge-invariant content |
| **total** | **6** | ✓ **[V]** |

So the attractive $3+1+2$ partition survives — **on the physical real slice**. Version 15 does not assert it for the full complex shell; the complex homogeneous bundle is the analytic parent, and the real slice is where the metric count is performed.

**Four of the six are assigned; two are not [D].** Gravity determines the shell geometry and Law I locks the fiber length to it — that is the $3+1$. The remaining $2$ are **left open**, deliberately.

> Earlier versions assigned those two to the odd matter sector, $T_-\to2$. **That assignment is retired** (§XI.1) — it was made because there happened to be two of them, which is not a reason.
>
> **A stronger claim would be wrong, and is not made.** A connection may vary freely while its class stays fixed: under $F\mapsto F+d\alpha$ with $\alpha$ a global 1-form, $\int_{S^2}d\alpha = 0$ by Stokes, so $c_1$ is untouched **[T]**. Connections form an affine space over the 1-forms, all sharing one class. **So fixed $c_1$ does not forbid connection dynamics.** What it forbids is specifically the old *integrated flux law* $c_1\propto M_-/m_{\rm unit}$, which made the class itself mass-dependent. The position here is simply that **the connection is unsourced and its deformations unassigned** — no impossibility claim is needed or made.

The two are **local connection cross-components** of an adapted metric, not two physical degrees of freedom: a $U(1)$ connection on an $S^2$ patch has two local components, but $A\mapsto A+d\lambda$ is gauge, and the gauge-invariant content is fixed only after that freedom is quotiented. The same discipline applies here as to the six above. They may remain unassigned until the theory itself says what they do.

## IV.1 The twin theorem, restated on the gravity base [V/T]

The static operator is $\Delta+3$, and Law I poses it on the spatial gravity slice $\Sigma_{\rm grav}\equiv\mathbb{RP}^3(\ell)$ of $B_{\rm grav}^{\rm static}\simeq\mathbb{R}_t\times\Sigma_{\rm grav}$, whose functions are the antipodally *even* functions on the double cover $\Sigma_{\rm rep}\equiv S^3(\ell)$. On $S^3$ the degree-$n$ harmonics have $\Delta = -n(n+2)$ and parity $(-1)^n$, so the kernel of $\Delta+3$ is exactly the degree-one dipoles — and those are **odd**. Hence

$$\ker\big(\Delta_{\Sigma_{\rm grav} }+3\big) = 0$$

and every source on the gravity base has a static solution **[T]**. Under $\pi:\Sigma_{\rm rep}\to\Sigma_{\rm grav}$ a point source $\delta_b$ lifts to $\pi^*\delta_b = \delta_x+\delta_{-x}$, which is automatically orthogonal to every dipole **[V]**.

> **One projective source downstairs is one same-sign antipodal pair on the cover.** The twin is not forced by solvability. It is what a single gravitational source on $\mathbb{RP}^3$ looks like when lifted to the resolved $S^3$ — the seed's sentence, *light resolves a representative; gravity resolves a class*, in its most literal form.

The older argument posed the operator on $S^3$ itself, where a lone pole has a nonzero dipole — a ball at the pole has $0.169$ — and a numerical solve returns the kernel mode as its symptom. That computation survives as a **consistency check**: an unsymmetrized source on the cover does not descend to the gravity base. The Green's functions, the twin's same-sign well and the plateau of §IV.8 are unchanged, since every one of them was computed with the lifted, symmetric source.

## IV.2 Closure and the $G$ identity [D]

The gravity base is the Einstein static universe: $\Lambda = 1/\ell^2$, $\bar\rho = 1/4\pi\ell^2$. The meridian flux through every latitude is $M_c$, and closure fixes it:

$$\frac{GM_c}{c^2\ell} = \frac{\pi}{2}$$

**A constraint, not a derivation.** It also reads as a *closure-radius identity*: $2GM_c/c^2 = \pi\ell$, the antipodal distance on $S^3(\ell)$. That is a Schwarzschild-radius comparison, not a horizon — $r_s = 2GM/c^2$ is defined for a vacuum exterior, while the Einstein-static arena has matter everywhere and a different global causal structure — so nothing here establishes a trapped surface.

> **Lifted and quotient masses — a lemma [T], new in the version-16 draft.** Let $\pi:\Sigma_{\rm rep}\to\Sigma_{\rm grav}$ — that is, $S^3(\ell)\to\mathbb{RP}^3(\ell)$ — be the double cover. A gravity-base source of mass $m$ at $b$ lifts to $\pi^*\delta_b = \delta_x+\delta_{\tau x}$, with coefficient $m$ at each preimage, so
> $$\int_{\Sigma_{\rm rep} }\pi^*\rho_g\;=\;2\int_{\Sigma_{\rm grav} }\rho_g$$
> Three masses must therefore be kept apart, and this document no longer calls all three simply $M$: **$M_{\rm rep}$**, matter resolved on the representational base; **$M_{\rm grav}$**, the gravity-base source; and **$M_{\rm lift} = 2M_{\rm grav}$**, the integral over the covering sphere of the lifted source $\tilde\rho_g := \pi^*\rho_g$. The lifted source is *not* the matter: when the twin is barren, $\rho_{\rm rep}\neq\pi^*\rho_g$ — one is the actual matter distribution, the other the gravitational source seen from the cover. Law I does not count barren matter twice; the lift of the quotient source simply has two images. For point sources under the natural pushforward normalization, $M_{\rm grav} = M_{\rm rep}$; for extended matter, once the optical and matter metrics depart from $r^*g_G$, the equality of the two integrated masses becomes part of the open coupling problem. **The closure mass $M_c$ above is a lifted mass**: $GM_c/c^2\ell = \pi/2$ is the same statement as $GM_{\rm grav}/c^2\ell = \pi/4$, and the closure-radius comparison pairs like with like — $2GM_{\rm lift}/c^2 = \pi\ell$ is the diameter of $S^3(\ell)$, $2GM_{\rm grav}/c^2 = \pi\ell/2$ that of $\mathbb{RP}^3(\ell)$.

## IV.3 Law I as the fiber-length equation [V]

Weak-field statics are conformally round, and the Hamiltonian constraint linearizes to

$$(\Delta_{S^3}+3)\,\delta\phi = -2\pi\,\delta\rho_+$$

the twin-theorem operator acting on the conformal factor. For a ball with its twin, $\phi^2 = 2.017$ at the ball's center and identically at the **empty** twin, agreeing to $10^{-11}$ **[V]**.

## IV.4 Weyl from the fiber length [V/D]

Wherever the stress is isotropic — vacuum, the arena constant, a static fluid — the electric Weyl tensor of a static solution is the trace-free Ricci tensor of its spatial metric, $E_{ij} = [R_{ij}(h)]^{\rm TF} = [N^{-1}D_iD_jN]^{\rm TF}$. For this theory's slices, $h = \phi^4g_{\rm ref}$ with $\phi = \sqrt{L_H/2\pi\ell}$, the reference Ricci tensor is pure trace and drops out, leaving the **exact** relation, in an orthonormal frame,

$$E_{\hat i\hat j} = \phi^{-4}\Big[-2\phi^{-1}\nabla_i\nabla_j\phi+6\phi^{-2}\nabla_i\phi\,\nabla_j\phi\Big]^{\rm TF}$$

with $\nabla$ the reference derivative **[D]**. The gradient term is genuinely nonlinear. In the weak field it drops out, leaving

$$E_{ij}\simeq-2\big[\nabla_i\nabla_j\phi\big]^{\rm TF}$$

whose trace-free Hessian is the tidal field, while the Hessian's trace is the matter. For Schwarzschild, $\phi = 1+m/2r$, the exact eigenvalues are $(m/R^3)(-2,1,1)$ in the areal radius $R = r\phi^2$ — radial stretching, transverse squeezing — and the linear formula gives the same pattern in the isotropic radius **[V]**, checked symbolically together with $E_{ij} = N^{-1}D_iD_jN$. The field is Petrov type D, with repeated principal null directions along $\partial_t\pm\widehat{\nabla L_H}$, the directions of steepest fiber-length change. *An earlier revision quoted $(m/r^3)(1,-\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)$, which are the eigenvalues of $[\nabla\nabla\phi]^{\rm TF}$ itself, not of $E$ — off by a factor of $-2$ — and presented the linear formula as exact* **[R]**.
## IV.5 The metric, written out [V/D]

$$ds^2 = -N^2dt^2 + L_H^2\big(d\theta+\alpha\big)^2 + \kappa^2du^2 + \phi^4g_{S^3}$$

with $N$ and $L_H$ from Law I, $\alpha$ the connection of Structure III, $u$ the meridian and $\kappa$ its scale. For a point mass, $\phi = 1 + GM/2r + O(r^2/\ell^2)$, and near the source the four-dimensional part is **isotropic Schwarzschild, as the $R/\ell\ll1$ local comparison solution** **[C]**:

$$ds^2_4 = -\left(\frac{1-GM/2r}{1+GM/2r}\right)^2dt^2 + \left(1+\frac{GM}{2r}\right)^4\big(dr^2+r^2d\Omega_2^2\big)$$

with closure corrections at $O(r^2/\ell^2)$ — about $6\%$ at 10 kpc in the galactic arena ($\ell = 16$ kpc), negligible on planetary scales; the old $4\times10^{-15}$ belonged to the retired cosmological $\ell = 63$ Gpc. Shells have proper area $4\pi r^2(1+GM/2r)^4$. The meridian scale $\kappa$ remains undetermined **[O]**.

## IV.6 Black holes [T/C]

Nothing in this version adds a charge to a black hole. As **local comparison solutions**, valid for $R/\ell\ll1$, a neutral secondary has the Schwarzschild exterior on the gravity base and a charged one Reissner–Nordström,

$$f(r) = 1 - \frac{2GM}{c^2r} + \frac{Gq^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0c^4r^2}$$

with $q$ the ordinary SI charge and no additional contribution — in the geometrized units of Part X, $G = c = 4\pi\epsilon_0 = 1$, simply $1-2M/r+Q^2/r^2$, with $M^2\geq Q^2$ for a hole. The exact black hole of the closed arena, with its constant $\Lambda = \ell^{-2}$ and its closure source, is open (§X.10).

> Earlier versions carried a second charge $P = g_2M/m_{\rm unit}$, built from the flux sector, which tightened extremality to $M^2\geq Q^2+P^2$ universally and was bounded by Mercury at $g_2/m_{\rm unit}<0.024$. **All of that is retired** (§XI.1): with the fiber's Chern class fixed at $\pm1$ by spin geometry, there is no mass-proportional flux to gravitate and no parameter to bound.

## IV.7 Charge and the twin [V/D]

Gauss on a closed section forces a compensating charge somewhere, and the seed puts nothing at the twin. With the compensator wherever the other charges are, the field of a charge $Q$ is regular at the twin and vanishes there — verified, $|E|\to0$ **linearly**. **The twin carries gravitational shape with no electromagnetic content**; a charged secondary's twin holds the $Q^2/r^2$ term as a *tidal* charge with $\nabla\cdot E = 0$ and $E = 0$. *Within the summed-source architecture this barrenness is strongly constrained: since gravity sums over sheets, field energy duplicated at the twin would add to active but not passive mass, and lunar laser ranging bounds the duplicated fraction at $\eta\lesssim5\times10^{-11}$ (§X.13, under the assumptions stated there).*

## IV.8 Orbits and the $v^2$ family [V]

On the gravity base a secondary is a well in the jellium, and closed-universe orbits are retrograde-precessing rosettes: apsidal advance $1.945\pi$ per radial period at small apocenter, falling to $1.836\pi$ mid-disk. The kinematic pattern speed $\Omega-\kappa/2$ falls by a factor 10 across a disk where Kepler alone gives 20 — **the closure term halves the winding rate of kinematic spirals.**

For circular orbits at colatitude $\chi$, $v^2(\chi) = \Phi'(\chi)\tan\chi$, and at the equator $\Phi'(\pi/2) = 0$ by twin symmetry, so $v^2_{\rm eq} = -\Phi''(\pi/2)$. Solving for a lump holding a fraction $f$ of the closure mass gives, converged as the lump shrinks,

$$v^2_{\rm eq} = 2.352\,f = 1.497\,\frac{GM_{\rm lift} }{\ell} = 2.994\,\frac{GM_{\rm grav} }{\ell}$$

**independent of how the lump is packed** — only the monopole survives at the equator. *(Earlier quotations of this coefficient mixed the two mass conventions; they are recorded in §XI.1.)*

**Extended profiles: the shape family [V].** For an exponential profile $\rho\propto e^{-\chi/h}$ about the pole, with its same-sign twin, the curve keeps its characteristic shape — a peak, a fall, a shallow dip, and a rise to a *nonzero* equatorial plateau — with the family parametrized by $h/\ell$:

| $h/\ell$ | peak $v^2$ (at $\chi$) | dip $v^2$ (at $\chi$) | equatorial $v^2$ |
|--:|:--|:--|--:|
| 0.01 | 9.73 (0.034) | 1.413 (0.785) | 1.498 |
| 0.03 | 3.32 (0.104) | 1.406 (0.785) | 1.492 |
| 0.10 | — rising, then a flat shoulder | — | 1.417 |
| 0.30 | — monotone rise | — | 0.923 |

all in units of $GM/\ell$. For compact profiles the dip sits at $\chi = \pi/4$, about 6% below the plateau, and the equatorial value tends to $1.497$; for broad ones the peak and dip disappear and the equatorial value falls. So "packing-independent" holds in the compact limit $h\ll\ell$, not for profiles comparable to the sphere.

**The intended arena is galactic [V/S/O].** The plateau lives at the scale $\ell$, and the intended reading is a *galactic* three-sphere — not a cosmological one.

> **Which mass.** Below, $M_{\rm gal}\equiv M_{\rm grav}$ is the galaxy's own gravitational mass, so a compact source gives $v^2_{\rm eq} = 2.994\,GM_{\rm gal}/\ell$; the factor of two against the lifted mass is the lemma of §IV.2, confirmed independently by a Kepler test on the near field **[V]**.

> **One arena.** Here $\ell$ is the radius of the galactic slice $\Sigma_{\rm grav} = \mathbb{RP}^3(\ell)$, and every result was computed on its double cover $\Sigma_{\rm rep} = S^3(\ell)$ with the lifted, symmetric source — which, by §IV.1, is exactly the gravity-base problem; there is no second, cosmological scale to confuse it with. What remains open is the arena's background: at galactic $\ell$ the Einstein-static relations demand a uniform closure source that Law I's source map does not yet generate (the closure row below; §XI.2, item 0′). If that background is ever replaced, the linear operator — and with it the coefficient $2.994$ — must be re-derived **[O]**.

At galactic scale the plateau is then of the right *order*. A compact $6\times10^{10}M_\odot$ gives $v\approx311$ km/s at $\ell = 8$ kpc and 220 km/s at 16 kpc. The Milky Way's own profile, taken **isotropically** on the sphere with its exponential scale $h = 2.6$ kpc, gives $235$ km/s at 8 kpc and $205$ km/s at 16 kpc, and **reaches 220 km/s at $\ell\approx13$ kpc** **[V]**. The model keeps this isotropic treatment deliberately, as the conservative choice; a thin-disk geometry enters only the detached wing (Part XII). ## IV.10 The Keplerian limit, quantified [V]

In the galactic arena the closure correction is **about $6.5\%$ at 10 kpc** for $\ell = 16$ kpc — not a nuisance but the onset of the plateau physics of §IV.8 — and $\sim10^{-20}$ at 1 AU, so planetary dynamics are untouched. *(An earlier cosmological reading, with $\ell = 63$ Gpc, gave $4\times10^{-15}$ at 10 kpc and a "jellium crossover" at 1.2 Mpc; both figures are retired.)*

## IV.11 The static wall [T/V]

> **Identical twin wells cannot generate a relative gravitational redshift between themselves.**

A static geometry has a timelike Killing vector, so Killing energy is conserved along null geodesics and $1+z = \sqrt{g_{tt}(\text{receiver})/g_{tt}(\text{emitter})}$. When emitter and receiver sit in **identical** wells the lapses are equal and $z = 0$ identically **[V]**. Since the seed makes the twin's well identical to the source's, no shift can arise between them, and every route to Hubble's law within the static theory ends there.

*A stronger sentence carried by earlier drafts — "on a static geometry there is no redshift" — is **false** and is retired (§XI.1): Schwarzschild is static and redshifts ($z = 0.21$ from $6M$ to $100M$, $0.73$ from $3M$ to infinity)* **[V]**. *What staticity forbids is cosmological redshift from expansion, not gravitational redshift.*

# PART V — THE SEED

## V.1 Light resolves; gravity quotients [S/T]

Matter and Maxwell fields live on $B_{\rm rep}$. An optical event is a point $x\in B_{\rm rep}$, so **light distinguishes $x$ from $\tau x$**: the electromagnetic field is not meridian-averaged.

Gravity first applies $q:\mathcal{M}\to B_{\rm grav}$, and $q(x) = q(\tau x)$. So its operation is

$$\text{gravity} = r^*\circ\,\text{Einstein}\,\circ r_*$$

push the resolved source down to the class, solve, pull the solution back up — with **$r = q\circ\iota$ throughout, not $q$** **[T]**. The distinction matters: $q:\mathcal{M}^5\to B_{\rm grav}^4$ is a *submersion*, so $dq$ annihilates the meridian direction and $q^*g_G$ is **degenerate** on $\mathcal{M}$ — a rank-4 form, not a metric. Whereas $r$ is a local diffeomorphism on each branch, so

$$g^{(G)}_{\rm rep} = r^*g_G$$

is a genuine Lorentzian metric on the representational base. **This is the object that determines what light does**, and its relation to general relativity's optical metric is the open PPN question of Law I.

## V.2 The barren twin [D/S]

Take $T(x)\neq0$ and $T(\tau x) = 0$. The quotiented source is $T^{(g)}([x]) = T(x)$, and the metric solved at $[x]$ lifts to **both** representatives. Therefore

$$T(\tau x) = 0\qquad\text{while}\qquad \text{curvature at }\tau x\neq0$$

> **This is the seed.** No dark particle has been placed at the twin. The discrepancy is a difference in **resolution** — light resolves a representative, gravity resolves a class — and the two bases of Part 0 are what make that sentence sayable.

## V.3 Why the twin is unobserved [V/D]

It is **empty**: no matter to emit, absorb or scatter, and electromagnetically barren by §IV.7. That is now the *only* reason. An earlier version added a second, causal one — the twin's light could not yet have arrived — but that belonged to the retired cosmological reading, where $\pi\ell/c = 646$ Gyr at $\ell = 63$ Gpc. In the galactic arena the crossing time is $\pi\ell/c\approx1.6\times10^5$ yr at $\ell = 16$ kpc, and light could have crossed the arena tens of thousands of times over the Galaxy's age. **The twin must be dark because it is barren, not because it is remote** — a sharper statement, with the auxiliary excuse removed.

## V.4 Kirchhoff at the poles: topological twin-sign pairing [T/V]

*The Hopf restrictions at the resolved crossings are globally constrained: their outward classes sum to zero. For a single meridian that forces the two crossings to carry opposite signs; for several meridians it does not pair them crossing by crossing. This section establishes exactly that, and states what it does **not** establish about where matter may sit.*

**The conservation law [T].** Alexander duality identifies a circle bundle over $S^4\setminus\Gamma$ with a 1-cycle $z = \sum_e a_e\,e$ on the graph $\Gamma$ of §II.1, where $a_e$ is the bundle's class on the small linking sphere of arc $e$. Every arc runs from pole to pole, so

$$\partial z = \Big(\sum_e a_e\Big)\big((-p)-p\big) = 0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\sum_e a_e = 0$$

The Hopf classes on the $2N$ arcs, oriented pole-outward, sum to zero: **Hopf class is conserved through the poles** — Kirchhoff's law at a vertex.

**The sign dictionary [V].** Slide each arc's linking sphere along the arc to the equator. It becomes the shell about that crossing point in $B_{\rm rep}$ with its **outward** orientation — and with the *same* sign at every crossing, since every pole-outward arc crosses the equator heading south (verified by direct orientation computation: sign $-1$ at both $\hat n$ and $-\hat n$ for pole-outward arcs, but $-1$ and $+1$ for the loop-oriented meridian). So in $B_{\rm rep}$'s own terms:

$$\sum_{\text{all }2N\text{ crossing points }x} c_{\rm out}(x) = 0$$

**The one-meridian case, checked independently [V].** Pull the Hopf bundle back along the retraction $S^4\setminus C\to S^2$, $x\mapsto(x_2,x_3,x_4)/|\cdot|$, with $C$ the meridian. On the outward shell about the source its degree is $+1$; about the twin, **$-1$**. Two independent computations, one answer: $c_{\rm out}(\hat n)+c_{\rm out}(-\hat n) = 0$.

**What is established: a sign pairing, independent of occupancy [V].**

> **The outward Hopf classes at all $2N$ crossings sum to zero.** For one meridian that is a pairing, $c_{\rm out}(\hat n) = -c_{\rm out}(-\hat n)$. For $N\geq2$ it is a single global relation, $\sum_i\big(a_{i,+}+a_{i,-}\big) = 0$, on a graph with $H_1(\Gamma)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2N-1}$, and it does **not** imply $a_{i,+} = -a_{i,-}$ for each $i$. With unit classes everywhere, Kirchhoff allows 6 sign patterns for two meridians, only 4 of them paired, and 70 for four meridians, only 16 paired **[V]**. Pairing meridian by meridian would need an additional restriction on the global bundle class, or extra geometry.

This is a property of the **global bundle, the orientation, and the restriction map** — it holds whether or not matter sits at either crossing. Accordingly Structure III is stated with $|c_1| = 1$ on every relevant linking shell and the sign *induced*, not assigned by occupancy.

**What is not established: that matter must avoid a sign [O].** An earlier draft of this section concluded that the barren twin is topologically forced. **That inference is withdrawn** (§XI.1). It ran: Part I's construction is identical at every source, so every *source* shell carries $+1$; the twin carries $-1$; so the twin cannot host a source. The middle step silently read Law 0 as fixing the **sign** at source shells relative to their outward orientation. Law 0 fixes the bundle *type* — $|c_1| = 1$, total space $S^3$ — and nothing more. Demanding the sign at source shells specifically is an **extra axiom**, and a telling one: it would make the sign at a crossing depend on whether matter sits there, so that topology would depend on matter content — precisely what the centrepiece disavows.

**An explicit counterexample [V].** Take the retraction bundle above with sources at *both* crossings of one meridian. Its outward degrees are $+1$ and $-1$; each source shell has $|c_1| = 1$, so Law 0 is satisfied at both. **A doubly occupied meridian is allowed.** What topology fixes is the pairing of signs, not the occupancy.

**What would turn pairing into barrenness.** An additional theorem or axiom of the form

$$\text{ordinary matter worldlines are compatible with only one induced Hopf sign}$$

— a **matter-selection rule**. It is not in the theory: Structure III is explicitly unsourced, with no matter coupling, so nothing currently ties matter to the sign. *Were* such a rule derived, the conclusion would follow, and it would be strong: with unit classes on every shell of the pencil, the allowed configurations would then be exactly one source and one barren twin per meridian — for **every** $N$ **[D, conditional]**. The proof is a count. Unit classes summing to zero means exactly $N$ plus signs and $N$ minus signs. Every meridian carries at least one source, and the rule puts sources on one sign only, so each of the $N$ meridians needs at least one plus; with exactly $N$ pluses, each has exactly one. Enumeration for $N\leq4$ agrees, with $2^N$ survivors — the choices of which crossing holds the source **[V]**. The pairing is *derived* here, although the global sum alone does not imply it.

**Nor is $-1$ a matter class [O].** It would be premature to say a twin could host "matter of the opposite Hopf class": nothing yet establishes that matter fields carry a Hopf-sign label at all. The prior question is whether the sign is **a property of the bundle's restriction and orientation alone, or a representation carried by a matter field**. Only the second could support a selection rule — and deriving one, rather than declaring it, would make barrenness genuinely forced, a considerably stronger result than asserting it.

**Scope.** Everything here assumes the static great-circle pencil, itself an assumption (§VIII.1), and point-like sources.

# PART VI — NEWMAN–JANIS AND KERR

## VI.1 The construction, in six steps [T/V]

**1.** Put Schwarzschild in Kerr–Schild form, $g_{ab} = \eta_{ab}+2Hk_ak_b$ with $H = M/r$ and $k$ null and geodesic for both metrics. Exact, not linearized.

**2.** Note the whole solution rests on one harmonic function: $\nabla^2(1/r) = 0$ away from the origin **[V]**.

**3.** Complexify and displace the source along the imaginary axis, $z\to z-ia$. **This step supplies the characteristic Kerr complex structure; it is not by itself the whole derivation** — see the caveat below.

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2} }\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+(z-ia)^2} }$$

still harmonic **[V]**.

**4.** The complex distance vanishes where $-2az = 0$ and $x^2+y^2+z^2 = a^2$, i.e. $z = 0$ and $x^2+y^2 = a^2$: **a ring of radius $a$** **[V]**. Schwarzschild's point singularity has become Kerr's ring.

**5.** With the oblate radius $R$ defined by $\frac{x^2+y^2}{R^2+a^2}+\frac{z^2}{R^2} = 1$, the complex distance is $R+ia\cos\theta$ and $H = MR^3/(R^4+a^2z^2)$ — **Kerr in Kerr–Schild form**.

**6.** The angular momentum is $J = Ma$, so $a = J/M$: **the imaginary displacement is the spin per unit mass.**

> ### ◆ WHAT THIS SEQUENCE DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH [T/O]
> **The complex displacement supplies the characteristic Kerr complex structure; recovery of the full metric additionally uses the Kerr–Schild null-congruence construction.** The original Newman–Janis prescription also manipulates a null tetrad and applies a *reality prescription*, and generalized Newman–Janis algorithms are known to be ambiguous away from special cases — different reality prescriptions give different metrics. The six steps above are a faithful account of the *complex-structure* content, not a self-contained derivation, and the earlier phrase "nothing is added; a source is moved" overstated that **[R]**.

## VI.2 Where this happens, and where it does not [S/D]

Newman–Janis acts on the **complexification of the representational base**:

$$B_{\rm rep}\hookrightarrow B_{ {\rm rep},\mathbb{C} }\xrightarrow{\text{shift} }B_{ {\rm rep},\mathbb{C} }\xrightarrow{\text{real slice} }B_{\rm rep}^{\rm Kerr}$$

And the canonical shells **follow the complex worldline**: at every point and radius the shell is $Q^2_{\rm aff}$ with lift $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, so the displacement moves the center of the entire shell system. Two roles stay separate:

$$\text{Kerr rotation} = \text{complex worldline displacement},\qquad \text{the fiber} = \text{the shell's spin lift}$$

> **Rotation is not winding around the fiber.** Earlier versions tried to make one construction carry rotation, the seed, the fiber and the dynamics at once. Here complexified spacetime handles Newman–Janis, spin geometry handles the shell lift, the meridians handle the seed, and Einstein's equations handle the fiber length. **No single construction is asked to do all four jobs.**

## VI.3 The $g = 2$ signpost [T/O]

Kerr–Newman satisfies $J = Ma$ and $\mu = Qa$, hence $\mu = (Q/M)J$ — the conventional $g = 2$ relation. Complex-worldline approaches to Einstein–Maxwell give a further reason to keep the complex center visible. **A future bridge target, not a derivation of the electron.**

# PART VII — SPIN, AND THE CATEGORY FIREWALL

## VII.1 What the spin geometry does supply [T]

Newman–Penrose two-spinors transform under $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ — the same group that lifts the complex shell (§I.6). The canonical fiber's compact part is therefore the spin phase, and the theory may legitimately constrain: spin structures, phase holonomy, chirality, principal spinors, allowed connections, topological charge, geometric relations among mass, charge and rotation, and the availability of zero modes.

## VII.2 What it does not [T]

$$\text{spinorial shell geometry}\ \not\Rightarrow\ \text{fermionic anticommutation}$$

Newman–Penrose spinors are **classical, commuting** geometric spinors. Grassmann-odd quantum fields are a further structure, and the implication is blocked by type, not by difficulty: a Grassmann number satisfies $\theta^2 = 0$, which is what makes $\psi^\dagger(x)^2 = 0$ and hence the exclusion principle, and nothing in a spin bundle produces it. **Any such derivation remains open.**

## VII.3 Statistics and exclusion are inputs, unchanged [T]

Spin–statistics, anticommutation and the Pauli exclusion principle enter this theory **exactly as they enter ordinary quantum field theory**, and nothing in v15 modifies, derives or threatens them.

*A note on what is not claimed.* Earlier drafts invoked the Finkelstein–Rubinstein argument — that exchanging two identical particles is locally homotopic to rotating one by $2\pi$ — to say statistics proceed as usual. **That invocation is withdrawn** (§XI.1). FR is a statement about loops in the configuration space of extended topological solitons, and the exchange/rotation identification depends on that configuration space's topology; it is not a theorem that a spacetime spin bundle by itself delivers fermionic statistics. Unless and until actual soliton configuration spaces are constructed here, the safe and sufficient statement is the one above, resting on §VII.2.

## VII.4 Twin parity [O]

The frame bundle of $\mathbb{RP}^3 = SO(3)$ is $SO(3)\times SO(3)$, with $\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$: **two independent** generators, a frame rotation (giving spin) and a trip around the twin loop (giving a parity $\tau = \pm1$). It is tempting to read the two $\mathbb{Z}_2$ factors as two independent particle quantum numbers. **That reading is not established, and the section is tagged open for that reason.** $\mathbb{RP}^3$ does have two spin structures, since $H^1(\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb{Z}_2) = \mathbb{Z}_2$; but the relation between the base loop, the frame rotation, the *chosen* spin structure, and a genuinely independent label on a field is subtler than reading off two factors of $\pi_1$. Settling it is an associated-bundle representation problem, not a homotopy count, and it has not been worked through here **[O]**.

# PART VIII — STATIC REGRESSION AND TESTS

## VIII.1 The static great-circle realization [S/D]

*An earlier draft claimed to **derive** this from topology. That claim is withdrawn — three steps of it were wrong (§XI.1). What follows is an exact construction of the realization, with its one assumption marked as an assumption.*

The intended specialization is

$$\mathcal{M}_{\rm static}\sim\mathbb{R}_t\times S^4,\qquad B_{\rm rep}^{\rm static}\sim\mathbb{R}_t\times S^3,\qquad B_{\rm grav}^{\rm static}\sim\mathbb{R}_t\times\mathbb{RP}^3$$

with dimensions $1+4 = 5$, $1+3 = 4$, $1+3 = 4$ **[V]**.

### The assumption [S]

> **In the static limit, the meridian structure is the antipodal great-circle pencil.** It is a *pencil*, not a foliation: distinct meridians meet at the two poles, which form a common singular set. Nothing in Law 0 or in the topology of $S^4$ forces this, and it is adopted as a structural choice.

### The construction, and the quotient [D/V]

Embed $S^4\subset\mathbb{R}^5$ and fix an axis $p\in S^4$, with poles $\pm p$. For each unit vector $n$ in the 3-sphere of $p^\perp$, form the **2-plane**

$$P_n = \operatorname{span}(p,n),\qquad C_n = P_n\cap S^4$$

Then $C_n$ is a great circle through both poles — verified: every point $\cos t\,p+\sin t\,n$ has unit norm, and $t = 0,\pi$ give $\pm p$ **[V]**. And since a plane is unchanged by negating a spanning vector,

$$P_n = P_{-n}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C_n = C_{-n}$$

verified pointwise **[V]**. So the meridians are in bijection with $S^3$ modulo the antipodal map:

$$\boxed{\ \text{space of meridians} = S^3/\{\pm1\} = \mathbb{RP}^3\ }$$

**exactly**, with no index theory, no foliation language, and no claim about closed orbits. And the bisection follows immediately: $C_n$ meets the equatorial $S^3$ at $n$ and at $-n$, the two points of §II.3.

### What is derived, and what is not

$$\text{antipodal great-circle pencil}\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathbb{RP}^3\qquad\textbf{[D]}$$
$$\text{Law 0} + \text{topology}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{antipodal great-circle pencil}\qquad\textbf{[O]}$$

The first is exact. The second is **not available**, and the attempt to supply it is retired (§XI.1).

**The remaining regression, in order [O]:** derive the quotient metric and its Green's function on $\mathbb{RP}^3$; recover the geodesic and orbit equations; **then** reassert the numbers of §IV.8.

## VIII.2 The golf ball [D]

*The question was: does a small body get a Hopf structure of its own, or does a large nearby body dominate?*

**In this version the question has a clean answer, and a different one from before.** The fiber is supplied by spin geometry, and every shell about every worldline carries a unit class:

$$|c_1| = 1$$

independent of the body's mass, its neighbours, or any parameter, with the sign induced globally by orientation and restriction rather than chosen per shell (Structure III). **A golf ball's shells carry the same unit class as Earth's.** There is no competition, no crossover radius, no dependence on what else is nearby — because the structure is a fact about the rotation group acting on a shell, not a tally of matter.

> Earlier versions answered this with a mass-proportional winding number, $M/m_{\rm unit}$ — $2.7\times10^{25}$ for the ball and $3.6\times10^{51}$ for Earth — together with a "quantization fork" over the value of $m_{\rm unit}$ and a crossover at $0.56\,\mu$m where Earth's flux overtook the ball's. **All of that is retired** (§XI.1). ## VIII.3 The Machian direction [T/D/V]

Vacuum general relativity already has the symmetry $(g,M)\to(\lambda^2g,\lambda M)$: every Schwarzschild exterior *is* a rescaled copy of every other, with mass the scale. So "isomorphic shells up to scale" is true and yields nothing new — the scale it fixes is mass, not size, and compactness $r_s/R$ ($1.4\times10^{-9}$ for Earth, $3.2\times10^{-27}$ for a golf ball) is untouched.

Rescaling $r_s$ with $GM$ fixed is **inconsistent**, since $r_s = 2GM/c^2$ is one number whenever the exterior has a single source. Separating the horizon from the far-field coupling would require the near field to be sourced by something the far field does not see.

> Earlier versions offered one: the flux energy $|F_\oplus+F_g|^2$ has a cross term present only when Earth is, giving the ball a near field that differs alone and in company, bounded by Mercury at $3\times10^{-4}$. **That is retired with the flux sector** (§XI.1). **So the Machian proposal now has no realizable form in the theory at all** — which is the honest position, and worth stating rather than leaving a retired mechanism standing in for one.

**A local Mach relation fails [V]:** reading $GM/c^2\ell = \pi/2$ with $\ell$ a sphere of influence gives $5\times10^{11}G$ for Earth and $3\times10^{22}G$ for the golf ball. The Mach relation closes a *universe*.

**Emergent $G$ [O]:** an open coupling problem, not a theorem. ADM and Komar masses carry $G$ in the conventional normalization, but matter Lagrangians contain inertial and rest-mass parameters that require no solution of Einstein's equations. The real difficulty is to define an operationally appropriate mass variable and show that a candidate emergent coupling reproduces **both** the inertial and the gravitational roles. *An earlier categorical claim — that $G$ is emergent iff mass has a $G$-free definition, and general relativity has none — is too strong and is retired* (§XI.1).

## X.0 The intuition, reconstructed [T/V/S/O]

*Part X became hard to follow because it split one intuition into several mathematically distinct pieces. This section puts them back in order, starting from the intuition itself.*

**The proposal, in the author's terms.** Reissner–Nordström might be "a small-charge limiting case of some projective gravity effect", unseen so far because extremal holes are rare. Start from Schwarzschild's two chiral pieces and move one of them along the meridian toward the twin: none moved is Schwarzschild, part-way is a charged hole, and "exactly one at each twin position IS an extremal black hole". Electric charge would be flux along the meridian, so that no charged source need exist upstairs. And an angle $m$ — "maybe literally just the imaginary value of the complexified meridian" — would carry the family from one end to the other.

**What Reissner–Nordström itself says [T].** In $f = 1-2M/R+Q^2/R^2$, gravity sees $Q^2$, never the sign of $Q$; only the electromagnetic field tells $+Q$ from $-Q$. That is the seed in miniature: the resolved sector knows something the gravitational side forgets. So the real question is whether $Q$ could be the *resolved, oriented* face of one geometric datum whose gravitational face is sign-blind.

**Separation fails; imbalance does not [D/V].** Moving a piece *along* the meridian cannot change gravity, because the quotient forgets meridian position — which is why the literal mechanism fails (§X.4). But Reissner–Nordström's spatial factor splits exactly into two factors (§X.7),

$$\Omega = \Big(1+\frac{m_+}{r}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{m_-}{r}\Big),\qquad m_\pm = \frac{M\pm Q}{2}$$

so that

$$M = m_++m_-,\qquad Q = m_+-m_-,\qquad \delta^2\equiv M^2-Q^2 = 4m_+m_-$$

Mass is the sum of two weights, charge their signed difference, non-extremality their product **[V]**. Schwarzschild is the balanced point, $m_+ = m_- = M/2$; an extremal hole has one weight driven to zero; a naked singularity has one weight negative. Swapping the weights leaves $M$ and $m_+m_-$ alone and sends $Q\to-Q$. So the intuition survives in a different form: **not two pieces moving apart in space, but their relative weights moving apart.**

**The angle, and where the complexified meridian enters [V/S].** Writing $(Q,\delta) = M(\cos m,\sin m)$ puts the whole subextremal family on a unit semicircle, with $m_+ = M\cos^2\tfrac m2$ and $m_- = M\sin^2\tfrac m2$ (§X.9): Schwarzschild at $m = \pi/2$, the two extremal holes at $m = 0$ and $m = \pi$. Now give the meridian a complex coordinate in multiplicative form, $w = e^{i(\chi+i\mu)} = e^{-\mu}e^{i\chi}$. Its angle $\chi$ is the position the quotient forgets; its modulus $|w| = e^{-\mu}$ is not a position at all. Setting $\tan\tfrac m2 = e^{-\mu}$ gives $\cos m = \tanh\mu$, $(M,Q) = \delta(\cosh\mu,\sinh\mu)$, and

$$m_\pm = \frac{\delta}{2}e^{\pm\mu},\qquad \frac{m_-}{m_+} = e^{-2\mu} = |w|^2$$

**[V]**. The modulus of the complexified meridian parameterizes exactly the *ratio* of the two weights — the chiral imbalance. The "rapidity" of §X.11 is the same family in hyperbolic rather than circular coordinates, with $m_\pm$ as its light-cone components; the angle and the rapidity are two coordinates on one family, joined by the half-angle map. One caution: the modulus that can carry this must be a *relative* modulus between the two chiral sectors, surviving after their common rescaling is quotiented — not the modulus of the shell's own $\mathbb{C}^\times$, which may be pure redundancy and cannot become physics merely because its algebra fits (§XIII.5). That the canonical space supplies such a relative $\mathbb{C}^\times$ is not established **[O]**. One part of the hope did not survive: that a galaxy-sized $\ell$ would amplify a small charge into a large mass. The three-sphere on which the angle lives has radius $\delta/4$, fixed by the hole, not by the arena (§X.9).

**Two readings of "no charge upstairs" [T/S].** *(A) The conservative one* is Wheeler's charge without charge: the flux obeys $d{\star}F = 0$ everywhere, yet a sphere linking a topological tunnel carries $\int{\star}F\neq0$ — as across a Reissner–Nordström bridge, whose two ends see opposite charges (§X.3). It needs no new physics, but the field and its stress-energy are real. *(B) The radical one:* the geometry comes first — the $Q^2/R^2$ part of the metric is canonical, and the electromagnetic field is whatever fits that mold. Then one structure must not be counted twice. Either the metric and the Maxwell field are two readouts of one underlying datum, so that Maxwell stress is not an additional source; or the canonical geometry supplies a stress of Maxwell form, with the actual field a separate and observational one — which the barren charged twin of §X.6 already hints at, carrying curvature of Reissner–Nordström form with no Maxwell field present. Neither mechanism exists in the action yet; here the intuition is ahead of the theory **[O]**.

**The sharpened hypothesis [S/O].** Suppose the canonical chiral data form an ordered pair $(D_+,D_-)$, read in two ways — gravitationally, symmetric under exchange, and electromagnetically, antisymmetric:

$$\mathcal G(D_+,D_-) = \mathcal G(D_-,D_+),\qquad \mathcal E(D_+,D_-) = -\mathcal E(D_-,D_+)$$

At the scalar level the gravitational readout is $M = m_++m_-$ and $\delta^2 = 4m_+m_-$, and the electromagnetic one is $Q = m_+-m_-$. Gravity would then see $Q^2$ and Maxwell $Q$ *because of the readout architecture*, not merely because Einstein–Maxwell happens to be quadratic in the field — a projective derivation of Reissner–Nordström's charge structure, with no monopole anywhere in $E$. That is the mathematically sharp descendant of the original idea: **charge as the antisymmetric readout of a chiral pair whose symmetric invariants determine gravity.**

**Where to look [O].** Kim's construction of Kerr as a nonlinear superposition of self-dual and anti-self-dual Taub–NUT constituents, extended to Kerr–Newman through chiral dyons, is the external reason to study chiral composition (§XI.3) — though it does not itself establish the identification. Two questions would decide the branch: *can chiral composition produce $M = m_++m_-$, $Q = m_+-m_-$ and $\delta^2 = 4m_+m_-$?* and *does the canonical $\mathbb{C}^\times$ act on the weights as $(m_+,m_-)\mapsto(e^\beta m_+,e^{-\beta}m_-)$?* If it does, the rapidity stops being a reparameterization of known physics and becomes a transformation of the canonical geometry (§XI.2, items e and f).
## X.1 The proposal [S]

Stated as fairly as possible:

- Take the chiral picture of §XI.3 — Schwarzschild as coincident self-dual and anti-self-dual constituents, Kerr as the same pair displaced in opposite imaginary directions.
- Instead of an imaginary displacement, move one constituent **along the meridian** to its twin position. One constituent at each crossing is to be the extremal hole; partial displacement a general Reissner–Nordström hole; no displacement, Schwarzschild.
- Motivation: Maxwell's field in the canonical space is divergence-free, so an electric monopole would really be flux threading the removed meridian.
- Constraint carried over: the **frozen matter** of Majumdar–Papapetrou — extremal holes in static equilibrium, gravity exactly balancing like-charge repulsion.
- Empirical latitude: no astrophysical black hole is known to be an extremal electrically charged Reissner–Nordström hole, and the actual electric charges of astrophysical holes are expected to be very small, so the exact extremal electric sector is **observationally poorly sampled** rather than wholly unconstrained.

## X.2 What lands exactly: extremality is lapse–fiber balance [V/S]

Reissner–Nordström in isotropic coordinates, written in this theory's variables, with $\delta^2\equiv M^2-Q^2$:

$$\Omega = \phi^2 = 1+\frac Mr+\frac{\delta^2}{4r^2},\qquad N = \frac{1-\delta^2/4r^2}{\Omega}$$

with areal radius $r\Omega$. Substituting reproduces $N^2 = 1-2M/R+Q^2/R^2$ identically **[V]**. Then, exactly,

$$\boxed{\ N\,\Omega = 1-\frac{M^2-Q^2}{4r^2}\ }$$

| hole | $N\Omega$ |
|:--|:--|
| Schwarzschild, $Q = 0$ | $1-M^2/4r^2$ — the defect of §III.2, recovered |
| general Reissner–Nordström | $1-(M^2-Q^2)/4r^2$ |
| extremal, $Q = M$ | $1$ exactly — Majumdar–Papapetrou |

> **The product of lapse and fiber factor is spatially constant if and only if the hole is extremal**, and the defect from balance is exactly the non-extremality $(M^2-Q^2)/4r^2$. The relation §III.2 retired as "exact $N\Omega = 1$" — retired because imposing it *universally* would make every body extremal — is not wrong. **It is precisely the extremal sector.**

**Frozen matter, in these variables [V].** In Majumdar–Papapetrou, $\Omega = U$ and $N = 1/U$ with $U = 1+\sum_iM_i/|x-x_i|$ harmonic — verified for two centres. So the frozen sector is the one in which **the fiber factor is harmonic and superposes linearly**; force balance is what permits the superposition. It is frozen thermally too: the same $\delta$ sets the surface gravity, $\kappa = \delta/(M+\delta)^2$, which vanishes exactly at extremality **[V]**.

**The one-parameter family the proposal wanted exists [D].** $\delta\in[0,M]$ runs from extremal ($\delta = 0$) to Schwarzschild ($\delta = M$). Its parameter, however, is the non-extremality — not a displacement along the meridian. And the family has an internal structure that realizes the proposal's "two halves" exactly (§X.7).

**Scope [S].** The identity is exact for Reissner–Nordström. Reading it as a statement about fiber *length* uses the locking $L_H = \Omega L_H^{\rm ref}$, posited in §III.2 only to first order. With the locking exact, lapse times fiber length records the non-extremality exactly; without it, the identity stands as a statement about $\Omega = \phi^2$.

## X.3 What lands in another form: a twin that recedes with charge [V/T/S]

The proposal pictured charge as the separation between a hole and its twin. Reissner–Nordström already contains such a twin — not the cosmological one, but the other side of its own Einstein–Rosen bridge.

**The bridge involution [V].** The map $(r,\hat n)\mapsto(\delta^2/4r,\,-\hat n)$ is an isometry of the isotropic spatial metric and reverses the lapse, $N\to-N$. It maps the throat sphere $r = \delta/2$ to itself — areal radius $M+\delta = r_+$, where $N = 0$ — and, because it acts antipodally on the angular sphere, has no fixed points at all.

**Its quotient is an $\mathbb{RP}^3$ geon [V/T].** Under $(X,\hat n)\mapsto(\tanh X,\ \operatorname{sech}X\,\hat n)$ the bridge's spatial slice $\mathbb{R}\times S^2$ becomes $S^3$ minus its two infinities, and the involution becomes the **antipodal map of $S^3$** **[V]**. The quotient is $\mathbb{RP}^3$ minus a point — the $\mathbb{RP}^3$ geon of the black-hole literature (Friedman–Schleich–Witt; Louko–Marolf) **[T]**, and the topology of this theory's own gravity base.

**But the charged geon does not carry ordinary electromagnetism [T].** The geometry quotients cleanly; the Maxwell field does not. For electric Reissner–Nordström the two ends carry $+Q$ and $-Q$, and the geon involution reverses the field, $F\mapsto-F$ — so $F$ does not descend as an ordinary $U(1)$ field on the quotient. The known resolution enlarges the gauge group to $O(2)\simeq U(1)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_2$, whose disconnected component implements charge conjugation as a gauge transformation; the quotient then exists, and charge is defined only up to overall sign. *(Supplied by a referee and recorded on that authority.)* This bears directly on §X.12.

**The twin recedes as charge grows [V].** The proper radial distance from isotropic radius $r_0$ through the throat to its twin is

$$D = 2r_0+2M\ln\frac{2r_0}{\delta}-\frac{\delta^2}{2r_0}$$

| $Q/M$ | $D/M$ at $r_0 = 5M$ |
|--:|--:|
| 0 | 14.51 |
| 0.866 | 15.97 |
| 0.995 | 19.21 |
| 0.99995 | 23.82 |
| $\to1$ | $\to\infty$ |

— finite for Schwarzschild, and diverging logarithmically at extremality, where the throat becomes infinitely long.

> **The proposal's picture is realized, with the right twin.** Twin separation grows with charge, the extremal hole is where the twin has receded to infinity, and Schwarzschild has the shortest bridge. The one correction to the proposal's assignment: Schwarzschild's separation is the *minimum*, not zero.

**Whether the bridge-twin is the arena's twin [S/O].** It is tempting to identify them — to let a hole's bridge run from $\hat n$ to $-\hat n$, so that the gravity base's identification of source and twin *is* the geon construction. The topology invites it: both involutions are antipodal maps of an $S^3$ with quotient $\mathbb{RP}^3$. The geometry does not yet support it: the bridge's throat has size $\sim M$, while the meridian from $\hat n$ to $-\hat n$ has length $\pi\ell$. **A structural parallel, not an identification.**

## X.4 What does not land: the meridian mechanism [D]

Three obstructions, each sufficient on its own.

**(1) Gravity cannot see the meridian — that is the seed.** The source reduction is $T^{(g)}_b = \sum_{x\in r^{-1}(b)}\big((dr_x)^{-1}\big)^*T_x$, and both crossings of a meridian lie over the same $b$. Moving a constituent from $\hat n$ to $-\hat n$ leaves $T^{(g)}$ unchanged, hence $g_G$ unchanged, hence $M^2-Q^2$ unchanged. The mechanism asks the one direction gravity is blind to to carry a gravitational parameter.

**(2) The constituents carry the wrong charge.** In the chiral picture of §XI.3 the self-dual and anti-self-dual Taub–NUT constituents carry opposite **NUT** charges — gravitomagnetic, not electric. Displaced to the two crossings they sit over one point of the gravity base, where the NUT charges cancel: the result on $B_{\rm grav}$ is Schwarzschild, not Reissner–Nordström.

**(3) The interpolation leaves the representational base.** A constituent partway along the meridian is off $B_{\rm rep}$ and outside the domain of $r = q\circ\iota$. The theory has no matter there, so the interpolating family is not even representable.

> **The mechanism fails on the architecture's own terms, and the failure is informative:** it confirms that meridian position is invisible to Law I — the seed doing its job.

## X.5 Charge as meridian flux: the carrier exists, but cannot be electric [D/T/O]

**A meridian flux already exists [D].** The Hopf class of Structure III is exactly a flux through the linking spheres of the removed meridians, conserved through the poles with the Kirchhoff pairing of §V.4. Structurally it is what "an electric monopole is meridian flux" asks for.

**But it cannot be electric charge [D].** Every source carries $|c_1| = 1$ whatever its electric charge, and neutral bodies exist. A quantity that is the same for a neutron star and a charged pion is not charge.

**And its native type is magnetic [T].** In the five-dimensional middle space, a removed meridian swept through time is a two-dimensional worldsheet, and $S^2$ links a codimension-3 worldsheet. Flux of a Maxwell 2-form through that $S^2$ is *magnetic*; the electric charge of a point particle in five dimensions is measured on $S^3$. On the four-dimensional base, electric–magnetic duality means the Reissner–Nordström metric sees only $Q^2+P^2$, so the distinction would not change the geometry — but "electric monopoles are meridian flux" names the wrong kind of flux.

**What would be entertainable [O].** A Wheeler-type *charge without charge*: Maxwell extended source-free to $S^4\setminus\Gamma$, with charges as periods on linking spheres. Kirchhoff at the poles would then enforce Gauss's law on the closed base automatically — and for a single meridian would place $-Q$ at the twin. **That reopens an identity retracted in version 6**, "the twin carries opposite electric charge," dropped because it puts charge exactly where the seed puts nothing. With many meridians Kirchhoff constrains only the total, so twins could remain empty; the single-charge case would still conflict with §IV.7. Open, and to be approached knowing that history.

## X.6 How weird extremal holes can be, inside the frozen architecture [D]

The proposal asked how far extremal holes may depart from Reissner–Nordström, given that none is observed. Three answers follow from Parts 0–IX without adding anything.

**(a) The barren twin of an extremal hole is geometrically extremal and electromagnetically empty.** Law I reads $T^{(g)}$ on the class, which contains the source's electromagnetic stress-energy; the solution lifts to both crossings; and by §IV.7 the field vanishes at the twin. So the twin has the extremal geometry — $N\Omega = 1$ exactly — with no charge anywhere near it. An observer there, applying Einstein's equation on $B_{\rm rep}$, would infer an **effective Maxwell-form stress tensor** — anisotropic, with the radial dependence of electromagnetic stress and algebraic strength $Q_{\rm eff}^2 = M^2$ — with no Maxwell field present to source it: **Maxwell-form gravitational stress without Maxwell matter.** The first explicit instance of the dark-sector target (§XI.2, item 8).

**(b) Exact frozen matter cannot exist on a closed representational base.** Majumdar–Papapetrou needs every centre charged with the *same* sign; Gauss on the closed $S^3$ needs the total to vanish. So a compensating charge must exist, and it breaks the balance. **In a closed projective universe, frozen matter is at best approximate.**

**(c) The extremal sector is observationally poorly sampled, but this theory does not exploit that.** Law I is Einstein–Maxwell on $B_{\rm grav}$, so near a hole the gravity base carries Reissner–Nordström as its $R/\ell\ll1$ comparison solution, extremal case included **[C]**; the exact closed-arena solution is open (§X.10). A deviation would need a new ingredient — and must not resurrect the retired charge $P$, whose $M^2\geq Q^2+P^2$ is exactly the kind of extremality modification §XI.1 records as inconsistent with this architecture.

## X.7 The two factors, and subextremality as a quadratic positivity condition [V/D/S]

*The proposal's motivating payoff was censorship: two halves, one always the closer-in, giving a quadratic constraint against naked singularities. What is realized exactly is weaker and cleaner — an algebraic factorization under which Reissner–Nordström subextremality becomes a positivity condition.*

**The factorization [V].** Reissner–Nordström's conformal factor is a product of two harmonic functions:

$$\Omega = U_+U_-,\qquad U_\pm = 1+\frac{m_\pm}{r},\qquad m_\pm = \frac{M\pm Q}{2}$$

Each $U_\pm$ is harmonic in three dimensions. The **factor weights** $m_\pm$ sum to the mass and differ by the charge,

$$m_++m_- = M,\qquad m_+-m_- = Q$$

and the balance defect of §X.2 is exactly their product:

$$\boxed{\ N\Omega = 1-\frac{m_+m_-}{r^2}\ }$$

| hole | factor weights | |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Schwarzschild | $m_+ = m_- = M/2$ | equal weights; $\Omega = (1+M/2r)^2$ |
| extremal, $Q = M$ | $m_+ = M$, $m_- = 0$ | **one factor weight vanishes** |
| super-extremal | $m_+m_-<0$ | **one factor weight becomes negative** |

> **What this is, and what it is not.** It is an exact factorization of one conformal factor. The weights $m_\pm$ are *factor parameters*: nothing yet shows that they are separately ADM masses, physical sources, chiral constituent energies, or independent dynamical sectors. So "charge is the imbalance between the factors" is an algebraic statement, not a claim that electric charge consists of moving gravitational mass between two sectors. If the chiral branch (§XI.3) later derives $m_\pm$ as self-dual and anti-self-dual constituent energies, the stronger language will have been earned. It is an excellent target, not a result.

**Subextremality is a quadratic positivity condition [V].** Since $m_+m_- = (M^2-Q^2)/4$, for either sign of $Q$ and $M>0$,

$$|Q|\leq M\iff m_+m_-\geq0\iff m_+\geq0\ \text{and}\ m_-\geq0\iff N\Omega\leq1\ \text{everywhere}$$

And the singularity's location fits the reading: for $M = 1$, $Q = 3/2$, the factor $U_-$ vanishes at $r = 1/4$, where the areal radius is exactly $0$ **[V]**. **A naked singularity sits at the zero of a negative-weight factor.** The proposal's "closer-in half" is the lesser factor, whose zero $r = -m_-$ lies behind the origin exactly when its weight is non-negative.

> **This is not cosmic censorship.** Censorship is a *dynamical* statement: that evolution from admissible initial data does not produce naked singularities. The factorization rewrites Reissner–Nordström's horizon condition as a positivity condition. It does not show that super-extremal states cannot form.

**The bridge-twin swaps the factors [V].** The involution of §X.3 is $r\mapsto m_+m_-/r$, with its throat at $r = \sqrt{m_+m_-}$ — the geometric mean of the weights — and it exchanges the factors: $U_\pm(m_+m_-/r) = (r/m_\mp)\,U_\mp(r)$, verified. So the bridge-twin exists exactly when $m_+m_->0$; it degenerates at extremality and does not exist for a super-extremal hole.

**What would turn positivity into exclusion [S/O].** Law I is Einstein–Maxwell, and super-extremal Reissner–Nordström solves it: the theory as it stands excludes nothing. A future principle requiring every hole to carry its bridge-twin *would* exclude super-extremal holes, since no twin exists when $m_+m_-<0$. Whether extremality would then be dynamically unattainable is a further question: the throat's divergence in §X.3 is suggestive of the third law, but a geometric divergence is not a dynamical bound. Such a principle would follow if the bridge-twin were the arena's twin (§X.3). And the tempting reading of the factors as *source and twin* fails: the seed makes every source's twin barren, whereas here only the extremal hole has a vanishing weight. What the factors are — plausibly the two chiral sectors — is open.

## X.8 The horizon as a ring one dimension up [V/D]

*The fallback: in the picture with 3-dimensional canonical shells, the analogue of Kerr's ring should sit one dimension up, and perhaps it is the horizon.*

**The dimensional count is exactly right [V].** Shift the $n$-dimensional Green's function $|x|^{-(n-2)}$ by $ia$ along one axis. The complex distance vanishes where that coordinate is zero *and* $|x| = a$ — an $S^{n-2}$ of radius $a$ in the orthogonal hyperplane, with the shifted function harmonic everywhere else, verified for $n = 3,4,5$. In three dimensions that is Kerr's ring, $S^1$. **In the four-dimensional cover it is an $S^2$**: shells go $S^2\to S^3$, and the singular set goes $S^1\to S^2$. A shift along the *meridian* direction places that $S^2$ in the representational base, centred on the source.

**And its symmetry is charge-like [D].** A shift preserves only the rotations fixing its direction. Within three-dimensional space, $SO(3)\to SO(2)$: axisymmetric — the symmetry of Kerr, which is **spin**. Along the extra direction, $SO(4)\to SO(3)$: spherically symmetric in the slice — the symmetry of Reissner–Nordström, which is **charge**. A real structural echo of Kaluza–Klein, where charge lives in the extra direction. And it points to the one form of the original mechanism §X.4 does not immediately kill: displacement along the meridian that is **imaginary** rather than real. Obstruction (1) concerns real positions on the meridian; whether the meridian quotient extends to complexified displacements is undetermined.

**But the $S^2$ is a singularity, not a horizon [D].** A horizon is a *regular* null surface; Kerr's ring is not Kerr's horizon but what the horizon dresses. And the singular $S^2$ has the naked signature: on the slice the shifted function is $1/\big((r-a)(r+a)\big)$, a linear factor vanishing at $r = a>0$ — the same algebraic form as the zero of a negative-weight factor in §X.7. **So the fallback, taken literally, produces exactly the kind of object the first idea wants to forbid**; the right analogy is *singular sphere $\leftrightarrow$ ring*, with the horizon as whatever dresses it — and whether anything does is the question of §X.7 again.

**Two further gaps [O].** No field in the present theory obeys a four-dimensional Laplace equation — the only one, the old Law II equation of versions 6–14, is retired with its permeability $\mu(\rho)$ — so the shifted Green's function has nothing to describe yet. And even granting one, it would not reproduce Reissner–Nordström on the slice: $1/(r^2-a^2)$ is not $U_+U_-$. The symmetry matches; the functional form does not.

## X.9 An extremality angle, and the amplification hope [V/D/S]

*A further proposal: an angular parameter $m$ — perhaps the imaginary part of the complexified meridian coordinate — such that a half-turn carries a hole to its twin; to first order $M\sim Q/m$, so that with $m$ measured on a leaf of large radius $\ell$, $M\propto Q\ell$, and a small charge yields a sizable mass. Schematically the two halves of §X.7 become, on a sphere,*

$$U_\pm = 1+\frac{q}{\sin r\,\sin m}\big(1\pm\cos r\cos m\big)$$

*Three parts of this land exactly. The amplification does not.*

**What the formula is: an angle on the extremality circle [V].** Near the source the halves carry $q\cot(m/2)$ and $q\tan(m/2)$, so $M = 2q/\sin m$ and $Q = 2q\cot m$:

$$\frac QM = \cos m,\qquad\frac\delta M = \sin m,\qquad\delta = 2q$$

The Reissner–Nordström family lies on the circle $Q^2+\delta^2 = M^2$, and $m$ is the angle around it: $m = \pi/2$ is Schwarzschild, $m\to0$ extremal. The prefactor is not the charge but $\delta/2 = \sqrt{m_+m_-}$ — the throat radius of §X.7.

**Real angle, subextremal; imaginary angle, super-extremal [D].** Since $\cos(i\theta) = \cosh\theta>1$ and $\sin(i\theta) = i\sinh\theta$, an imaginary $m$ is a hole with $Q>M$ and imaginary $\delta$. **The subextremal domain is exactly the real range of the extremality angle** — a statement about a parametrization, not a mechanism. And were $m$ literally an imaginary displacement along the meridian, it would land in the super-extremal domain — the verdict §X.8 reached by another route. *That holds for the displacement read as an imaginary **angle**; read as the rapidity it natively is, it covers the subextremal domain instead (§X.11).*

**The antipodal map swaps the halves, exactly [V].** $U_\pm(\pi-r) = U_\mp(r)$ identically. Writing $U_\pm = 1+\frac{q}{\sin m}\frac{1}{\sin r}\pm q\cot m\cot r$: the $1/\sin r$ piece is antipodally *symmetric* — a same-sign pole at the twin, the twin theorem's shape — and carries the mass; the $\cot r$ piece is *antisymmetric* — an opposite pole, the $\cot\chi$ Green's function — and carries the charge. So $\Omega = U_+U_-$ is twin-symmetric, as the seed requires of gravity, while the charge flips sign at the twin, as version 6's retracted identity had it.

**As written it solves nothing; corrected, it is exact [V].** On the unit $S^3$, $\cot r$ is harmonic but $\Delta(1/\sin r) = 1/\sin r$, so the formula is schematic. The exact version uses the conformal-harmonic pair $1/\sin(r/2)$ and $1/\cos(r/2)$ — both annihilated by $\Delta-\tfrac34$ — and drops the $1$:

$$\phi^2 = \chi\eta,\qquad\chi = \frac{A}{\sin(r/2)}+\frac{B}{\cos(r/2)},\qquad\eta = \frac{B}{\sin(r/2)}+\frac{A}{\cos(r/2)},\qquad A^2 = \cot\tfrac m2,\ B^2 = \tan\tfrac m2$$

Mapped to flat space by $\rho = 2\lambda\tan(r/2)$, the physical metric $\phi^4g_{S^3}$ becomes $\Omega^2\delta$ with $\Omega = U_+U_-$ and halves $2\lambda\cot(m/2)$, $2\lambda\tan(m/2)$ — **exactly isotropic Reissner–Nordström, with $Q/M = \cos m$** **[V]**. The swap $\chi(\pi-r) = \eta(r)$ holds exactly.

> **What the exact version is.** The poles at $r = 0$ and $r = \pi$ are the bridge's two asymptotic ends, its throat is the equator $r = \pi/2$, and the antipodal swap is the bridge-twin of §X.3. **The formula, made exact, is the Einstein–Rosen bridge seen on its natural three-sphere** — confirming §X.3 and §X.7 from a third direction. *That three-sphere is a conformal frame of radius $\delta/4$, not this theory's arena; §X.10 says why the difference is decisive.*

**Why the amplification does not survive [V/D].**

1. *The scale is not free.* In the exact version $m_+m_- = 4\lambda^2 = \delta^2/4$, so the sphere's radius is $\lambda = \delta/4$ — set by the hole's own non-extremality, not by a galaxy or the cosmos. There is no free $\ell$ to amplify with.
2. *Small $m$ is near-extremality, not small charge.* $Q = M\cos m\approx M$ as $m\to0$; what is small is $\delta$. "A large $M$ with small $Q^2$" is, in this parametrization, a large $M$ with small $M^2-Q^2$.
3. *The numbers run the other way.* In geometrized units the electron has $Q/M = 2.0\times10^{21}$ and the proton $1.1\times10^{18}$ — far out on the **naked** side, at $m = 49.8\,i$ and $42.2\,i$. Elementary particles need mass *suppressed* relative to charge, not amplified; astrophysical bodies are neutral, where $M\sim Q/m$ would give $M\approx0$; and a local relation of the same shape — mass tied to a length through the closure identity — already failed in §VIII.3, by eleven to twenty-two orders.

**A consequence for any exclusion principle [D].** Point 3 constrains §X.7: any principle excluding super-extremal holes must be stated for *classical black holes*, not for elementary particles, which violate $Q\leq M$ by up to twenty-one orders of magnitude. Quantum objects are not Reissner–Nordström solutions — their Compton wavelengths dwarf both their gravitational and their electric radii — but a principle stated too broadly would forbid the electron.

**A pointer, not a result [T/S].** Kerr–Newman's bound is $a^2+Q^2\leq M^2$: spin and charge enter on an equal footing, and the extremality circle becomes a sphere, $a^2+Q^2+\delta^2 = M^2$. Since $a$ is an imaginary displacement within space (§VI), the bound reads *as if* charge were the component of a single imaginary displacement along a further direction — the charge-like symmetry of §X.8. Suggestive; not established.

## X.10 Kerr–Newman, and what the exact three-sphere is — and is not [V/T/D/O]

*§X.9 found that the two-halves formula, corrected to half-angles, is an exact solution. This section asks the questions that decide whether that matters here: does it extend to spinning holes; is it genuinely a single hole; does it alter electromagnetism; and, above all, does it live on this theory's closed base?*

### The Kerr–Newman form [V/T]

Newman–Janis applied to Reissner–Nordström's Kerr–Schild function $2H = 2M/r-Q^2/r^2$, with $\frac1r\to\frac12\big(\frac1\rho+\frac1{\bar\rho}\big)$, $\frac1{r^2}\to\frac{1}{\rho\bar\rho}$ and $\rho = r+ia\cos\theta$, gives

$$2H = \frac{2Mr-Q^2}{r^2+a^2\cos^2\theta}$$

— the Kerr–Newman function **[V]**. Its horizons are the roots of

$$\Delta = r^2-2Mr+a^2+Q^2 = (r-r_+)(r-r_-),\qquad r_++r_- = 2M,\qquad r_+r_- = a^2+Q^2$$

With $(Q,a) = M\cos m\,(\cos\psi,\sin\psi)$ one finds $\delta^2\equiv M^2-a^2-Q^2 = M^2\sin^2m$ **[V]**:

$$\boxed{\ Q^2+a^2+\delta^2 = M^2,\qquad r_\pm = M(1\pm\sin m)\ }$$

**The extremality circle becomes a sphere.** The subextremal domain is the real range of the polar angle $m$; the azimuth $\psi$, apportioning the budget between charge and spin, is free.

**Two sets of halves, and which survives [V/T].** Reissner–Nordström factorizes in two gauges:

| gauge | halves | differ by | product |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| isotropic (§X.7) | $(M\pm Q)/2$ | $Q$ | $\delta^2/4$ |
| areal — the horizons | $(M\pm\delta)/2 = r_\pm/2$ | $\delta$ | $Q^2/4$ |

exchanged by $Q\leftrightarrow\delta$, i.e. $m\to\pi/2-m$ **[V]**. **For Kerr–Newman only the horizon halves survive.** The isotropic factorization needs conformally flat slices, and Kerr admits no axisymmetric conformally flat slicing that reduces to Schwarzschild's (Garat–Price) **[T]**. The $U_+U_-$ of §X.7 belongs to non-spinning holes.

**Charge is not displaced [V].** In the derivation the mass term is complexified — $1/r$ becomes the real part of $1/\rho$ — but the charge term is inserted by hand as $1/|\rho|^2$. Newman–Janis treats spin as a displacement and charge as a squared modulus. The bound treats them alike, $a^2+Q^2$; the derivation does not. **"Charge as a displacement along a further direction" (§X.8, §X.9) remains a suggestion.**

### The exact three-sphere is a single monopole hole [V]

The worry that it might secretly be a multipole or two-body configuration can be settled. $\phi$ depends only on the polar angle of the three-sphere, so it is $SO(3)$-symmetric about the axis through both poles: **a pure monopole.** And the two poles are **not two bodies**: they are the two asymptotic ends of a single Reissner–Nordström bridge, each with ADM mass $M$ — the isometry of §X.3 exchanges them — and the hole itself is the **equatorial throat**. So "source at $r = 0$, twin at $r = \pi$" is reinterpreted as *the two ends of one bridge*.

### Electromagnetism: untouched on the bridge, not on the geon [V/T]

The time-symmetric slice has scalar curvature exactly $2E^2 = 2Q^2/R^4$ — the Einstein–Maxwell constraint, verified. On the two-ended bridge the field is ordinary radial Maxwell flux threading the throat; outward-oriented at each end it reads $+Q$ at one and $-Q$ at the other — **Wheeler's charge without charge** — and nothing about electromagnetism is modified. **That ceases to be true under the geon quotient**: identifying the two ends reverses the field, and ordinary $U(1)$ electromagnetism does not descend; the gauge group must be enlarged to $O(2)$ (§X.3). So identifying the far end with the arena's twin would be an assertion about makeup twice over — the twin would carry $-Q$, reopening version 6's retraction (§X.5), *and* the global gauge group would have to gauge charge conjugation.

### But it does not live on this theory's base [V/D]

This is the point on which everything turns, and it is the genuine risk of smuggling.

**Its three-sphere is the wrong one.** The exact solution's $S^3$ has radius $\delta/4$ and is a *conformal frame* of an open, two-ended space. This theory's base is the Einstein static universe on a sphere of radius $\ell$, with time-symmetric constraint

$$R_{\rm phys} = 2E^2+16\pi\bar\rho+2\Lambda = 2E^2+\frac{6}{\ell^2}$$

The exact solution has $R_{\rm phys} = 2E^2$. **It misses by exactly the closure term, everywhere [V].**

**And the construction cannot be carried over [V].** On the unit sphere the constraint reads $-8\Delta\phi+6\phi = 6\phi^5$ for this theory's base, against $-8\Delta\phi+6\phi = 0$ for the $\Lambda = 0$ vacuum. The second is linear — the conformal Laplacian — so poles superpose and halves can be multiplied; the first is **nonlinear**. The building block $1/\sin(r/2)$ solves the vacuum equation exactly and fails the theory's, with residual $-24/\big((1-\cos r)^2\sin(r/2)\big)$. Without superposition there is no two-halves product on the real base.

**The ends change character as well [V].** Near a singular point the $\phi^5$ term dominates, and $\phi = Cr^{-1/2}$ balances it when $C^4 = 1/3$. The physical metric is then $\tfrac13\big[(d\ln r)^2+d\Omega^2\big]$ — **a cylinder of radius $\ell/\sqrt3$**, not an asymptotically flat end. With the closure source present, the far side of a throat is a Nariai-like tube, as in the periodic Schwarzschild–de Sitter slice.

> **So "is there a tweak that satisfies the field equations for real?" has a two-part answer.** *Yes* — in standard Einstein–Maxwell, with no change to electromagnetism: the half-angle formula is an exact single-monopole Reissner–Nordström hole drawn on a *conformal* three-sphere, subextremality condition $m_+m_-\geq0$ intact, and Kerr–Newman supplies its spinning form. *Not yet* — on this theory's closed base, where the constraint is nonlinear, superposition fails, and the ends become tubes. **An exact charged hole in the Einstein static universe is open.** Near any real hole the difference is of relative order $R^2/6\ell^2$ (§IV.10), negligible near any hole — about $10^{-9}$ at a parsec — but some $6\%$ at 10 kpc in the galactic arena: close to a hole every observation sees the exact solution, while on the arena's own scale it does not.

### Does the complexified meridian supply the angle? Not as an angle [D]

The complexified meridian is $\mathbb{C}^\times$: a compact real angle and a non-compact imaginary part. The real angle is precisely what gravity cannot see (§X.4), so it cannot parametrize the base geometry. The imaginary part admits no half-revolution, and trigonometric functions of an imaginary argument turn hyperbolic — the naked side (§X.9). And in the formula itself the twin is reached by $r\to\pi-r$, a change of *position*; $m$ moves nothing, but labels the hole's charge-to-mass ratio. *Superseded in part by §X.11: read not as an angle but as the rapidity it is on $\mathbb{C}^\times$, the imaginary part does supply $m$ — its real range covering exactly the subextremal domain.*

### Circular orbits, as a sanity check [V]

For a neutral test particle in Reissner–Nordström, $\Omega^2 = M/R^3-Q^2/R^4$, and a static observer measures

$$v^2 = \frac{MR-Q^2}{R^2-2MR+Q^2}$$

In the isotropic radius this takes a two-halves form, each half weighted by the square of the *other* half's factor:

$$\boxed{\ v^2 = \frac{r\,\big[m_+(r+m_-)^2+m_-(r+m_+)^2\big]}{\big(r^2-m_+m_-\big)^2}\ }$$

| hole | $v^2$ |
|:--|:--|
| Schwarzschild, $m_\pm = M/2$ | $4Mr/(2r-M)^2$ |
| **extremal**, $m_- = 0$ | $M/r$ **exactly** |
| any, far away | $\to M/r$ |
| naked, $Q>M$ | negative for $R<Q^2/M$: gravity repulsive, no circular orbits |

> **In the frozen sector, the circular-orbit speed is exactly Newtonian in the harmonic radius.** At extremality $r = R-M$, and wherever timelike circular geodesics exist their local speed is $v^2 = M/r$ with no corrections — which does not mean every radius admits a stable circular orbit — the kinematic face of the harmonic, linearly superposing fiber factor of §X.2. And the repulsive core that would expose a naked singularity sits, whenever $Q\leq M$, safely inside the horizon, since $Q^2/M\leq M\leq r_+$.

For Kerr–Newman in the equatorial plane, verified:

$$\Omega = \pm\frac{\sqrt{MR-Q^2} }{R^2\pm a\sqrt{MR-Q^2} }$$

— prograde and retrograde split by the spin, with the same $MR-Q^2$ deciding where orbits exist.

**No plateau, as there should not be.** All of these fall as $v^2\to M/R$ because the exact solution is asymptotically flat. The plateau of §IV.8 comes from the closure term — the very term found missing above. The sanity check is consistent with, not in tension with, the rest of the theory.

## X.11 A rapidity coordinate, and what "emergent $M/Q$" would need [V/T/D/S]

*The proposal's deepest intuition: charge is not an independent parameter but a structural feature of the canonical space, merely manifesting as charge on the base — and the imaginary part of the complexified meridian, read as a polar angle, supplies the extremality angle $m$. The second half yields a clean coordinate. The first would need a mechanism, which is not yet in hand.*

**The imaginary meridian coordinate as a polar angle — via a rapidity [V].** The complexified meridian is $\mathbb{C}^\times$: $w = e^{i(\chi+i\mu)} = e^{-\mu}e^{i\chi}$, with $\chi$ the compact real angle and $\mu = -\ln|w|$ non-compact. Compactify $\mathbb{C}^\times$ to its Riemann sphere and $\mu$ becomes a latitude, $\tan(\vartheta/2) = e^{-\mu}$:

$$\cos\vartheta = \tanh\mu,\qquad\sin\vartheta = \operatorname{sech}\mu$$

Setting $m = \vartheta$ gives $Q/M = \tanh\mu$. The real meridian, $\mu = 0$, is the Riemann sphere's equator and the neutral hole; the poles $\mu\to\pm\infty$, absent from $\mathbb{C}^\times$, represent the extremal boundary.

> **Real $\mu$ parametrizes exactly the subextremal domain $|Q|<M$, and $\mu\to\pm\infty$ represents its extremal boundary.** Both are statements about a coordinate. They do not prevent nature from occupying the super-extremal domain, and "infinite $\mu$" is not dynamical unattainability unless some dynamics makes finite evolution produce finite changes in $\mu$. *(This refines §X.10, whose "no" took $\mu$ as an imaginary angle, where $\cos(i\mu) = \cosh\mu$ lands in the super-extremal domain; read as a rapidity, $\mu$ covers the subextremal domain instead.)*

**In this coordinate, the parameters lie on a timelike cone [V].**

$$(M,Q) = \delta\,(\cosh\mu,\ \sinh\mu),\qquad M^2-Q^2 = \delta^2,\qquad m_\pm = \tfrac\delta2\,e^{\pm\mu}$$

The Reissner–Nordström parameter space is represented as a $1+1$-dimensional timelike momentum cone with invariant $\delta$. A shift of $\mu$ acts on $(M,Q)$ as a Lorentz boost (verified), rescaling the factor weights oppositely, $m_\pm\to e^{\pm\beta}m_\pm$, at fixed product; extremal parameters lie on the null boundary and super-extremal ones are spacelike. The bridge-twin's exchange of factors (§X.7) is $\mu\to-\mu$, i.e. $Q\to-Q$, consistent with the $\pm Q$ at the bridge's two ends (§X.10). **These are properties of a parametrization.** That the ADM mass and electric charge *literally are* components of a higher-dimensional momentum is a separate claim, not established here.

**Where the parametrization stops [V].** The electron's $Q/M = 2.0\times10^{21}$ admits no real rapidity; its parameters lie far outside the cone. The picture is for classical holes — the boundary §X.9 drew.

**What would make it physics: the Kaluza–Klein intuition, and its limits [T/D].** There is a classic mechanism in which charge *is* momentum — momentum along a compact extra direction appears downstairs as charge. Two claims must be kept apart:

| claim | status |
|:--|:--|
| **particle intuition**: $(E,p_5)$ behaves as momentum data, with $M^2-Q^2$ an invariant mass | standard, for point particles |
| **black-hole reduction**: a charged hole downstairs from a boosted vacuum solution upstairs | **not Reissner–Nordström** — vacuum Kaluza–Klein reduction gives Einstein–Maxwell–dilaton theory with coupling $a = \sqrt3$ |

In this theory the candidate extra direction would be the meridian, which would sidestep §X.4's obstruction: that concerned *position* along the meridian, whereas Kaluza–Klein turns *momentum* into charge. But the route carries three costs, each an architectural change to the frozen Parts 0–IX:

| cost | why |
|:--|:--|
| **a dilaton** | a circle of dynamical size couples its radius to $F^2$, and freezing it is consistent only when $F^2 = 0$ — against Law I's "no additional scalar", and yielding dilatonic rather than Reissner–Nordström holes |
| **electromagnetism relocated** | charge would couple to the meridian connection, which would *be* the Maxwell field — replacing Law IV's separate field on $B_{\rm rep}$ |
| **a free circle action** | the meridian has no global orientation — the twin reverses it (§V.4) — and the static pencil has fixed points at the poles |

And one consequence: were charge meridian momentum, the twin's reversal of the meridian's orientation would make it **change sign at the twin**.

**The cross-chiral fact [V/T].** Independently of any mechanism, charge's gravitational footprint is two-sided. In Newman–Janis for Kerr–Newman,

$$2H = M\Big(\frac1\rho+\frac1{\bar\rho}\Big)-\frac{Q}{\rho}\cdot\frac{Q}{\bar\rho}$$

— mass as a **sum** over the two chiral centres, charge squared as their **product** — mirroring the spinor form of the Maxwell stress tensor, $T_{ab}\propto\phi_{AB}\bar\phi_{A'B'}$, self-dual times anti-self-dual. So the natural home for "charge as structure" is the chiral composition of §XI.3, and a sharp theorem target is whether a Kim-style composition of charged chiral constituents produces the $Q^2$ cross-term.

## X.12 Status of this branch [D]

**An internal hierarchy.** Part X is not all of one kind, and should be read by grade:

| grade | content |
|:--|:--|
| **exact general-relativity facts** | $N\Omega = 1-(M^2-Q^2)/4r^2$; $\Omega = U_+U_-$; the bridge inversion and its throat; the infinitely long extremal throat; Reissner–Nordström on a *conformal* $S^3$; the Kerr–Newman extremality sphere; circular-orbit speeds |
| **useful parameterizations** | the factor weights $m_\pm$; the extremality angle $m$; the rapidity $\mu$; the $(M,Q)$ hyperbola |
| **structural parallels** | the $\mathbb{RP}^3$ geon; charge conjugation at the bridge's far end; the two-factor and chiral pictures |
| **projective hypotheses** | $m_\pm$ as canonical or chiral sectors; $\mu$ as a meridian rapidity; the bridge-twin as the arena's twin |
| **not established** | charge emergence; any censorship mechanism; dynamical unattainability of extremality |

Graded this way the branch is stronger, because what remains speculative no longer hides inside identities that are simply properties of Einstein–Maxwell theory. But the grading also makes plain that nearly all the exact content is standard: this theory supplies the *vocabulary* — $\Omega$ as fiber length, the twin — and not yet the physics.

**So the branch is frozen here.** It is kept as a record of what the extremal sector looks like in this theory's variables, and should not be extended without a numerical solution on the closed base (§XI.2, item g), a decision to unfreeze Parts 0–IX, or progress on the question below.

**The fork it surfaces.** The structural routes to charge the branch has examined — the Reissner–Nordström bridge and its geon, and orientation-reversing meridian momentum — each give the twin charge-conjugate structure, as do the Hopf pairing across each meridian (§V.4) and the proposal's own formula (§X.9). Parts 0–IX chose an electromagnetically barren twin with its compensating charge elsewhere (§IV.7), after version 6 retracted the twin's opposite charge. What is established is precise, and limited:

> **A structural-charge realization of these types is incompatible with the present barren-twin Maxwell assignment.**

It is *not* established that charge in this theory must arise by either route. So there are three choices, not two:

1. **keep Law IV and the barren twin**, and regard the bridge, geon and Kaluza–Klein correspondences as mathematical analogies only;
2. **promote structural charge**, and revise the electromagnetic sector of the twin;
3. **find a third structural mechanism** whose charge information does not descend as a conjugate field at the twin.

**The sharpest question it leaves [O].** The charged geon (§X.3) shows that the global quotient of a charged bridge does not carry ordinary $U(1)$ electromagnetism: the involution sends $F\mapsto-F$, and the quotient exists only once the gauge group is enlarged to $O(2) = U(1)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_2$, with charge conjugation gauged. So:

> **Does the global projective quotient naturally enlarge $U(1)$ to $O(2)$?**

If it does, "the twin is charge-conjugate" stops being a metaphor and becomes a statement about the actual global gauge bundle — choice 2, made structural. That is the most interesting thing Part X has uncovered. **§X.13 answers it**, and in doing so makes choice 1 strongly favored within the summed architecture.

## X.13 Does the projective quotient enlarge $U(1)$ to $O(2)$? [V/T/D]

*§X.12 left this as the sharpest question of the branch. It has a precise answer, and the answer settles most of the fork.*

**Within Parts 0–IX: no, because nothing electromagnetic is quotiented [D].** Law IV places Maxwell on the representational base, and light is precisely the field that resolves representatives — "light resolves a representative; gravity resolves a class." The gravity base receives electromagnetism only through stress-energy, which is quadratic in $F$ and blind to its sign. No gauge bundle ever descends to $\mathbb{RP}^3$, so none is enlarged.

**What descent requires [V/T].** Suppose instead that the field on the cover were required to satisfy $\tau^*F = \pm F$. The twin map $x\mapsto-x$ on $S^3$ preserves orientation — $\det(-I_4) = +1$ — so $\tau^*$ commutes with the Hodge star, and $\tau^*F = \pm F$ gives $\tau^*\rho = \pm\rho$:

| | untwisted, $\tau^*F = +F$ | twisted, $\tau^*F = -F$ |
|:--|:--|:--|
| on the quotient | an ordinary $U(1)$ field on $\mathbb{RP}^3$ | an $O(2)$ field: charge conjugation around the non-contractible loop |
| charge at the twin | the **same** sign | the **opposite** sign |
| Gauss's law on the closed $S^3$ | a charge and its twin total $2Q\neq0$: net charge on each sheet must vanish | a charge and its twin total $0$: satisfied automatically |
| Gauss–Stokes | $d{\star}E = \rho$ on a closed orientable slice integrates to $\int\rho = 0$: **net neutrality** | the charge density is valued in the local system $\mathbb{R}_{\rm C}$ twisted by the charge-conjugation character $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^3) = \mathbb{Z}_2\to\{\pm1\}$ — not the orientation character, since $\mathbb{RP}^3$ is orientable — so there is **no globally defined signed total charge** and Stokes imposes no neutrality; lifted to $S^3$, an anti-invariant density integrates to zero automatically |
| an isolated charged body | **forbidden** | **allowed** — its compensator is its own conjugate twin |

In cohomological terms the two columns are the invariant and anti-invariant parts of $H^3(S^3) = \mathbb{R}$, on which $\tau$ acts by its degree, $+1$, so that $H^3(\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R}$ while $H^3(\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb{R}_{\rm C}) = 0$ **[T]** — but the Gauss–Stokes statement is the physics. So the answer to §X.12 is sharper than yes or no:

> **The projective quotient does not itself enlarge $U(1)$.** Untwisted Maxwell descends perfectly well, as ordinary $U(1)$ — but then Gauss's law on the compact slice forbids an isolated net charge. **A descended isolated charge requires twisted descent**: charge conjugation gauged around the non-contractible loop, hence $O(2)\simeq U(1)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_2$, the minimal charge-conjugating extension, as in the charged geons.

**But neither way of making it descend survives [V/D].**

*(a) Keep Maxwell on the cover, and let gravity sum over sheets as Law I does.* A conjugate twin then carries real field energy, and $T^{(g)}_b = \sum_{r^{-1}(b)}\big((dr)^{-1}\big)^*T$ counts it — since $T[-F] = T[F]$. **Every electrostatic field energy would gravitate twice**, while inertia stays local on the cover, so active and passive gravitational mass would differ by the fraction of a body's mass stored as electrostatic energy. From the nuclear Coulomb energy that fraction is $2.3\times10^{-3}$ for iron-56 and $1.5\times10^{-3}$ for aluminum-27, a difference of $8.5\times10^{-4}$; lunar laser ranging bounds the difference between their active-to-passive ratios at $3.9\times10^{-14}$ (Singh, Müller, Biskupek, Hackmann and Lämmerzahl 2023, a hundredfold improvement on Bartlett and van Buren's $4\times10^{-12}$). If a fraction $\eta$ of electrostatic energy is duplicated in active mass only, the offset is $\approx\eta\,\Delta f_C$, so **$\eta\lesssim5\times10^{-11}$: an order-one duplicate is excluded by about ten orders of magnitude** **[V]**, within the assumptions listed below. It would also give every charged hole an effective charge $\sqrt2\,Q$, and extremality at $|Q| = M/\sqrt2$.

*(b) Let the Maxwell field itself descend — identified rather than summed, as in the geon.* Charge then gravitates normally, but light lives on the gravity base and resolves classes rather than representatives, contradicting the seed's defining sentence. And the twin would carry the conjugate of every emission: a **luminous** twin rather than a dark one — whose light, in the galactic arena of §IV.8, would arrive in about $1.6\times10^5$ years at $\ell = 16$ kpc.

> **The structural lesson — and its limits.** The quotient map $q$ is an identification, here as in the geon; what differs is how fields meet it. **Law I's source reduction sums resolved representatives over each fiber**, whereas a geon's gauge field is required to descend equivariantly — and the two agree exactly when the twin is empty. **Within the summed-source architecture, an order-one duplicate of electromagnetic energy at the twin is excluded extremely strongly**: $\eta^{\rm EM}_{\rm twin}\lesssim5\times10^{-11}$. That rests on four assumptions, each natural under the present Law I but each a theory choice: (1) twin field energy enters $T^{(g)}$ as an additional copy; (2) that nonlocal copy does not enter inertial or passive mass; (3) nuclear Coulomb energy maps into the duplicate with the same fractional strength; (4) no other global bookkeeping of field energy avoids counting one physical field twice. So the twin is electromagnetically barren to about $10^{-10}$ *in this realization*; exact barrenness is not derived. The lunar bound carries its own modeling assumptions too, chiefly about the Moon's interior, which its authors note are critical.
>
> The section also sharpens the comparison with the geon. There, geometry and the Maxwell bundle descend *together*; here, gravity descends through the quotient while Maxwell remains representative-resolving. **A quotient of the geometry need not be a quotient of every field living over it** — which is exactly what the seed says.

**What this does to the fork of §X.12 [D].**

| choice | status after this section |
|:--|:--|
| 1. barren twin, correspondences as analogies | **strongly favored**: within the summed architecture, order-one electromagnetic content at the twin is excluded, $\eta\lesssim5\times10^{-11}$ |
| 2. structural charge with a conjugate twin | **excluded in its global form**, under the stated assumptions: global twin-equivariance duplicates electromagnetic energy in active mass (summed) or abandons the seed (descended) |
| 2′. a conjugate mouth **for charged black holes only**, via the bridge-twin of §X.3 | **survives** — a local configuration, not a global equivariance. Untested, since astrophysical holes carry negligible charge, with a sharp consequence: effective charge $\sqrt2\,Q$ and extremality at $\lvert Q\rvert = M/\sqrt2$ |
| 3. a third structural mechanism | untouched |

**Consequences for the open items of §XI.2 [D].** Charge without charge on $S^4\setminus\Gamma$ (item a), taken as a global structure, *is* twin-equivariant electromagnetism, and falls under (a) or (b): excluded in its global form. The bridge-twin as the arena's twin (b) and "every hole carries its bridge-twin" (d) survive only in the hole-specific form 2′. And the $O(2)$ question (e′) is answered.

## XI.2 Open

*The one-arena move leaves three immediate mathematical jobs: (i) pose the static Green problem intrinsically on $\mathbb{RP}^3$, begun in §IV.1; (ii) derive the origin of the uniform closure source, item 0′; and (iii) construct the canonical-to-optical observable map, item 0.*

0. **Compact gravity, noncompact optics — now the sharpest structural problem.** *Can $g_G$ control gravitational dynamics on a compact projective arena of galactic radius $\ell$, while Maxwell and detector observables recover an effectively noncompact optical geometry — reproducing local GR, lensing, surface brightness as actually measured, and distance duality?* §V.1 sets the optical metric to $r^*g_G$; with that, a galactic $S^3$ caps angular-diameter distances at $\ell$ and dies observationally (§IV.8). So either $g_{\rm optical} = r^*g_G$ and the galactic arena is abandoned, or the optical sector needs new structure and V.1 must change. Such a construction must say which standard optical premise it generalizes — the single optical metric, null-geodesic propagation, photon-number and phase-space conservation, or the map from canonical histories to detector observables — since ordinary metric optics conserves surface brightness and obeys $D_L = (1+z)^2D_A$. The right next object is an **observable map** $\mathcal O:\{\text{canonical photon histories}\}\to(z,\theta,F,\Delta t,\text{image shape})$: what it predicts for $D_A$, $D_L$, surface brightness and lensing shear will show whether Etherington reciprocity or Liouville's theorem has to fail, instead of deciding in advance. **This problem belongs to the model itself**: our own galaxy's sky already holds the LMC and M31 beyond the cap. The detached wing's apparent-scale rule (Part XII) would be one application of the same construction, not a separate problem, and the author's canonical-optical-readout idea, now written out as Part XIII, is a candidate for it — subject to the constraint of §XIII.3 that only phase-type holonomy survives inside standard quantum mechanics. Answered, the galactic arena becomes new geometry; otherwise it fails cleanly.

0′. **How can Law I's source map generate the closure background?** At galactic $\ell$ the Einstein-static arena needs a uniform gravity-base source of $2$–$2.6\times10^{17}M_\odot$ for $\ell = 13$–$16$ kpc, some four million times the Galaxy's baryons (§IV.8). Law I takes its source from the meridian reduction of *resolved matter*. If the background is real stress-energy that is absent optically, the model has introduced a huge dark component; if it is geometric, it belongs on the left-hand side of Law I, or must emerge from the quotient itself — for instance from a degenerate primary spread across all meridian classes, which would have to be derived explicitly. This is the second structural problem, alongside item 0.

1. **The global $E$** (§II.1b). Part I's shell lives on the *normal space*, a space of vectors; passing to nearby points of the middle space uses the exponential map, which is local, with curvature, cut loci and caustics obstructing it globally. Whether **one** six-dimensional $E$ restricts to the unit Hopf bundle around **every** worldline at once is unproved. **The version's central construction problem** — and a sharper one than what it replaces, since the local spin geometry is settled and only the gluing is not. ## XI.3 Chiral composition: two bridges and two obstructions [T/V/O]

*Kim's result (§XI.2) suggests a bridge to the chiral sector. Two parts of it are established, two are blocked, and one is a stated theorem target. None is folded into the architecture.*

### Bridge 1 — the shell group is the diagonal of the chiral pair [T]

Complexified four-dimensional spin geometry factorizes, and the stabilizer of a non-null direction $u$ sits **diagonally** inside it:

$$Spin(4,\mathbb{C})\cong SL(2,\mathbb{C})_L\times SL(2,\mathbb{C})_R\ \supset\ \Delta SL(2,\mathbb{C}) = SL(2,\mathbb{C})_{\rm shell},\qquad g\mapsto(g,g)$$

whose compact form is the familiar $Spin(3)\cong SU(2)\hookrightarrow Spin(4)\cong SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$. **This is standard group theory, and it places the shell group of Part I rather than positing it.** The whole chain reads

$$Spin(4,\mathbb{C})\ \xrightarrow{\ \text{stabilize }u\ }\ SL(2,\mathbb{C})_{\rm diag}\ \xrightarrow{\ /\,\mathbb{C}^\times\ }\ Q^2_{\rm aff}\ \xrightarrow{\ \text{real form}\ }\ S^2$$

with dimensions $12\to6\to4\to2$ **[V]**. Given $u$, the rest of §I.2–§I.4 is much less arbitrary than it looked: **one shell spin group is the diagonal residue of two chiral spin groups.**

### Bridge 2 — self-dual Taub–NUT carries the same shell type [T]

In Gibbons–Hawking form $ds^2 = V^{-1}(d\tau+\omega)^2+V\,d\mathbf{x}^2$ with $\nabla\times\omega = \nabla V$, the potential $\omega$ is a Dirac monopole and each constant-radius three-surface is a circle bundle over the angular $S^2$ — for unit charge, the Hopf fibration $S^1\to S^3\to S^2$. **So the elementary chiral constituents of Kim's construction carry the same shell *type* as the canonical shells here.** More than a coincidence of names.

### Obstruction 1 — the same bundle, the reciprocal warp [V]

With $V = 1+2m/r$ and a dimensionless Hopf angle $\psi$, Taub–NUT's constant-$r$ slice is

$$g_r = Vr^2\,d\Omega_2^2 + 4m^2V^{-1}\big(d\psi+\cos\theta\,d\phi\big)^2$$

so with $b$ the horizontal scale and $c$ the Hopf-fiber scale, $b^2 = Vr^2$ and $c^2 = 4m^2V^{-1}$.

**(a) The warp factors are reciprocal.** The horizontal coefficient carries $V$; the vertical carries $V^{-1}$. Law I does the opposite of reciprocal — it warps base metric and fiber *alike*, both by $\Omega^2$. **That is the obstruction**, and it is stated in the warp factors deliberately: the physical radius of a fixed-coordinate shell also carries the factor $r$, and in Taub–NUT both physical scales in fact shrink toward the nut. *(An earlier table here showed the warp $V^{1/2}$ under a heading suggesting a physical length; withdrawn.)*

**(b) The shell is squashed everywhere except the nut.**

$$\frac cb = \frac{2m}{r+2m}$$

| $r/m$ | $V$ | $c/b$ |
|--:|--:|--:|
| 10 | 1.200 | 0.167 |
| 1 | 3.000 | 0.667 |
| 0.3 | 7.667 | 0.870 |
| 0.01 | 201.0 | 0.995 |

A **Berger sphere** for every $r>0$ — most squashed far away — and round only in the limit $r\to0$ **[V]**. *An earlier formula, $1/V^2r^2$, compared an unnormalized fiber coordinate with dimensionless angles, and its "round at $r = 1-2m$" added a pure number to a length. Retired (§XI.1).*

> **The shells agree as bundles and disagree as geometries.** So "derive the fiber-length locking inside the chiral Taub–NUT sector" is not available: done naively it gives the wrong sign *and* the wrong metric. The sharp question is instead **why the same Hopf topology should carry a reciprocal metric law** — and no answer should be adopted merely because it repairs the sign.

### Obstruction 2 — this branch cannot derive time [D]

The timelike direction $u$ is presupposed **three times over**: by $N_\gamma = \{\xi:g_\mathbb{C}(\xi,\dot\gamma) = 0\}$, which needs $\dot\gamma$; by Kim's stationary composition, which uses $u^\mu$; and by Bridge 1 itself, since *which* diagonal depends on which direction is stabilized.

> **A composition law defined on this shell architecture cannot derive $u$, because $u$ already defines the architecture on which it acts.** This closes the chiral route to time in the present version. Rebuilding $E$ *prior* to choosing a real form or a non-null direction, and showing a diagonal reduction emerges dynamically, would be a different and more foundational version — not a result available here.

### The theorem target — an equivariance test, stated without prejudging [O]

Kim's composition contains a bilinear $\ell^{\dot\alpha\alpha}\propto\tilde o_+^{\dot\alpha}o_-^{\alpha}$. Let $U(1)_H\subset SL(2,\mathbb{C})_{\rm diag}$ act on the two principal-spinor lines with weights $q_\pm$. Then the **necessary and sufficient local phase-invariance condition for that bilinear** is

$$\boxed{\ q_+ + q_- = 0\ }$$

verified: with $h_\theta = \operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta},e^{-i\theta})$ acting diagonally, opposite-weight lines give an invariant bilinear and same-weight lines do not **[V]**. *Local, not global:* full descent of the composed field through $E\to\mathcal{M}$ needs more — compatible normalizations, patches, connections and gluing. The weight condition is the algebraic core of that theorem, not the whole of it.

*An earlier formulation of this test — "identify $U(1)_H$ with the anti-diagonal of $U(1)_L\times U(1)_R$" — is retired* **[R]**. It conflated two different objects: the **diagonal $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$**, which is a spacetime spin-group embedding, with an **anti-diagonal $\mathbb{C}^\times$**, which is the projective rescaling redundancy of a bilinear's two representatives. $U(1)_H$ lies in the diagonal, and the cancellation must come from *representation content*, not from relocating the subgroup by fiat. Stated as a weight condition, it is something to prove rather than to assume.

### What the branch is actually for [S]

Not time, and not the fiber-length locking. The natural job is

# PART XII — DETACHED WING: OTHER GALAXIES

> **Status: detached by design.** The model is a single galactic arena — ours — with no cosmological slot. This part is scoped but deliberately **not attached**: the aspiration to derive the Tully–Fisher relation for *other* galaxies. Its intended route is a later abstraction or reframing — an optical rule relating canonical histories to the sizes and fluxes an observer assigns — and **not** the construction of an arena metric for each galaxy, which is taken in advance to be a dead end. Nothing here is a claim of the model. The computations below were run once, as exploratory compatibility checks: they map what any future rule must deliver, and they are not evidence for one.
>
> **What any attachment must satisfy [D].** In the reading that survives (reading (i) below), the rule enlarges a galaxy's apparent size by up to about five times *without* enlarging its inferred mass. **Ordinary metric lensing cannot do that.** Geometric-optics lensing conserves surface brightness — Liouville's theorem: magnification scales flux and solid angle together, so apparent size grows as the square root of the magnification while inferred mass grows as the magnification itself, leaving inferred surface density unchanged. That is precisely the literal reading (ii), which the checks below exclude. And Etherington reciprocity, $D_L = (1+z)^2D_A$, holds whenever spacetime is metric, photons follow null geodesics and photon number is conserved. So an attached rule must say which of these premises it generalizes — the single optical metric, null-geodesic propagation, photon-number and phase-space conservation, or the map from canonical histories to detector observables. What this shows is that the rule cannot be ordinary magnification — not yet that it must break surface-brightness conservation. A readout that changes the inferred angular-diameter distance, or the assignment of physical radius, rather than magnifying angular area, might preserve measured specific intensity while changing inferred size. The observable map of §XI.2's item 0 will decide which premise, if any, has to fail. Either way it is a new optical law, not a lens, and it must still reproduce local GR, lensing, surface brightness as actually measured, and distance duality. It is the same construction §XI.2's item 0 asks for.
>
> **The acceptance test, if the wing is ever attached [D].** One optical map, one arena rule, at most one or two universal parameters, and **no per-galaxy freedom** — then predict all 175 SPARC rotation curves and their photometry. Until a rule of that kind exists, the wing stays detached.

## XII.2 The surface-brightness test, with a thin disk [V]

**The test.** The plateau coefficient depends on $h/\ell$, and under the arena law $h/\ell$ is fixed by surface density — so the lemma predicts a surface-density dependence of the Tully–Fisher normalization, which the observed relation lacks: it has about $0.1$ dex of scatter and **no residual trend with surface brightness**. The size of the predicted trend depends on the galaxy's geometry. A spherical exponential profile gave offsets exceeding a decade. > Below that, **no arena radius of any kind** puts a thin exponential disk on the observed relation **[V]**. The threshold sits at MOND's transition surface density, because it is set by the same constant; the physical difference is that MOND's asymptotics depend only on enclosed mass, while the plateau depends on how the mass is spread relative to $\ell$.

# PART XIII — RENDERING, AGREEING STRUCTURE AND CANONICAL OPTICS: THE VERSION-17 BRANCH [T/D/V/S/O]

*This part collects the author's newer intuitions and gives each the most precise form currently available. Parts 0–XI are unchanged by it, and nothing here is yet a law. Its purpose is to find the right abstraction — and to discard quickly whatever the abstraction cannot support.*

## XIII.0 The intuitions

**Canonical optics — the camera principle.** An observed event need not be a unique point of canonical space. Several distinct canonical endpoints may constitute *the same* observable event, provided their readout agrees: their positions must project to exactly the same point (**rule A, exact**), while their directions need only agree to within the resolving power of whatever physical channel could record the difference (**rule B, resolution-limited**). A camera is only the example; what matters is whether a physically instantiated record could tell the alternatives apart. The slogan: *the observed event is rigid downstairs; the space of its canonical realizations may be flexible upstairs.*

**Agreeing structure.** One point of the underlying space might appear *twice* in an output space, each appearance keeping different parts of a common structure — as if one "painted a logo on an object in the underlying space and quotiented different parts of it away so as to turn it into two apparently different logos." Would anyone have noticed? No: each appearance keeps something the other lacks, so the two read as two things.

**Centrades.** A centrade is a canonical object that can be packed and unpacked. Fully unpacked in the Kerr direction it carries no angular momentum; in the Reissner–Nordström direction, no charge. A field is the irreducibility that remains. Unpackings conflict — unpacking one can limit another, "whack-a-mole" — so there is a budget.

**Many rendered spacetimes.** Each global unpacking scheme outputs its own visual spacetime. Ours is singled out not by the laws but by the vast number of microstates and the relational complexity it supports; other renderings — every Kerr hole tuned to $a = 0$, say — are equally outputs of the same substrate, inaccessible because the patterns that make up an observer do not survive in them. The picture: a hexagonal sheet of richly linked nodes, each carrying an infinite single-file tower above and below. Its inhabitants would never notice the towers, though they move through them.

**The galactic plane and the spiral.** The stress-energy upstairs may be roughly isotropic, with the galactic plane the fixed set of an involution. And a star passing either of the two points per radial period where its velocity is perpendicular to its position might be rendered on the opposite side, so that the metric's precessing rosettes look like spiral arms.

## XIII.1 Three things version 16 already contains [D/V]

Before any new structure is added, the frozen architecture already realizes three of these intuitions in miniature.

**One class, two appearances.** The representational slice double-covers the gravity slice: each gravity-base point $b$ appears twice, as $x$ and its twin $\tau x$, and the two appearances carry different content — the matter at $x$, none at $\tau x$. That is the agreeing-structure picture exactly: one gravitational class, two appearances retaining different parts of it, which light reads as two places. **The seed is the first instance** (§XIII.4).

**A family of renderings over one gravity base.** Swapping source and twin on a meridian leaves the reduced source $T^{(g)}$ — a sum over both appearances — unchanged, and so leaves $B_{\rm grav}$ unchanged. With the conditional selection rule of §V.4 there are $2^N$ occupancy assignments for $N$ meridians, all rendering over one and the same gravity base. This realizes, modestly, the strongest answer to the invariance question of §XIII.6: $B_{\rm grav}[U] = B_{\rm grav}$ for every rendering $U$.

**A budget that is not a product.** The Hopf classes at the crossings are $\pm1$ each, but Kirchhoff's condition forces their total to vanish, so flipping one forces a compensating flip elsewhere. The admissible sign patterns — 6 for two meridians, 70 for four — do not factor meridian by meridian (§V.4). That is a verified, if tiny, whack-a-mole budget: local states under a global, non-product compatibility condition.

## XIII.2 Canonical optical kinematics [T/D/O]

**The state space and the readout [D].** Given an optical projection $Q_{\rm opt}$ from canonical space to an optical base, a canonical optical state is a point $e$ with a direction $[v]$ whose image $[dQ_{\rm opt}(v)]$ is null in the optical metric; the states form a null-projective subbundle of $\mathbb{P}(TE)$. The readout at resolution $R$ is

$$\mu_R(e,[v]) = \mathcal C_R\big(Q_{\rm opt}(e),\,[dQ_{\rm opt}(v)]\big)$$

where $\mathcal C_R$ coarse-grains directions at the resolution of the physically available channel. **Rule A:** $Q_{\rm opt}(e_1) = Q_{\rm opt}(e_2)$ exactly. **Rule B:** the angle between $[dQ_{\rm opt}(v_1)]$ and $[dQ_{\rm opt}(v_2)]$ is below $\epsilon_\theta(R)$.

> **A type subtlety [D].** Rule B defines a *tolerance* relation, not an equivalence: $v_1\approx v_2$ and $v_2\approx v_3$ need not give $v_1\approx v_3$. Turning "observational equivalence" into an equivalence relation requires partitioning directions into resolution cells, which then declares two nearly identical directions distinct whenever a cell boundary happens to separate them. Physically, distinguishability is graded rather than binary; the clean object is the overlap of records (§XIII.3), with rule B as its coarse shadow.

**Connection, curvature and holonomy [T].** With the vertical bundle $V_{\rm opt} = \ker dQ_{\rm opt}$ and a horizontal complement, $TE = H_{\rm opt}\oplus V_{\rm opt}$, every base path lifts uniquely to a horizontal path through each starting point. The curvature $\Omega_{\rm opt}$ — the vertical part of the bracket of horizontal lifts — measures the failure of those lifts to close: lifting a small loop of area $A$ returns to a point displaced vertically by about $\Omega_{\rm opt}\cdot A$, and for principal connections the Ambrose–Singer theorem makes the holonomy algebra the one generated by curvature transported along the lifts. This gives the heuristic $\|\Omega_{\rm opt}\|L^2\ll1$ its invariant form: **new effects are suppressed exactly when the holonomy of every loop an experiment can close lies below that experiment's resolution.** "Same measured endpoint, different canonical endpoint" — $Q_{\rm opt}({\rm Hol}_\gamma(e)) = Q_{\rm opt}(e)$ with ${\rm Hol}_\gamma(e)\neq e$ — is precisely nontrivial holonomy. Whether the connection's curvature is small locally and large globally is a property to be derived, not assumed **[O]**.

**Which base optics resolves [D/O].** The seed says light resolves a representative in $B_{\rm rep}$. But version 16 supplies no projection onto $B_{\rm rep}$: it is embedded in the middle space, not a quotient of it. The natural candidate, retraction along the meridians, is two-valued, because each meridian crosses $B_{\rm rep}$ at a source and at its twin. **So an optical readout through the meridians is itself a correspondence, not a map** (§XIII.4). A separate optical base is needed only if the optical geometry must depart from $r^*g_G$ globally — the problem of §XI.2's item 0.

**The two hidden directions [D].** If the vertical bundle contains both the Hopf-phase and the meridian directions, their holonomies act very differently: holonomy along the Hopf phase shifts a *phase* — the same state, reached with a different phase — while holonomy along the meridian displaces the canonical *position*, producing a different state. The next section shows why that difference decides the branch.

## XIII.3 Coherence: what geometry can and cannot change [T/D]

**Standard quantum mechanics [T].** Alternatives interfere only if they end in the same final state of *everything* — system, detectors and environment. If the complete final states are $|\Psi_1\rangle$ and $|\Psi_2\rangle$, the interference term carries their overlap, $\Gamma_{12} = \langle\Psi_2|\Psi_1\rangle$. No detector need be involved: if the system's own final states differ — as two genuinely distinct canonical endpoints would — then $\Gamma_{12}\to0$ and the alternatives add as probabilities, not amplitudes. For a coarse final class $Y_f$ of orthogonal states $f$,

$$P(Y_f) = \sum_{f\in Y_f}\big|\langle f|U|i\rangle\big|^2$$

and the coherent class amplitude $\mathcal A(Y_i,Y_f) = \int_{\mu_R(\gamma(1)) = Y_f}\mathcal D\gamma\,e^{iS/\hbar}$ is standard only when the class contains a single state.

**The dilemma [D].** "Several distinct canonical endpoints constitute one observable event" therefore has three readings:

1. The endpoint differences are **physical**. Then they are themselves which-path records; the alternatives are orthogonal and add incoherently. No new interference — at most, a loss of it.
2. They are **gauge**, a redundancy of description. Then there is no physical spread to exploit.
3. The composition rule of quantum mechanics changes, so that amplitudes add coherently across physically distinct states. That is genuinely new physics, facing every precision interference test there is.

**The way through [D].** One realization survives inside standard quantum mechanics: the holonomy of a *phase*. When two alternatives reach the same final state along different base paths, their relative phase is the holonomy around the loop they enclose — the Aharonov–Bohm pattern, physical even though the connection itself is not. **The Hopf-phase direction can therefore carry the proposal coherently**: same observable endpoint, different canonical phase, observable as an interference shift. The meridian direction cannot, unless meridian position is unrecordable in principle; displacement along it is which-path information, and its signature would be decoherence rather than new images. The Aethic determinacy criterion $\mathrm{Det}_R(\xi_1,\xi_2)$ is then best defined as the overlap of the complete final states, with rules A and B as its coarse readout.

**Signatures [S].** Phase-type holonomy would appear as path-dependent interference phases in closed optical loops, beyond the known Sagnac and gravitational phases — tightly constrained locally. Position-type spread would appear as a loss of fringe visibility accumulating with path length — constrained by long-baseline interferometry. Neither is multiple imaging: ordinary lensing gives one source many images; this gives one image many canonical histories.

## XIII.4 Agreeing structure [T/D]

**Rendering as a correspondence [T].** A map cannot send one point to two. If one underlying point can appear twice in a rendered space, the primitive object is a correspondence — an incidence space of appearances with two legs,

$$\mathfrak X\xleftarrow{\;s\;}\mathcal I_U\xrightarrow{\;t\;}V_U$$

with $s(i_1) = s(i_2) = x$ while $t(i_1)\neq t(i_2)$. Fundamental identity and rendered location come apart. The version-16 seed is the case in which the underlying space is the gravity slice and each class appears as a point and its twin (§XIII.1). The substrate is written $\mathfrak X$ so as not to change what $E$ means.

**What each appearance keeps [D].** Give appearance $i$ a kernel $K_i\subset T_x\mathfrak X$ of forgotten directions, so that its rendered tangent space is $T_x\mathfrak X/K_i$. Three levels of information then sit in a lattice, $K_1\cap K_2\subset K_1,\,K_2\subset K_1+K_2$:

| quotient | what it holds |
|:--|:--|
| $T_x\mathfrak X/(K_1\cap K_2)$ | what the two appearances reveal together |
| $T_x\mathfrak X/K_i$ | what appearance $i$ shows by itself |
| $\mathcal A(i_1,i_2) = T_x\mathfrak X/(K_1+K_2)$ | **the agreeing structure** — what both retain, and so the only ground on which they can be compared |

with $\dim\mathcal A = \dim\mathfrak X-\dim K_1-\dim K_2+\dim(K_1\cap K_2)$. In terms of observables, if $\mathcal O_i$ are those surviving appearance $i$, agreement lives in $\mathcal O_1\cap\mathcal O_2$. **Which-path distinguishability between two appearances is governed by how differently they quotient, not by any separation of underlying points.** And the question "would humans have realized it by now?" has a precise answer: an inhabitant who sees only $T/K_1$ in one place and $T/K_2$ in another has no internal reason to identify them — exactly as with the logo.

## XIII.5 Centrades: fields as unfinished reductions [T/S/O]

**A centrade [D].** Provisionally, a centrade is a local carrier of several potentially incompatible reductions. Centrade $C_i$ has a space $P_i$ of unpacking states; a global unpacking $U = (u_i)$ must satisfy compatibility conditions $\mathcal C_\alpha(U) = 0$, and the admissible unpackings

$$\mathscr U_{\rm adm} = \Big\{U\in\prod_iP_i\;:\;\mathcal C_\alpha(U) = 0\Big\}$$

do not form a product. **That non-product structure is the budget**; no additive conservation law is assumed. The Hopf-sign condition of §V.4 is a verified miniature (§XIII.1).

**Fields as residual obstructions [T/S].** The reversal — a field measures what failed to unpack, not something packed in — has standard precedents. An almost complex structure fails to be integrable by exactly its Nijenhuis tensor; a $G$-structure fails to be torsion-free by its intrinsic torsion; a connection fails to be flat by its curvature. In each case the "field" is the obstruction to completing a reduction **[T]**. The proposal is that charge and spin are obstructions of this kind — $Q\sim{\rm Obs}_Q(U)$ and $a\sim{\rm Obs}_K(U)$, each vanishing when its channel is completely unpacked **[S]**.

**The Kerr–Newman budget [T/S].** Kerr–Newman's horizon condition, $a^2+Q^2+\delta^2 = M^2$ (§X.10), puts the family on a unit sphere in $(a,Q,\delta)/M$ — the charged unit circle of §X.0 extended by a rotational axis. Schwarzschild sits at $\delta = M$, fully unpacked in both channels; extremal Reissner–Nordström at $Q = M$; extremal Kerr at $a = M$. Reading this as a competition among reductions is new physics; general relativity supplies only the identity.

**The relative chiral modulus [D/O].** The complexified-meridian modulus of §X.0 must be a *relative* modulus between the two chiral sectors, $w_{\rm rel}\sim\lambda_+/\lambda_-$, surviving after their common rescaling is quotiented — not the absolute $\mathbb{C}^\times$ of the shell, which may be pure redundancy. With that, $|w_{\rm rel}|^2 = m_-/m_+$ reads as a packing ratio: $\mu = 0$ is balanced and chargeless, and $|\mu|$ measures how unevenly the two chiral sides remain packed. Whether such a relative modulus is a genuine invariant is open.

## XIII.6 Many rendered spacetimes [S/O]

**The renderer [D].** A renderer $\mathcal R:\mathscr U_{\rm adm}\to\mathfrak M_{\rm vis}$ assigns each admissible unpacking $U$ a rendered spacetime $V_U$. Ours is one point of $\mathfrak M_{\rm vis}$, not the substrate.

**Why ours [S].** Many microscopic unpackings render the same macroscopic world. Its multiplicity, $\Omega[V] = \mu\big(\mathcal R^{-1}([V])\big)$, and entropy $S[V] = \log\Omega[V]$ could make one rendering overwhelmingly represented. The tower picture adds a second factor, **relational complexity**. A single-file tower has many nodes but no cycles — its first Betti number $\beta_1 = E-V+C$ is zero — while a hexagonal sheet has an extensive number of independent cycles, and with them the redundancy that lets a pattern maintain itself. Visibility would track the capacity for redundantly supported relational structure, not the amount of underlying stuff. An observer-persistence functional $\mathscr P(O,U)$ — how well the relations constituting a complex self-maintaining pattern survive unpacking $U$ — can stand in for talk of consciousness: in other renderings the underlying material persists, but no coherent pattern of "you" does.

**What stays invariant [O].** Is $B_{\rm grav}[U] = B_{\rm grav}$ for every $U$, as in the miniature of §XIII.1; only isomorphic; or itself rendered? Is $E$ invariant, or a rendered descendant of a deeper $\mathfrak X$? These are the architectural questions of the branch.

**The diagram analogy [S].** A Feynman diagram is a term in the expansion of an amplitude, not a trajectory. The disciplined form of the analogy is that rendered classical spacetime is to the substrate as the semiclassical, tree-level description is to the full sum, with quantum corrections as residual sensitivity to structure that the macroscopic rendering suppresses. Both the Dirac electron and a Kerr–Newman hole have $g = 2$ classically, and the electron's anomaly $a_e$ comes from loops. That coincidence is a signpost, not evidence; the conjecture would be earned only by deriving $a_e$ from unpacking sectors.

## XIII.7 The galactic plane, and the orbital flip [T/V/D]

**The plane as a fixed set [T].** On $S^3\subset\mathbb{R}^4$ the involution $\jmath(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4) = (x_1,x_2,x_3,-x_4)$ squares to one and fixes a great two-sphere, locally a plane. An isotropic source upstairs with a planar visible locus is therefore kinematically possible; why luminous matter would select the fixed set is open.

**The orbital flip, computed [V].** The intuition — at the two turning points per radial period, where velocity is perpendicular to position, a star is rendered on the other side — has two readings that differ in the plane. "The opposite radial side", $\mathbf x\mapsto-\mathbf x$, is a half-turn and preserves handedness; a genuine "handedness flip" is a reflection. Both were integrated on precessing orbits in a flat-rotation-curve potential, and on a disk of 3000 phase-mixed orbits:

| | (i) opposite radial side, a half-turn | (ii) handedness flip, reflection across the radial line |
|:--|:--|:--|
| a single orbit | keeps circulating prograde, but jumps $2r$ across the centre at every turning point — continuous only in a two-sheeted glued space | **collapses exactly onto one fixed half-arc**: a non-precessing spiral segment, traced back and forth |
| a disk of stars | cannot create structure: the rendering commutes with rotations, so an axisymmetric disk stays axisymmetric; it acts only on odd-$m$ patterns | no global pattern either; and every star reverses its rendered azimuthal motion each half radial period |

Reading (ii) does something remarkable to single orbits. In any spherical potential, consecutive half-arcs of an orbit are mirror images across their shared apsidal line, so a rosette is literally built from reflections — and the handedness flip applies exactly those reflections in reverse. Each star's rendered path becomes one stationary spiral segment, spanning about half a turn in this theory's closed-arena potential **[V]**. But it pays with motion: for half of every radial period a star would appear to move backward, so the rendered disk would show no net rotation — contradicted by Doppler rotation curves and by stellar proper motions, which show coherent rotation. Reading (i) keeps the motion but, commuting with rotations, cannot turn an axisymmetric disk into a spiral **[D]**, as the simulation confirms: all low-order Fourier amplitudes stay at the noise level.

So **neither simple reading makes spirals from a symmetric disk**. What survives is the observation that a handedness flip freezes rosettes into spiral segments — which a subtler rendering, treating positions and velocities differently or not commuting with rotations, would have to reconcile with coherent rotation **[O]**.
