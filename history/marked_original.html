Where else is this happening, and to whom?

You can trace, point by point, where the improvised wording of my 2023 manuscript already carried the exact structures I would only later state in proper academic terms. What makes that worth seeing isn't my ideas — it's that this can happen at all, and that it is surely happening to others right now. Click to read why.

The public vocabulary we use to describe our own thinking is not a neutral medium. It emerged from one dominant cognitive branch, and it behaves less like a common tongue than like a branch-specific dialect that every other branch must translate itself into. When a neurodivergent person reaches for that vocabulary to describe their internal cognition, the words rarely fit as calibrated: they end up assigning different semantics to the same words, or the same semantics to different words, because the allistic terms on offer were tuned to a different set of cognitive intuitions than the ones actually driving the thinking. That forces a quiet choice — point at the right topics, or use the descriptive words non-superficially — and the choice is invisible from both sides of the conversation. Terms get redefined by extrapolation, or worn as mere labels, and no one involved is aware of what was actually arrived at. I coined the term linguistic masking to give this a name.

There is a second layer, and it is visible throughout the annotations. When you hold a framework internally and know its downstream implications from experience, you write as though the reader can see them too — and they can't. When I described what I now call the extrusion principle, I wrote about consciousness “jumping realities.” From inside, I could see plainly that this implies an entire counterfactual and modal structure; but I had never heard of counterfactual semantics as a field, so I had no way to flag the implication for a reader, and the reader had no way to know it was there. The annotations try to surface exactly this: not just which principle each phrase maps to, but what it quietly implies from inside that is invisible from outside.

Watching this happen to my own work changed how I read everyone else's. I now ask what linguistic masking is hiding in the words of other thinkers on the margins. This page exists to make the phenomenon concrete: with my case laid out, you can trace, point by point, where an improvised arrangement of words corresponds one-to-one to structures I could only articulate years later, once I had the academic vocabulary to carry them.

I hope you find this story as eye-opening as I have — not because of my particular ideas, but because of what it shows is possible at all. The burning question is where else it is happening right now, and who else among the neurodivergent population is getting zero help extracting the substance from their own linguistic masking.

Solving the Measurement Problem: A Comprehensive Model Based on Three Postulates

Ajax Benander — May 2023

Abstract

The measurement problem is one of the most notorious and difficult open questions in all of physics. Why is it that quantum states seem to change their appearance just because of viewing or lack thereof? In the following paper, a possible solution will be explained using only three simple postulates which seek to generalize the concept of quantum superposition beyond just the subatomic world, and in the process remove any hints of questionable anthropocentric bias from the quantum theory. At its finest, this reasoning is potentially at once the missing piece to quantum theory that Einstein longed for, and the confirmation of correctness that Bohr was after.

1. Introduction to the Aethus

The fundamentals of Quantum Bayesianism [3], or QBism for short, will be assumed to be accurate for the sake of this paper's argument. In fact, they will be incorporated into a fundamental postulate, the first of three to be proposed in these pages, which, if accurate, will serve as a complete and sufficient explanation for the measurement problem.

This first postulate, named the postulate of subjective reality, argues that since QBism proposes that quantum states are only in effect relative to the observer, then in fact, through a generalization, all states in the universe are only in existence relative to an observer. This may seem inherently solipsistic, but it is not at all under the right interpretation. Instead, it shares more similarities with the basic argument that the universe may only be seen from a perspective. When absent of a perspective, all of reality loses its meaning. This argument works in much the same way.

In order to avoid a detrimental anthropocentric bias, we will give a name to the arbiter of reality, perspective, or what have you, which is human-independent. Let this reality-perceiving entity be referred to as an Aethus. By virtue of the simple fact that you, the reader, are currently conscious and observing reality, you have an Aethus, which itself is directly representative and integrated with such things. An Aethus is a vantage point to the universe, but one which is strictly connected to it in both directions, such that reality loses meaning in the absence of an Aethus, and also an Aethus loses meaning in the absence of a corresponding reality to attach it to. In other words, reality comes in pairs – a reality coupled with its Aethus.

Postulate 1 (The Postulate of Subjective Reality) Anything may only be real relative to an Aethus.

A key next step is to declare what an Aethus actually stands for regarding a person's experience of reality. This is that a person's Aethus represents the collective presentation of all known truths that are currently accessible to them. That is, an Aethus is synonymous with a person's knowledge. Specifically, however, it is the deepest degree of such things, such that even if a person interprets said truths incorrectly and believes to know something differing from the contents of their Aethus, and still the Aethus remains unaffected by such things, all the while remaining definite and truthful depending exactly on what is accessible for certain.

For these reasons, an Aethus is very much like a set, in that it may contain or disclude “elements”, so to speak. The difference is merely in the fact that an Aethus is somewhat more concrete, and is physically attached to an organic being so that it may perform organic tasks. For a human, the classic five senses, among other things, add to that human's Aethus by virtue of teaching them this or that, and so, in effect, they gain a fundamental knowledge of the world around them. So, of course, other animals may have Aethae as well, as they are just as capable of collecting and storing mental information. Note that this does indeed open the doors to fictitious or collective Aethae.

Note that an Aethus is constant and unchanging, which naturally means that the same creature or person in a different time possesses a different Aethus, since, afterall, the timecode is known to be different, which itself is separate information, hence implying a separate Aethus.

2. Aethic Superposition

This section is where the first major deviation from our classic expectations for reality will be made. And yes, these deviations apply to all levels of scale, meaning that the assumption that the “quantum world” is somehow inherently different from the “classical world” will be argued against.

The first argument is that of quantum superposition. The idea now is that any single object or concept will be in superposition, that is relative to an Aethus, strictly following wherever it cannot be explained by said Aethus. That is, any attribute of reality which is independent of an Aethus, which simply put, means it exists “outside” the scope of said Aethus, will occupy all possible states at once that it may occupy without influencing the Aethus. For example, if your back is turned long enough for a parking lot to be filled with cars, then assuming your friend has parked in the lot, their car occupies every single space at once relative to your Aethus at the time. It is, in effect, in superposition. This of course sounds like an absurd proposition, as arguably humans are directly wired to object to such a concept, but it is what would be implied by the framework of the Aethus.

A natural objection that a human might make in the face of such a hypothesis, is that one may simply invalidate the claim that one's friend has parked, at least partially, in all spaces at once, by checking, and sure enough, seeing the car parked at a single location. But, naturally, the Aethus framework has an explanation: simply by checking that the car is in a specific location, you have birthed a new Aethus entirely from your own Aethus, by simply adding the car's location to the mix. That is, you added the property of “car parking space index” to your Aethus, pulling a precisely random element from the superposition in the process. By definition, this new Aethus is a separate Aethus because its contents are not identical, however it may be argued that it is a kind of “child” Aethus of the old one, meaning it is identical except for added attributes. Even though the time code is different between the two Aethae, we may argue that this new one is still a child of the old one if we imagine attaching that old time code to itself as we might do with spatial displacement, (as in, at this time I held these coordinates. Now we say, at this time I held this time. This does mean that time is now two separate concepts — the coordinate time and the Aethic time, but this will be further elaborated on later).

Note that this process is not as simple as simply ceasing the superposition of one concept — rather it is more like compressing the superposition in one particular way by virtue of “saturating” the Aethus with some hint of further information — but information that is nonetheless never entirely binary and fully known. For example, perhaps you mistake a similar looking car for that of your friend's, leading you to falsely believe your friend's car being in that spot is within your Aethus. By simply believing your friend's car is in that spot, your Aethus has nonetheless contracted the superposition of your friend's car, because the likelihood of a car thought to be that of your friend's being that of your friend's is higher than the likelihood of a randomly selected car. This is all a way of saying that the superposition was still altered in some way, as now the Bayesian stakes are not identical, however still an attribute has not, nor ever will be, fully lifted out of superposition in one way or another.

Perhaps strangely, even though the parking location of your friend's car is now known, and thus is, at least roughly, “out of superposition”, still you must remember that before you checked, you were unaware of that car's location, so even now, with the car in plain sight, it is in superposition relative to your Aethus of five minutes ago. That is simply what such an Aethus would imply. In effect, because your Aethus has changed, so too has reality for you, because as mentioned earlier, a reality is only ever rooted to an Aethus by this framework, and is not objective. Your consciousness jumped realities so to speak.

The simple rule of an Aethus regarding superposition is that any concept in superposition relative to an Aethus has no sway on the observation of said Aethus in the moment, or in other words, is statistically independent to it. This automatically allows us to mark any Aethus which does not follow this rule as invalid. Furthermore, we may postulate the converse of this basic principle. Again, breaking this calls for the invalidity of an Aethus, so by taking the contrapositive of the conjunction of both rules, a second major postulate of the Aethus is attainable.

Postulate 2 (The Postulate of Superposition) If an Aethus is valid, then any given property will be in superposition relative to it if and only if it is statistically independent to it.

3. Aethic Validity

One immediate response to the postulate of superposition might be to use it as a tool for identifying invalid Aethae. For instance, by simply taking the contrapositive of the statement: If any given property of an Aethus is statistically independent to it but not in superposition relative to it, or in superposition relative to it but not statistically independent to it, then such an Aethus would be invalid. What this essentially argues for is two tools of determining the invalidity of an Aethus.

Using the claim that an Aethus containing a property which is in superposition relative to it is invalid, we may immediately modify the law of noncontradiction in a physical sense. Specifically, so long as an item is not possessed by an Aethus, it may exist in all cooperating states at once, which, of course, implies contradiction is merely prohibited only in the case of properties within an Aethus.

Beyond here, we may start to build the final major postulate of Aethic logic, which may seem more complicated because it harnesses two generations of Aethae instead of one, but is nonetheless quite simple at its heart.

The general concept is that for any central Aethus, such as your own Aethus at this moment, it follows that, logically, it should be impossible for you to necessarily wind up in an invalid Aethus. We may think to perhaps define this as follows: “If an Aethus is valid, then no child Aethus of it may contradict any of its properties.” This is close to the final realization, but still not quite accurate. To see why, we might argue that in order to prevent future Aethic invalidity through a child Aethus of your own, the universe diverts you away from such an invalid child Aethus by adding a property to your Aethus to prevent it from existing. That is, if there is only one obscure property which triggers a contradictory child Aethus, then simply because there are other options of child Aethae to avoid that signifies that all hope was not yet lost, hence proving that it is not invalid.

The solution to describing an Aethus which is necessarily doomed is slightly more specific. That is, it would have to be an Aethus for which no amount of property accumulation, no matter how clever, can prevent a potential contradiction. Such a concept may read as follows: “For some central Aethus, if for every one of its child Aethae there exists a further child Aethus that contradicts any of its properties, then said central Aethus is invalid.”.

The last note to make here is that this only covers the hopeless situation. However, if there are still options of child Aethae which necessarily prevent contradiction, then it simply follows that one of these savior Aethae must in fact take the form of your own. If there are many as defined by many properties, then as we can say for certain is that they are all in superposition for taking the form of your own Aethus until a further child Aethus picks one over the other. Let us now finalize this postulate:

Postulate 3 (The Postulate of Aethic Certainty) If some Aethus is valid, then there exists a child Aethus to it for which every one of its own child Aethae strictly do not contradict any of the properties of said Aethus.

This general concept is logically sufficient, however one interesting consequence of it may be quickly developed which implies that it has the potential to literally add to the Aethus itself. That is, given some central Aethus, if we are to remove all child Aethae of it which are invalid, we are left with, oftentimes, a condensed set of potential child Aethae. In effect, this puts the nature of all such condensed-possibility Aethae in superposition with the central Aethus itself. The defining additional properties to each child Aethus beyond the central Aethus must be in a mutually exhaustive superposition of existence relative to the original Aethus.

One last note about the yields of this postulate regards the state of transitivity of invalid Aethae to child Aethae. If we assume that some central Aethus is invalid as per the postulate of Aethic certainty, then it follows that for all child Aethae there exists a further child Aethus which is invalid. This naturally implies that every invalid Aethus must have all invalid child Aethae. To continue with the family vocabulary, if we suppose that a “parent” Aethus is the inverse of a child, then it follows that your Aethus is invalid if any one of your parent Aethae is invalid.

4. Solution to the Measurement Problem

We are now ready to, with the help of a real-world thought experiment, solve the measurement problem itself. That real world example will be the double-slit experiment.

A beam of electrons is fired at a twice-pierced plate. The experimenter begins firing the electron beam, and sure enough, the back plate is lit up with an interference pattern. If we analyze the Aethus of the experimenter, then we may note that such an Aethus is unaware of which slit each electron is passing through, and so, by the logic supplied by the first two postulates, we may argue that such a beam is in superposition relative to that experimenter's Aethus. Therefore, each electron passes through both slits at once, and winds up creating the interference pattern by the logic of Feynman's path integral. The back plate itself, we may argue, is like a kind of wave function collapse of sorts, because of the mere fact that where the electron touches this plate will be accessible to the Aethus of the experimenter. Therefore, when the experimenter updates their Aethus with the location of each electron, they will randomly select an item from the superposition, compressing it accordingly.

Then, of course, comes the classic headache – when we boot on the detector. This is precisely where the Aethic framework is needed, because of the scenario where the experimenter destroys the data before they look at it.

Let us suppose that you are the experimenter, and you have run the detector during the double-slit experiment, which is now over. However, you have yet to check the pattern on the back plate. You start by subdividing the result into two potential Aethae: one in which there is an interference pattern, and another in which there is not. This corresponds to the notion that the particle went through both slits at once, versus one slit but not the other in both ways, respectively speaking.

You decide to begin by analyzing the validity of the Aethus where you see an interference pattern when you look. In such an Aethus, it follows that it contains many child Aethae, each one of which corresponds to a potential scenario. However, to every one of those child Aethae, there is a potential for you to check the data on the detector, regardless of whether you actually do or not. That is, just due to the nature of quantum uncertainty, if a detector was not running, then it would be forever impossible to work out which slit, but because it was running in this scenario, it is continuously possible to work out which single slit the particle went through. Therefore, according to the postulate of Aethic certainty, this hypothetical Aethus must be invalid, in effect adding to the Aethus that it cannot exist, or in other words, removing it from the superposition.

All that is now left, then, is for the particle to either go through one slit and not the other in either way. So long as the detector has not yet been checked, it follows that, relative to your Aethus, the particle went through one slit but not the other in both ways, as is the superposition. Your Aethus, then, passes the test of the postulate of Aethic certainty, because there exists child Aethae which have no further contradictory child Aethae of their own. Such child Aethae are the scenario where the particle went through the “first” slit and not the second, and alternatively, through the second and not the first.

It may seem counterintuitive how this is different from going through both slits at once, but to Aethic logic it is certainly distinct – for instance, it does imply that one slit is not being passed through no matter what.

If, on the other hand, you are to keep the detector off during the running of the experiment, there are again several possibilities: either it goes through both slits at once, the first slit but not the second, the second but not the first, or neither. Because of the uncertainty principle, it follows that because no measurement was made of the particle's slit of traversal, then that information is permanently lost, and will forever remain outside any child Aethus of your own. Therefore, every one of your child Aethae cannot contradict the slit index property. All that is left is to apply the contrapositive of the postulate of superposition. Because we are fully aware that the particle's slit is forever unknown, it follows that, in order for your Aethus to be valid, it must be so that it is also in superposition. Therefore, the slit through which the particle passed is both slits at once, and hence the interference pattern is the direct result.

In effect, through the logic of the Aethus, the measurement problem is solved.

5. Applications

Let us now demonstrate the power of Aethic reasoning by applying it to certain mysteries of quantum physics, starting with quantum entanglement. Suppose that there are two people, Alice and Bob, who each possess an entangled particle held at opposite sides of the universe. If Alice checks her particle, then relative to her Aethus, Bob's particle must possess the opposite state. However, noteworthily, Bob's particle, at least relative to his Aethus, will not collapse its superposition when Alice checks her particle. Moreover, even after Alice has checked, relative to her Aethus from before she checked, both particles are still in an entangled superposition. And when checking the reverse case, we may also argue that relative to Alice's current Aethus after the check, the particle was always in the state she now observes. Therefore, we are to get around the problem of hidden variables vs. non-local-realness by simply stating that reality is what changed with the observation rather than any inherent property itself.

A simpler version is the notorious Schrödinger's cat itself. The experimenter, prior to observing the state of the cat, will not contain the wellbeing of the cat in their Aethus, and so therefore by the postulate of superposition such a thing will be in just that: superposition. Moreover, once the experimenter checks, their Aethus will add the state from a random spot in the superposition. As stated many times before, relative to their prior Aethus the cat is still and always will be in superposition, and so whatever state they uncover with their new Aethus will merely be the state of the cat relative to this present Aethus, and will accordingly be so for the future and immediate past as well.

One key concept to discuss is the Aethic implication for the nature of time. As was touched upon earlier, it is a requirement that the time as a dimension be separated from the time perceived by one's Aethus. One interesting consequence is a solution to time asymmetry by the second law of thermodynamics, through stating that time symmetry was never broken in the first place. Specifically, the Aethic logic argues that the second law of thermodynamics is a property of the Aethus rather than of time. A strange consequence is that if somehow time were to reverse in an instant, the universe would not merely play backwards, but entropy instead would continue to increase.

Therefore, the very act of reversing time would generate a new reality of time which has branched off of the old one, a notion we may perhaps label as “Anti-Time”, to reference an analogy to antimatter. The detailed physical consequences Ajax then sketches — velocity and magnetic field reversing while position and acceleration do not, the football's flipped trajectory, the biosphere slowly inverting — are vivid illustration rather than derived framework content.

While on the topic of time and alternate realities, a quick point of interest is to regard the Aethic perception of the multiverse. I refer to it as “The Aethic Tree”. The Aethic Tree is essentially the net product of recursively tracing out all valid parent and child Aethae to one's own, and repeating this process infinitely. After this has been done, the Aethic Tree will contain every possible Aethus in the universe, in effect equating to every possible reality. I have now written multiple papers on the subject of what happens when one applies the mediocrity principle to one's location on the Aethic Tree, and the results point to the Rare Earth Hypothesis. The application of the mediocrity principle to the Aethic Tree is what I call “the accordance principle”.

It is key in understanding the Aethic framework that it is interpreted so as to not imply unrelated or contradictory conclusions. Namely, it argues for a quantum universe of utmost absence of anthropocentric bias. In the case of Schrödinger's cat, the very nature of assuming that any human experimenter may collapse the wave function while the cat is but a leaf in the wind places a heavy divide between the consciousness of the cat and the consciousness of a human. An Aethus now attaches to any organism or concept, to the point where the human aspect of things is no more fundamental than a pebble or a dragonfly.

It is as if every human is fundamentally linked up to the grand ticking clock of the universe, which is not only outwardly relativity defying, but again assumes a kind of Cartesian interpretation of the place of humans with respect to the rest of nature. But with the Aethic perception, humans cause a wave function collapse not due to some ethereal status of ours, but because the human mind is a node in every last experiment we have or ever will do. Generally speaking, the fact that all observations made by all observers are consistent is quite derivable from Aethic reasoning while not straying into a human-oriented universe perception.

Back in the case of Bob and Alice, let us focus on Alice's Aethus. Suppose that Alice has already checked her particle and found spin A. From here, we may either add to Alice's Aethus that Bob sees B (the complement), or not. We may immediately derive that if he sees something other than B, such a version of Alice's Aethus is invalid by the postulate of Aethic certainty, because it holds a contradiction. Therefore, all that is left as valid is for Bob to see B, and so such a thing is automatically added to Alice's Aethus. In other words, their observations are consistent. This serves as a further lemma that no two Aethae within a reality may hold opposing information to that present within the other, hence implying that all human experimenters are consistent when accurate.

A further quick point of interest might be where human evolutionary buildup has gradually diverted from the Aethic mindset. One such example is the nature of imagining what is happening at some unseen location or time. By virtue of it being in superposition compared to your current Aethus, it follows that actually observing the happenings would be physically impossible, as one cannot observe a superposition. If anything, even the act of imagining what is happening is in effect generating a pretend Aethus inside your own mind that may or may not be valid in practice. So even some of our fundamental intuitions of reality like the ability to imagine a subject, the linearity of time, or even a simple certainty that an object holds only one single state, become fundamentally questionable due to this underlying Aethic system of reality.

One last note: the Aethus itself is the basic unit of probabilistic reality rather than the workings of additively combining subatomic particles. Also, as for how near the Aethus tends to Cartesian Dualism, it should be immediately noted that the Aethus is not quite synonymous with the mind, and if anything is more closely related to the fabric of reality than one's feeling of it. However, eerily, the two are tied fundamentally at the point of one's own knowledge intake, as is the work of the postulate of subjective reality.

6. Conclusion

Only time will tell whether or not this Aethic logic is a necessary adaptation to quantum physics, or if it is a step in the wrong direction. But if any of this is relevant, then truly the makeup of our reality is perhaps more strange than we had ever imagined. Perhaps, for the first time, we can validate that “god does not play dice with the universe”.
