You can trace, point by point, where the improvised wording of my 2023 manuscript already carried the exact structures I would only later state in proper academic terms. What makes that worth seeing isn't my ideas — it's that this can happen at all, and that it is surely happening to others right now. Click to read why.

The public vocabulary we use to describe our own thinking is not a neutral medium. It emerged from one dominant cognitive branch, and it behaves less like a common tongue than like a branch-specific dialect that every other branch must translate itself into. When a neurodivergent person reaches for that vocabulary to describe their internal cognition, the words rarely fit as calibrated: they end up assigning different semantics to the same words, or the same semantics to different words, because the allistic terms on offer were tuned to a different set of cognitive intuitions than the ones actually driving the thinking. That forces a quiet choice — point at the right topics, or use the descriptive words non-superficially — and the choice is invisible from both sides of the conversation. Terms get redefined by extrapolation, or worn as mere labels, and no one involved is aware of what was actually arrived at. I coined the term linguistic masking to give this a name.

There is a second layer, and it is visible throughout the annotations. When you hold a framework internally and know its downstream implications from experience, you write as though the reader can see them too — and they can't. When I described what I now call the extrusion principle, I wrote about consciousness “jumping realities.” From inside, I could see plainly that this implies an entire counterfactual and modal structure; but I had never heard of counterfactual semantics as a field, so I had no way to flag the implication for a reader, and the reader had no way to know it was there. The annotations try to surface exactly this: not just which principle each phrase maps to, but what it quietly implies from inside that is invisible from outside.

Watching this happen to my own work changed how I read everyone else's. I now ask what linguistic masking is hiding in the words of other thinkers on the margins. This page exists to make the phenomenon concrete: with my case laid out, you can trace, point by point, where an improvised arrangement of words corresponds one-to-one to structures I could only articulate years later, once I had the academic vocabulary to carry them.

I hope you find this story as eye-opening as I have — not because of my particular ideas, but because of what it shows is possible at all. The burning question is where else it is happening right now, and who else among the neurodivergent population is getting zero help extracting the substance from their own linguistic masking.

Solving the Measurement Problem: A Comprehensive Model Based on Three Postulates

Ajax Benander — May 2023

Verbatim restoration (2026). The body text below is the complete May 2023 manuscript, verbatim from the deposited PDF — including its typos (“bizzare,” “sightless,” “certainly” for “certainty”), all four original footnotes, and the references. The colored phrase-marks and the labeled purple panels are the 2026 annotation layer; nothing else on this sheet is. Abstract

The measurement problem is one of the most notorious and difficult open questions in all of physics. Why is it that quantum states seem to change their appearance just because of viewing or lack thereof? In the following paper, a possible solution will be explained using only three simple postulates which seek to generalize the concept of quantum superposition beyond just the subatomic world, and in the process remove any hints of questionable anthropocentric bias from the quantum theory. At its finest, this reasoning is potentially at once the missing piece to quantum theory that Einstein longed for [1], and the confirmation of correctness that Bohr was after [2].

1 Introduction to the Aethus

The fundamentals of Quantum Bayesianism [3], or QBism for short, will be assumed to be accurate for the sake of this paper’s argument. In fact, they will be incorporated into a fundamental postulate, the first of three to be proposed in these pages, which, if accurate, will serve as a complete and sufficient explanation for the measurement problem.

This first postulate, named the postulate of subjective reality, argues that since QBism proposes that quantum states are only in effect relative to the observer, then in fact, through a generalization, all states in the universe are only in existence relative to an observer. This may seem inherently solipsistic, but it is not at all under the right interpretation. Instead, it shares more similarities with the basic argument that the universe may only be seen from a perspective. When absent of a perspective, all of reality loses its meaning. This argument works in much the same way.

In order to avoid a detrimental anthropocentric bias, we will give a name to the arbiter of reality, perspective, or what have you, which is human-independent. Let this reality-perceiving entity be referred to as an “Aethus”. By virtue of the simple fact that you, the reader, are currently conscious and observing reality, you have an Aethus, which itself is directly representative and integrated with such things. An Aethus is a vantage point to the universe, but one which is strictly connected to it in both directions, such that reality loses meaning in the absence of an Aethus, and also an Aethus loses meaning in the absence of a corresponding reality to attach it to. In other words, reality comes in pairs – a reality coupled with its Aethus.

Postulate 1 (The Postulate of Subjective Reality) Anything may only be real relative to an Aethus.

A key next step is to declare what an Aethus actually stands for regarding a person’s experience of reality. This is that a person’s Aethus represents the collective presentation of all known truths that are currently accessible to them. That is, an Aethus is synonymous with a person’s knowledge. Specifically, however, it is the deepest degree of such things, such that even if a person interprets said truths incorrectly and believes to know something differing from the contents of their Aethus, and still the Aethus remains unaffected by such things, all the while remaining definite and truthful depending exactly on what is accessible for certain.

For these reasons, an Aethus is very much like a set, in that it may contain or disclude “elements”, so to speak. The difference is merely in the fact that an Aethus is somewhat more concrete, and is physically attached to an organic being so that it may perform organic tasks. For a human, the classic five senses, among other things, add to that human’s Aethus by virtue of teaching them this or that, and so, in effect, they gain a fundamental knowledge of the world around them. So, of course, other animals may have Aethae as well, as they are just as capable of collecting and storing mental information. Note that this does indeed open the doors to fictitious or collective Aethae. So long as a defining pattern is identifiable, an Aethus may be attached to it.

Note that an Aethus is constant and unchanging, which naturally means that the same creature or person in a different time possesses a different Aethus, since, afterall, the timecode is known to be different, which itself is separate information, hence implying a separate Aethus.

The Aethus now being officially defined as the representation of an entity’s truthful and accessible knowledge, the next step is to determine whether or not any given Aethus is possible to exist.

2 Aethic Superposition

This section is where the first major deviation from our classic expectations for reality will be made. And yes, these deviations apply to all levels of scale, meaning that the assumption that the “quantum world” is somehow inherently different from the “classical world” will be argued against. The following propositions may seem to be some form of bizzare and unnatural, but in effect they make quantum physics not only more tangible, but perhaps more easy and straightforward to understand, because now there may finally be personal context to its notions.

The first argument is that of quantum superposition. The idea now is that any single object or concept will be in superposition, that is relative to an Aethus, (by the postulate of subjective reality), strictly following wherever it cannot be explained by said Aethus. That is, any attribute of reality which is independent of an Aethus, which simply put, means it exists “outside” the scope of said Aethus, will occupy all possible states at once that it may occupy without influencing the Aethus. For example, if your back is turned long enough for a parking lot to be filled with cars, then assuming your friend has parked in the lot, their car occupies every single space at once relative to your Aethus at the time1. It is, in effect, in superposition. This of course sounds like an absurd proposition, as arguably humans are directly wired to object to such a concept, but it is what would be implied by the framework of the Aethus.

A natural objection that a human might make in the face of such a hypothesis, is that one may simply invalidate the claim that one’s friend has parked, at least partially, in all spaces at once, by checking, and sure enough, seeing the car parked at a single location by one sensory mechanism or another. But, naturally, the Aethus framework has an explanation: simply by checking that the car is in a specific location, you have birthed a new Aethus entirely from your own Aethus, by simply adding the car’s location to the mix. That is, you added the property of “car parking space index” to your Aethus, pulling a precisely random element from the superposition in the process, (as is the axiom). By definition, this new Aethus is a separate Aethus because its contents are not identical when comparing back and forth between them, however it may be argued that it is a kind of “child” Aethus of the old one, meaning it is identical except for added attributes. Even though the time code is different between the two Aethae, we may argue that this new one is still a child of the old one if we imagine attaching that old time code to itself as we might do with spatial displacement, (as in, at this time I held these coordinates. Now we say, at this time I held this time. This does mean that time is now two separate concepts — the coordinate time and the Aethic time, but this will be further elaborated on later). Note that this process is not as simple as simply ceasing the superposition of one concept with respect to one’s Aethus — rather it is more like compressing the superposition in one particular way by virtue of “saturating” the Aethus with some hint of further information — but information that is nonetheless never entirely binary and fully known. For example, perhaps you mistake a similar looking car for that of your friend’s, leading you to falsely believe your friend’s car being in that spot is within your Aethus. By simply believing your friend’s car is in that spot, your Aethus has nonetheless contracted the superposition of your friend’s car, (assuming you have good judgment), because the likelihood of a car thought to be that of your friend’s being that of your friend’s is higher than the likelihood of a randomly selected car from the lot being that of your friend’s, at least supposedly. This is all a way of saying that the superposition was still altered in some way, as now the Bayesian stakes are not identical, however still an attribute has not, nor ever will be, fully lifted out of superposition in one way or another.

Perhaps strangely, even though the parking location of your friend’s car is now known, and thus is, at least roughly, “out of superposition”, still you must remember that before you checked, you were unaware of that car’s location, so even now, with the car in plain sight, it is in superposition relative to your Aethus of five minutes ago, or anytime before you physically checked. That is simply what such an Aethus would imply, given the nature of superposition being directly correlated to that which is unknown and independent of knowledgeable accessibility. In effect, because your Aethus has changed, so too has reality for you, because as mentioned earlier, a reality is only ever rooted to an Aethus by this framework, and is not objective. Your consciousness jumped realities so to speak.

The simple rule of an Aethus regarding superposition is that any concept in superposition relative to an Aethus has no sway on the observation of said Aethus in the moment, or in other words, is statistically independent to it. This automatically allows us to mark any Aethus which does not follow this rule as invalid, or in other words, it will forever stay on paper, and will never exist in our physical universe. Furthermore, we may postulate the converse of this basic principle, arguing that if an item is in superposition with respect to an Aethus, then it must be statistically independent to it. Again, breaking this calls for the invalidity of an Aethus, so by taking the contrapositive of the conjunction of both rules, a second major postulate of the Aethus is attainable.

Postulate 2 (The Postulate of Superposition) If an Aethus is valid, then any given property will be in superposition relative to it if and only if it is statistically independent to it.

It may seem that such a concept is sudden and hard to trace to previous evidence, but truly it is just an extrapolation of the concepts already defined in Aethic superposition.

The last and final postulate, now, will complete the picture of Aethic reasoning, and enable the measurement problem’s solution.

3 Aethic Validity

One immediate response to the postulate of superposition might be to use it as a tool for identifying invalid Aethae. For instance, by simply taking the contrapositive of the statement, something of the following form is reached: If any given property of an Aethus is statistically independent to it but not in superposition relative to it, or in superposition relative to it but not statistically independent to it, then such an Aethus would be invalid. What this essentially argues for is two tools of determining the invalidity of an Aethus: either the Aethus contains a property which is also in superposition, or the Aethus doesn’t contain a property which is not in superposition. These are two different arguments, but both point to an invalid Aethus. In this section, a third argument to test the invalidity will be postulated, which note is logically additional to the postulates named so far.

Using the claim that an Aethus containing a property which is in superposition relative to it is invalid, we may immediately modify the law of noncontradiction in a physical sense. Specifically, so long as an item is not possessed by an Aethus, it may exist in all cooperating states at once, which, of course, implies contradiction between them at least some of the time. The key, then, is that contradiction is merely prohibited only in the case of properties within an Aethus, or perhaps within an Aethus and also in superposition. That is, for the car example, we cannot claim that the car is in parking space A relative to an Aethus and is in superposition between parking spaces A through Z relative to the same Aethus, because, for instance, that would mean it is also in space B, C, D, and so on, which all differ from the Aethus’s certainty of the car being in A.

Beyond here, we may start to build the final major postulate of Aethic logic, which may seem more complicated because it harnesses two generations of Aethae instead of one, but is nonetheless quite simple at its heart.

The general concept is that for any central Aethus, such as, for the sake of example, your own Aethus at this moment, it follows that, logically, it should be impossible for you to necessarily wind up in an invalid Aethus, as such an Aethus is invalid to begin with. We may think to perhaps define this as follows: “If an Aethus is valid, then no child Aethus of it may contradict any of its properties.” This is close to the final realization, but still not quite accurate. To see why, we might argue that in order to prevent future Aethic invalidity through a child Aethus of your own, the universe diverts you away from such an invalid child Aethus by adding a property to your Aethus to prevent it from existing. That is, if there is only one obscure property which triggers a contradictory child Aethus to your own, then simply because there are other options of child Aethae to avoid that signifies that all hope was not yet lost at the point of your own Aethus, hence proving that it is not invalid in the first place.

The solution to describing an Aethus which is necessarily doomed, so to speak, is sightless more specific. That is, it would have to be an Aethus for which no amount of property accumulation, no matter how clever, can prevent a potential contradiction. Such a concept may read as follows, if we are to take advantage of the family-style Aethus terminology. Consider this the event horizon of sorts of Aethus invalidity: “For some central Aethus, if for every one of its child Aethae there exists a further child Aethus that contradicts any of its properties, then said central Aethus is invalid.”

The last note to make here is that this only covers the hopeless situation of invalidity. However, if there are still options of child Aethae which necessarily prevent contradiction with your original Aethus, then it simply follows that one of these savior Aethae must in fact take the form of your own. If there are many as defined by many properties, then as we can say for certain is that they are all in superposition for taking the form of your own Aethus until a further child Aethus picks one over the other. (Also note that if the superposition described consists of one and only one property, then clearly said property will simply be added to one’s own Aethus accordingly.) Let us now finalize this postulate through presenting the contrapositive of this general statement. Here is this framework written out in the form of our final postulate for Aethic logic.

Postulate 3 (The Postulate of Aethic Certainty) If some Aethus is valid, then there exists a child Aethus to it for which every one of its own child Aethae strictly do not contradict any of the properties of said Aethus.

This general concept is logically sufficient to describe the remainder of this paper, however one interesting consequence of it may be quickly developed which implies that it has the potential to literally add to the Aethus itself. That is, given some central Aethus, if we are to remove all child Aethae of it which are invalid for their very attribute of being physically impossible, we are left with, oftentimes, a condensed set of potential child Aethae from any given Aethes compared to what may be generated by blindly adding properties to generate child Aethae. In effect, this puts the nature of all such condensed-possibility Aethae in superposition with the central Aethus itself. In the form of a statement this reads that, for some central Aethus, it follows that out of the set of of all child Aethae to it which hold no further child Aethae that contradict any of the properties of the central Aethus, the defining additional properties to each one of the child Aethae in said set beyond the central Aethus must be in a mutually exhaustive superposition of existence relative to the original Aethus. If this set is empty, meaning for all child Aethae of the central Aethus, there exists a further child Aethus to each one of them which contradicts a property of the central Aethus, then said central Aethus is invalid. (With this last statement just being a rephrasing of the contrapositive of the postulate of Aethic certainty).

One last note about the yields of this postulate regards the state of transitivity of invalid Aethae to child Aethae. If we assume that, for the sake of argument, some central Aethus in question is to be invalid as per the postulate of Aethic certainty, then it follows that for all child Aethae to that central Aethus, there exists a further child Aethus which is invalid. This naturally implies that for each one of these “first generational” child Aethae to the original central Aethus, any one of their own child Aethae are also direct child Aethae to the central Aethus, simply due to the act of wrapping up both generations of property additions into a single property, which is a fully allowed. Supposing that an Aethus may be written as its own child if we are to add a kind of empty property, then it follows that for each first generational set of child Aethae to the central Aethus, they contain one, (being themselves), or more child Aethae of their own from that original first generational set. And, because it is guaranteed that every child Aethus from that first generational set has a child Aethus which contradicts a property of the central Aethus, and also that, by the definition of child Aethae, every Aethus in that first generational set also contains this same property, then it follows that all Aethae in the first generational set have all child Aethae which themselves must have contradictory child Aethueses of their own to the original property. This itself satisfies the condition of the postulate of Aethic certainty for an invalid Aethus, and so, in conclusion, every invalid Aethus must have all invalid child Aethae. To continue with the family vocabulary of Aethae, if we suppose that a “parent” Aethus is the inverse of a child, so an Aethus identical to a reference Aethus except with one or more removed properties, then it follows that your Aethus is invalid if any one of your parent Aethae is invalid.

4 Solution to the Measurement Problem

We are now ready to, with the help of a real-world thought experiment, solve the measurement problem itself as a tool to demonstrate the use of these three postulates. That real world example will be the double-slit experiment, so without further ado, let us continue onward.

To begin with, let us run the experiment in thought-experiment form. A beam of electrons is fired at a twice-pierced plate, which itself is in front of another plate. The experimenter and observer, (whose Aethus we will analyze), begins firing the electron beam, and sure enough, the back plate is lit up with an interference pattern. If we analyze the Aethus of the experimenter, then we may note that such an Aethus is unaware of which slit each electron is passing through, and so, by the logic supplied by the first two postulates, we may argue that such a beam is in superposition relative to that experimenter’s Aethus. Therefore, each electron passes through both slits at once, and winds up creating the interference pattern by the logic of Feynman’s path integral, (or in other words, the electrons pass through both slits, so in effect they are introducing half and half of their superposition into each slit, resulting in them collectively winding up in some spatial points more often than others). The back plate itself, we may argue, is like a kind of wave function collapse of sorts, because of the mere fact that where the electron touches this plate will be accessible to the Aethus of the experimenter. Therefore, as postulated before, when the experimenter updates their Aethus with the location of each electron on the back wall, they will randomly select an item from the superposition, compressing it accordingly. In this case, this means that the electron will select a random ending trajectory on the back plate as determined by its preexisting superposition of location there relative to the Aethus of the experimenter directly before they observed the resulting location.

Then, of course, comes the classic headache – when we boot on the detector and begin logging the slit that each next electron passes through. This is precisely where the Aethic framework is needed, because of the scenario where the experimenter destroys the data before they look at it.

Let us imagine that the experimenter, all while not looking at the pattern on the back plate, records the slit that each electron travels through on a detector, however the detector is nonetheless never played back to the eyes of the experimenter. As has been shown innumerable times, when the pattern on the back plate is checked, it will be seen that the interference pattern has disappeared, replaced by two regions of high concentration right behind the slits, exactly as would have happened had one analyzed every last electron’s measurement from the detector instead of ignoring it. Why then, is an observation not even used counted by the universe as an observation nonetheless? What is the difference, truly, and a difference that is that forms the heart of the measurement problem. Funnily enough, with just the right manipulation, the solution becomes not only tangible, but intuitive due to Aethic reasoning.

The general idea here is to apply the postulate of Aethic certainty as a tool to observe what will happen. Let us suppose that you are the experimenter in our thought experiment, and you have run the detector during the double-slit experiment, which is now over. However, you have yet to check the pattern on the back plate, and are attempting to ponder, using Aethic reasoning as a tool, what you will observe. You start by subdividing the result into two potential Aethae: one in which there is an interference pattern, and another in which there is not. This corresponds to the notion, at least to an extremely high degree of likelihood, that the particle went through both slits at once, versus one slit but not the other in both ways, respectively speaking. For the sake of argument, you decide to begin by analyzing the validity of the Aethus where you see an interference pattern when you look. In such an Aethus, it follows that it contains many child Aethae in the universe, each one of which corresponds to a potential scenario for what you might do next, for example. However, to every one of those child Aethae, there is a potential for you to check the data on the detector, regardless of whether you actually do or not. That is, just due to the nature of quantum uncertainty, if a detector was not running, then it would be forever impossible to work out which slit the particles went through, but because it was running in this scenario, it is continuously possible to work out which single slit the particle went through. Therefore, according to the postulate of Aethic certainty, this hypothetical Aethus must be invalid, in effect adding to the Aethus that it cannot exist, or in other words, removing it from the superposition, (as per the postulate of superposition)2. All that is now left, then, is for the particle to either go through one slit and not the other in either way. So long as the detector has not yet been checked, it follows that, relative to your Aethus, the particle went through one slit but not the other in both ways, as is the superposition. Your Aethus, then, passes the test of the postulate of Aethic certainty, because there exists child Aethae which have no further contradictory child Aethae of their own. Such child Aethae are the scenario where the particle went through, say, the “first” slit and not the second, and alternatively, through the second and not the first. If, for instance, it went through the first and not the second, then it follows that your own current Aethus is unaware of which specific slit it went through anyway, just knowing that it did not go through (at least) one of them. Hence, for either case of the particle going through the first slit or the second slit, as observed on the detector, no contradiction is raised, so either Aethus holds all non contradictory child Aethae, which disallows the postulate of Aethic certainty to make your Aethus invalid in this case. The only remaining possibility for invalidity, now, is through the postulate of superposition. But sure enough, it does not point to invalidity, because it is known that the particle may not go through both slits at once due to the previous work by the postulate of Aethic certainty on such a scenario, so the Aethus knows this – all that is left, then, is for the particle to go through the first slit and not the second slit as well as the second slit and not the first slit simultaneously in superposition. It may seem counterintuitive how this is different from the general going through both slits at once, but to Aethic logic it is certainly distinct – for instance, it does imply that one slit is not being passed through no matter what. Altogether the postulate of superposition processes this by triggering a superposition of the remaining unknowns, which themselves are the slit of traversal of the particle given that at least one slit was not traversed. Therefore, for such a child Aethus, no contradictions are possible through either postulate, and so the state of the particle going through one slit and not the other in both ways is valid, thus implying that this will be your observed result.

If, on the other hand, instead you are to keep the detector off during the running of the experiment, and then predict what pattern you shall see upon looking at the back plate, there are again several possibilities: either it goes through both slits at once, the first slit but not the second, the second but not the first, or neither. Because of the uncertainty principle, it follows that because no measurement was made of the particle’s slit of traversal, then that information is permanently lost, and will forever remain outside any child Aethus of your own. Therefore, due to this, every one of your child Aethae cannot contradict the slit index property of the particle within your own Aethus, and so the postulate of Aethic certainly has decidedly not proven your Aethus in that case to be invalid. All that is left, then, is to apply the contrapositive of the postulate of superposition as a final test to check the validity of your Aethus. If it also fails to prove your Aethus is invalid, then it must be valid, as it is physically impossible to prove it invalid. The only way, now, for your Aethus to be invalid, as according to this postulate, is for the particle slit to be known by your Aethus but also be in superposition, or be unknown by your Aethus and not be in superposition. Because we are fully aware that the particle’s slit is forever unknown in this case, it follows that, in order for your Aethus to be valid, it must be so that it is also in superposition. Therefore, the slit through which the particle passed is both slits at once, and hence the interference pattern is the direct result.

In effect, through the logic of the Aethus, the measurement problem is solved.

5 Applications

The measurement problem is solved by the three postulates of Aethic reasoning, however of course this does not prove that they themselves are correct.

Let us now demonstrate the power of Aethic reasoning by applying it to certain mysteries of quantum physics, starting with the noteworthy application of Aethic reasoning is its implications for quantum entanglement. Suppose that there are two people, Alice and Bob, who each possess an entangled particle held at opposite sides of the universe. If Alice checks her particle, then relative to her Aethus, Bob’s particle must possess the opposite state from wherever he is out in space. However, noteworthily for the practical application of this, Bob’s particle, at least relative to his Aethus, will not collapse its superposition when Alice checks her particle, and likewise if Bob checks his particle, then that will not collapse the superposition of Alice’s particle then with respect to her Aethus. Moreover, even after Alice has checked, relative to her Aethus from before she checked, both particles are still in an entangled superposition, and likewise, even after Bob has checked, relative to his Aethus from before, both particles are and always will be in an entangled state of superposition. And when checking the reverse case, we may also argue, likewise through a simple application of the postulate of subjective reality coupled with the postulate of superposition, that relative to Alice’s current Aethus after the check, the particle was always in the state she now observes, as is the case with Bob’s particle relative his post-observational Aethus. Therefore, we are to get around the problem of hidden variables vs. non-local-realness [4] by simply stating that reality is what changed with the observation rather than any inherent property itself in a consistent reality.

A simpler version of this thought experiment is the notorious Schrödinger’s cat itself, in which case a cat entangled to a particle is either alive or dead with respect to the Aethus of a human experimenter. In such a case, the experimenter, prior to observing the state of the cat, will not contain the wellbeing of the cat in their Aethus, and so therefore by the postulate of superposition such a thing will be in just that: superposition. This, hence, explains why the cat is in superposition. Moreover, once the experimenter checks the status of the cat, their Aethus will add the state from a random spot in the superposition, thus causing the outcome to update accordingly by whatever relevant probabilities are present. As stated many times before, relative to their prior Aethus the cat is still and always will be in superposition, and so whatever state they uncover with their new Aethus will merely be the state of the cat relative to this present Aethus, and will accordingly be so for the future and immediate past as well. If the cat is dead, then if the timecode of its death is ambiguous, perhaps such a thing may also be in superposition until determined, but for the meantime will be in superposition relative to the current Aethus.

One key concept to discuss beyond this is the Aethic implication for the nature of time. As was touched upon earlier, it is a requirement for the hypothesis of later versions of oneself holding child Aethae of previous versions that the time as a dimension be separated from the time perceived by one’s Aethus. There are some interesting consequences to this notion, one of which is a solution to time asymmetry by the second law of thermodynamics, through stating that time symmetry was never broken in the first place. Specifically for this, the Aethic logic argues that the second law of thermodynamics is a property of the Aethus rather than of time. That is, when hypothesizing that each time one adds to their Aethus, such an added property is selected perfectly at random from the corresponding superposition, the second law of thermodynamics is easily derived from such a claim, just by arguing that heightening entropy is so much more likely than lowering entropy. A strange consequence of this, however, is that if somehow, hypothetically speaking, it was possible for time to reverse in an instant, then the universe would not merely play backwards, but entropy instead would continue to increase. That is, by the postulate of subjective reality, the very act of reversing the flow of time in the universe would only ever be a property of some Aethus, and so, relative to that Aethus, hypothetically the fundamental hypothesis that each new child Aethus randomly compresses the superposition would result in entropy continuing to increase relative to that Aethus. Abstract

The question of the measurement problem in quantum mechanics has been an ongoing inquiry among physicists and philosophers for the last century or so, and has as of yet remained unsolved. In this paper, a potential solution, leaning on simple and fundamental laws of logic in our universe, will be formulated and explained. The main idea is that superposition itself is a broader concept than we have always thought, and is in fact rooted to the perceiver of their own reality more so than the cosmos itself, allowing for the same time-frozen object/concept to be in separate forms of superposition depending on the temporal iteration of the mind that is viewing it, among other things. Along the way, the concepts of knowledge, perception, and contradiction will be formulated and generalized, allowing for our basic outlook of the universe to be fundamentally charted. In the end, concepts like Schrödinger’s cat and the double-slit experiment will be explained to sufficient detail regarding their existing nature.

1 Introduction

The double-slit experiment is an intriguing test which has captured the awe of physicists for over a century. When a beam of electrons is fired at a twice pierced plate, they seem to fall upon a back wall as if they had gone through both slits at once, rather than only one or the other as we might classically expect. But, perhaps eerily, when the vagueness of the unknown is challenged, and an observer of any form watches the electrons siphon through the slits, and sure enough only one slit is chosen again, causing the pattern on the back wall to change accordingly. It may seem quite frustrating and unexplainable why this happens, but in the following pages, a pitch will be supplied for exactly why this happens, due simply to the basic power of logical reality.

2 Reality

Let us begin with an observation on the more metaphysical side of things: every comprehension we make about reality – that is every single perception – must pass through the wiring of the human brain in order for you to become aware of it. This may seem trivial at first, but if you think about it, it serves to be an extremely overbearing influence. We cannot see otherwise, and we never will, (quite by definition), and so this strict rigidity to our outlook on reality is sure to have its consequences, whatever they may be. Imagine if for every experiment you ever run, you may only observe the results through purple-colored glasses. Surely some objectivity will be lost, if there even is such a thing. There are concrete ramifications for this when regarding the human brain, such as our inability to detect neutrinos, but such concrete ramifications usually have workarounds given sufficient creativity. It is truly, then, the abstract ramifications where the real fun happens.

For the sake of this paper, I have created a simple framework to illuminate the abstract concept of knowledge. As far as I can tell, knowledge as a concept is a subclass of this logic, and not the other way around. Therefore, it must also apply to whatever it is the human brain does when generating consciousness out of observation. Imagine for a moment that a set of knowledge could be quantified. Let this set of knowledge be called an Ethus. On the contrary to the sets and lists we find in computer science, an Ethus is far more organic, and yet equal in its simplicity. An Ethus is like a canteen which holds knowledge like water, and in the end it represents the collective presentation of all such knowledge. This knowledge cannot be considered to be built of bits, because such a system would fail to capture its essence1. The Ethus contains an infinite stream of knowledge, and also a finite stream, depending simply on how one processes it. One interpretation is to consider every piece of knowledge as being part of a single, complicated property, and such a property would then be representative of the Ethus itself. We may try to describe the Ethus using language, a feat which is sometimes doable – but sometimes it is also less doable due to the abstract nature of things. You couldn’t exactly describe the entire content of your memory in a sentence of two, and the same is often the case of sufficiently complex Ethuses.

If we imagine one’s own mind as being a kind of Ethus, then interesting results begin to unfold. For one, the act of observing the cosmos is the act of adding to one’s Ethus. In fact, our entire understanding of reality must pass at some point through our Ethus in order to be comprehended by us: and in fact it is quite possible that the two are one in the same all along. This, essentially, leads into the fundamental claim of this paper, for which the known effects of quantum physics would be a subclass: reality itself can only exist relative to an Ethus. Without an Ethus to link it to, reality is meaningless, to the point where we should not consider them separate in the first place. That is, to every Ethus, there is a reality, and to every reality, there is an Ethus – they are manifestations of each other. But trying to consider a reality without an Ethus defeats the entire point, and for that matter is physically impossible. This is an extremely counterintuitive concept to a human, so I will supply more information. Reality comes in pairs, so to speak – an observation and an Ethus working together. Crucially, reality is not a standalone thing – it must be rooted to an Ethus to have any meaning whatsoever.

3 Superposition

The next part of the story is equally interesting. Essentially, if we picture the universe as being the way it is relative to your mind’s Ethus, then what happens for those inevitable properties which cannot be explained by such an Ethus? If you think about it, we can list a whole sum of properties of reality which cannot be explained one way or another using the Ethus alone, and these properties, all together, resemble an entirely new Ethus just by themselves due to their clumping nature. If we combine all of these differing properties together into a single whole, and stick it in an Ethus, then we create an Anti-Ethus, which is simply the partner of the Ethus. That is, for every Ethus, there exists a corresponding Anti-Ethus, which contains all the properties in reality which would have zero net effect on the Ethus if they were altered. In other words, the Anti-Ethus is statistically independent to the Ethus. As an example, if the Ethus held a property that x = 1, then assuming y is independent of x, the Anti-Ethus might hold a property that y = k, where k is a number. This property of independence still holds as the Ethus becomes more convoluted, but will of course become more convoluted itself as this happens. All this being said, for any property of reality which is partially correlated to the Ethus, but not fully, it may simply be subdivided into an Ethus component and an Anti-Ethus component which are placed into the corresponding compartments.

The key idea of the Anti-Ethus, then, is that everything within it will be in superposition when regarding reality. So, in the example of where x = 1 and y = k, it is so that literally y = k, in that it exists in all possible states at once. This does not, however, imply uniform distribution, because we may very well add to the Ethus that P(y < 0) > 70%, for example, which would still be perfectly valid. In that case, the physical value of y is still uncertain when using the Ethus as a guide, but enough is unknown to create whatever skewed distribution would follow given that property. In a way, the Anti-Ethus is still uniform, but only when fitting with the terrain of the Ethus. That is, the superposition of the Anti-Ethus will never arbitrarily skew one way or another without the direct order of the Ethus to do so. If the Ethus is a container, then the Anti-Ethus fits that container like a liquid.

4 Contradiction

And that leaves one final important rule of reality: no contradictions are permitted. Even if a contradiction occurs somewhere in the infinite superposition of an Anti-Ethus upon reality, then something is wrong, and so reality will take the simplest possible path to avoid that, adding to the Ethus accordingly. Note that a contradiction may be defined as an Attribute in the Ethus holding two possible values instead of just one – which unlike the Anti-Ethus, is strictly forbidden. This includes a scenario where a property in the Anti-Ethus conflicts with its corresponding occurrence in the Ethus. Either way, the Ethus in question is invalid2. If we picture a child Ethus as meaning an Ethus with added information from another Ethus, then a parent Ethus would be the product of removing information from an Ethus. Contradictions cannot trigger on the usual plane of reality, where an Ethus is center stage, but technically such a concept is only a special case of a more general concept: if an Ethus with removed information from another causes a contradiction, then both Ethuses are invalid. Another way of putting it is that every child of an invalid Ethus is also invalid3.

5 Assembly

Putting all this together allows for three fundamental rules of reality.

The Three Rules (January form) 1. Anything may only be real relative to an Ethus. 2. Any attribute of an Anti-Ethus will be in superposition relative to its Ethus. 3. An Ethus is invalid if a contradiction is triggered anywhere in the superposition of its parent Ethuses.

These three basic laws of Ethus manifestation are sufficient to describe all superposition related queries, and furthermore make some intriguing implied claims. For one, there is no objective reality of the universe, as if to say that a particle in superposition is such relative to the universe. Instead of this, any superposition only relates to a corresponding Ethus, which, crucially, may very well differ between people. If an experimenter is unaware of the state of Schrödinger’s cat, then relative to their Ethus, the cat is in superposition of being alive and dead, because such a state sits in the Anti-Ethus of the experimenter’s mind. However, even if such an experimenter looks in the box and becomes aware of the answer, the cat will still be in superposition relative to an unknowing bystander, and as a matter of fact, so too will the experimenter, because that bystander does not know how they reacted. However, the second the experimenter tells the bystander how the experiment went, and they will also add the information to their Ethus – however not entirely. Technically there is a chance the experimenter is lying, and for that matter, there is a chance the cat quantum fluctuated to an alternate state in the time it took for the bystander to become informed. Therefore, it is physically impossible to perfectly add a state to the Ethus at some certainty one way or another. A superposition may never truly collapse – rather it can only compress4. All of this being said, the precise breakdown of Schrödinger’s cat according to the Ethus logic is that A) The cat was only in superposition5 relative to the experimenter’s unknowing Ethus before they checked the box, (and crucially, the cat remained in superposition forever beyond then relative to such a past Ethus), and that B) The cat was in the same state the experimenter observed the entire time, relative to their Ethus at the temporal moment of and following the observation. This logic allows us to generalize superposition and also perhaps make it more compatible with Einsteinian thought in the process, because information perhaps cannot travel between two realities at all, not to mention faster than light. The possibilities here are intriguing, but for the remainder of this paper we will continue to hug the propositions of these three rules alone, and see where it leads.

6 Thought Experiments

Take, for another example, the double-slit experiment itself. Let us imagine running a dual thought experiment here: one where the particles are electrons in a laboratory, and another where they are humans on a soccer field. Set up on this field is every needed analogue to the normal experiment, except instead of two slits on a plate, there are two doors on a long constructed wall, which itself is in front of another wall with numbered labels from one to a hundred along it. In front of the two doors, concealed from the back wall, are one hundred participants eager to mimic electrons, and standing blindfolded on the bleachers is our classic quantum observer. Let the games begin!

Every participant in the soccer field holds a smartphone with two numbers texted to them: the first is their door to travel through – either one or two – and the second is the spot on the back wall where they are to go stand. Both numbers are calculated precisely randomly given the setting, just as would be the case for the normal double-slit experiment, which means that if the people did indeed go through both doors at once, they would also appear in an identical wavelike pattern. Each person awaits their text, and then upon receiving, heads through the corresponding door(s) and stands at the back wall. This goes on for twenty minutes, and soon the experiment has been run. Now all that is left is for the blindfolded quantum observer to see the results. They walk, still blindfolded, to the back wall, and then on the count of three, remove the blindfold to see... two major regions of people on the back wall, which does not signify superposition at all. So, what went wrong?

The red herring here is to assume the people never were in superposition. Relative to the observer right now who looks at the wall, no, the people are not in superposition, however relative to the observer ten minutes ago when they were blindfolded over by the bleachers, the people were indeed in superposition. Technically those are two separate Ethuses, and so they may very potentially correspond to separate realities6.

When the observer was blindfolded ten minutes ago, and the experiment was still running, each new participant who walked through a door did so without the knowing of the observer, and so as far as their Ethus could tell, they were walking through both doors at once. Only when the observer bothered to look at the results did the superposition compress into the result. The observer at that moment could not see the numbers on the participants’ screens, so still the actual door index of each person is unknown, but nonetheless the fact that no single person traveled through two doors is certain to them now that they see the pattern of where they stand. The reason for this is quite simple: if the observer’s Ethus was aware that each person went through both doors, due perhaps to a wavelike pattern on the back wall, then if they were to ask each person which door they went through, surely gathering one door for each, the results would be contradictory. The observer may not currently be speaking to another person to gather information, but if we create a parent Ethus where this known is removed, contradiction will instantly trigger somewhere in the superposition if there is a wavelike pattern of people on the back wall. Thus, rule number three would be broken if such an Ethus was permitted, and so, blatantly speaking, it is not. Therefore the observer physically cannot observe a wavelike pattern of people on the back wall, (without some kind of weird fluke obviously).

But on the microscopic scale, of course, is where everything gets weird. One may be inclined to call the quantum world an entirely separate system abiding by entirely separate rules, but by the logic of this paper, that would be overthinking it. Truly, then, the same exact principles are working down there as they are up here, but with one red herring-typed catch: Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle. The idea is actually stupidly simple when you look at it closely – so long as the electrons were not observed when going through the slits, then by the uncertainty principle, we may determine that that information is permanently lost. Therefore, we may remove attributes at will from our Ethus as the observer, and never once will a contradiction occur. Therefore, calling the electrons in a superposition long after the experiment is over is totally valid, and so that they will be just that. If, however, we observe the electrons going through the slits, then when we observe the pattern later on, a wavelike result would break rule number three. We obviously cannot break rule number three, and so such an Ethus cannot take the form of one’s mind. Instead, we will have to observe the particles in the form of the two groups, just as we did with the people. As for the exact reason why such a thing would break rule number three, it is because if we imagine a parent Ethus to the observer’s where the knowledge of whether or not they checked the detector is removed, then in certain regions of the superposition of that they have seen the electrons go through one slit or the other7 in addition to seeing the wavelike pattern on the wall8. Of course this is a contradiction, and so as long as the detector was active during the experiment, the particles must form two groups on the back wall. The idea, though, is that all one has to do is feed Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle into this Ethus logic, and the precise conclusions of the double-slit experiment will be generated. And the added bonus is that we could also just not, specifically when regarding the macro scale, and then those results will follow as well.

7 Human Experience

Another quick topic of interest might be how this all relates to the human experience. You would be crazy, as a human, not to consider everything you have read some form of absurd. But then again, it probably is not absurd in actuality. We are specifically programmed by paleomammalian environmental evolution to think it is anyway, though, because if we were inherently open to this Ethus-oriented perception of the world, we would have been crushed by the cruel grasp of mental evolution ages ago. So in a way every one of us is living a lie every time we assume that reality is A) objective, and B) not consistently riddled with superpositions. I am not recommending that you saturate your social life with these new truths, because frankly that will probably lead any one of us to madness, but still it’s not all bad. This Ethus logic is in no way shape or form in favor of solipsism, for a few key reasons. For one, every person you interact with has their own Ethus, every one of which is equal in its dignity to your own. The thing is, you are simply seeing a reality relative to your Ethus, however all of them are seeing a reality relative to their Ethuses as well. Interestingly, the various superpositions in these realities will never quite line up, but both are equally valid with respect to each Ethus – you just happen to be in yours and they happen to be in theirs. It is like the multiverse all played out right in our own world. And no, this is not anthropocentrically based. Any old knowledge collecting entity in the universe may have an Ethus if you suppose, and for that matter even abstract things like thought experiments may. It’s just that every time you’re not aware9 of something, then when wiring to your own Ethus, that thing is in superposition. But relative to any example of what that thing is, you yourself may be in superposition. And remember that key time component as well, which states that the same mind at different times is a different Ethus. At the moment of your roommate going to work while you stay home, they may be in superposition regarding certain attributes, but once they come home and tell you about their day, now relative to your new Ethus, they were not in superposition during that workday.

Also regarding the human experience is our imaginations. Our capacity to imagine is designed by evolution to fit with our funny mammalian outlook on the universe where everything is objective. Back in the soccer field experiment, when the blindfolded observer is sitting patiently on the bleachers during the experiment, they may try to imagine what is going on over by the doors. They may imagine a person going through each door, and they imagine the people settling over at the walls. Perhaps they ask the question, “What’s happening over there right now? What does the pattern look like?” But, now, with this Ethus logic, the answer is strange – essentially there is no pattern, because even imagining one is adding more knowledge to their Ethus. In a sense, the person has generated an entire pretend Ethus to try and grasp what is going on, and a pretend Ethus which has no guaranteed logical validity at that. In actuality, the events of the doors and the back wall at the time, while in superposition, cannot be observed in the first place while the observer is in their current blindfolded state. So the answer to what it looks like is that it looks like nothing, because such a human-bias-oriented question defeats the purpose. Even more technically speaking, the act of “looking” is the act of bouncing photons off an object and catching them in one’s eyes. But, well, such a concept is immaterial and irrelevant when regarding a superposition. This does not mean that the people over there are mindless ghosts or something, but rather they are doing every possible thing they could be doing at once, all depending on the various likelihoods predefined in the observer’s Ethus.

8 Conclusion

So, the moral of the story is that all of this Ethus logic is just quite strange, and that we ourselves are strangely resistant to it by nature. We may one day know the great truths of physics and metaphysics, but in the meantime, all we can do is keep asking questions, and crucially, keep clearing away the clouds of bias which have locked up our perception for millennia. Maybe, then, we will finally see the sun which illuminates our every passing second in this extraordinary universe.
