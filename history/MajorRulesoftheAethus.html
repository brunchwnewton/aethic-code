• The double-slit experiment’s strangeness and the measurement problem at large are perfectly derivable using intuitive logic given the right framework. Let’s test that claim!
  • The deterministic causal chain is fallacious — because instead the information about the universe is incomplete — and way too incomplete to allow for classic causality.
  • A human/observer can occupy multiple timelines at once, and in fact this is the natural state of reality rather than the alternative.
  • Specifically, you inhabit the set of all timelines at once which comply with your knowns about reality. For instance, if you are unaware of something, then you are by definition composed across every timeline of every alternate state of that thing. (For instance, if you are unaware of your great-great grandfather’s name, then you are physically on all timelines of all different names he has that don’t raise a contradiction to your knowns). (ALSO, if a tree falls in the forest, and no one is around to hear it, does it make a sound? More like, if no one is around to hear it, then it fell and didn’t fall at once).
  • So, naturally, everything in the universe is an analogue of Schrödinger’ Cat, and its quantum superposition becomes an attribute of your knowns that everything shares rather than an unwieldy causal connection to the quantum realm.
  • As such, the line between the quantum and the classical realms blurs entirely. (Yes, I know this is insane, but it solves the measurement problem hypothetically, so maybe we give it the time of day???)
  • Note how reality is empirically indistinguishable in this system versus the deterministic one we typically favor — that is until quantum effects enter the picture, which between the two seem to favor the new version, (as we’ll see with further rules).
  • The same human at two different times are two different quantum observers, (because they have different sets of knowns). (To overcome the bias of continued mind).
  • Reality then becomes entirely relative to the knowns of the observer. At this point I create a terminology for this entity of fundamental knowns: an Aethus. Too complicated to go over its rigorous definition here, but it’s sort of like Type Theory more than Set Theory. Also very recursively oriented. I just needed a mathematical object that lines up with conscious information gathering, and I think this is it.
  • Anything may only be real relative to an Aethus. Your friend and your dog have different Aethae than you, as does yourself from yesterday at 2 pm. Therefore, attached to each of them, is an entirely distinct reality which exists purely in relation to their respective Aethae. This is my 1st major postulate.
  • The concept of timelines themselves now go out the window for lack of compatibility with the Aethus. Instead of, “you are on a timeline”, or even my update of “you are on multiple timelines”, the mindset becomes more, “you are in an Aethus on the Aethic Tree, and reality adjusts in relation to your node on that tree.” That is, the concept of weighing time lines as separate causal chains is somewhat misleading, so really the best idea is to consider the weights of attributes and states themselves, which just so happen to be Aethically deviated. Also, yes, you have the gift of being able to traverse this tree, which is how you gain information and update reality, hence collapsing, (or perhaps compressing), the wave function.
  • But the wave function and the particles don’t actually change — your reality/Aethus does.
  • Time becomes two concepts: dimensional time and Aethic time. That way we have a referential coordinate for each point in spacetime and how it changes under the relation to the Aethus.
  • To put the concept of multiple timelines at once more formally now, since timelines become somewhat irrelevant: we might write that if an Aethus is valid, (aka capable of working with these rules), then any given property will be in superposition relative to it if and only if it is statistically independent to it. Statistical independence is just a more rigorous way of defining what exists “outside” the Aethus. This is my 2nd major postulate.
  • A clear distinction is now needed between different types of superpositions. At this point language starts to break down for describing quantum states accurately, since, well, nobody’s visited this before it seems. You can still visualize things here, but you need to lean into permutation reasoning, and you’ll note here that alternate states might hold the same linguistic statement.
  • Child Aethus means add knowns, parent means subtract them.
  • We declare a generalization of the concept of contradictions to the Aethus mindset: consider any Aethus, A. If for all children of it, each one contains their own child which contradicts at least one known of A, then A is deemed an “invalid Aethus”.
  • By the contrapositive, for any valid Aethus, A, it contains a child B for which all of its further children cannot contradict the knowns of A. This is my 3rd major postulate.
  • This rule immediately invalidates an Aethus for which somebody performs the double-slit experiment, leaves the detector on, and gets an interference pattern. This is because for every child Aethus of the experimenter thereafter, it is possible to contradict the state of the particles going through two slits with the realization from the detector that they went through one only.
  • Conversely, it becomes impossible to invalidate the interference pattern in an Aethus where the detector is not running. Thus this becomes the only valid state. That’s another major rule: when it’s impossible to invalidate an Aethus, it must thus be valid.
  • So the double-slit experiment’s outcome becomes the collection of four different Aethae, each representing an output. This is where permutations come in. 1) Neither are gone through, 2) Slit A but not slit B gone through, 3) Slit B bot not slit A gone through, 4) Both slits gone through at once.
  • Anyways, with the detector on, we invalidate case 4 only, leaving the Aethus in superposition between 1 & 2 & 3, that is until the spot of the particle on the back screen is checked, thus narrowing the superposition, (for instance closer to 2 than 3), or a detector is checked, essentially collapsing it altogether relative to the experimenter’s new Aethus.
  • With the detector off, it’s a bit more subtle. First of all, the result is that we invalidate 2 & 3, leaving the Aethus in superposition between 1 & 4. If you refer to the earlier rule, where you’re on all timelines at once which satisfy your knowns, or put more Aethically, all states which are statistically independent to your Aethus are in superposition relative to it, we might suppose that the slit of traversal is now certainly statistically independent to the observer’s Aethus, because they are of course unaware of it and cannot access the data by any means, (as a consequence of the uncertainty principle). As such, that must be in superposition by that rule, so the fact that it is not in Aethae 2 or 3 immediately raises a contradiction in themselves. By a corollary of the third major postulate, any child of an Aethus which contradicts itself also contradicts it, so neither can be valid. Note the difference from detector-on, where we forcefully suggest that 2 and 3 are not statistically independent to the Aethus, given that we have already crossed off case 4. Here we cannot do such a thing.
  • So, yes, when the detector is on during the double-slit experiment, what happens is that we go from superposition between the particle going through both slits at once, to one but not the other in both ways. This specifically is the statement for which language breaks down, because both at once is technically accurate for both versions, but the subtlety is important here. Refer to permutations!
  • If this is right, then now that should encourage us to go on the hunt for more uncertainty principles, which, you’ll notice, will each have their own ripple effects on reality due to the third major postulate. If it can be formulated, then it’s something you can plug into the Aethus postulates.
