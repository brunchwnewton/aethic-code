How this works: each dot is an animal with a body mass and a crowd-sourced combat score, the same Bradley–Terry baseline the recalibration page uses (about 4,494 matchups, Lion = 100). Theropod scores are multiplied by that page's anatomy factor: a lognormal model with median 4.19× and ×/÷1.285 per log-space standard deviation, so the 2σ band runs from 2.54× to 6.92×. Mammals carry the baseline unchanged. The buttons select the calibration; the shaded band shows the ±2σ range around the theropod curve.

The fits: a power law per group, fitted here in the browser by median regression (least absolute deviations in log–log space), which is robust to outlier votes. Mammals below 1 kg are excluded from the fit, since the matchup graph has little cross-scale calibration there; the curves are drawn dashed where they extrapolate below the fitted data. Because the anatomy factor multiplies every theropod score by the same number, it shifts the theropod curve without changing its exponent.

What the gap means: the vertical distance between the curves is the anatomy advantage at equal mass. It is a sensitivity model placed on the crowd baseline, not something the votes identify, which is why the page shows the band rather than a single curve. The headline figures above are computed from the fits at the selected calibration; the ancestor marker sits at 100 g, below the mammal data, on the extrapolated part of the curves.
