# Galaxy perspectival profiles: working draft

*23 November 2026. Exploratory working file, not part of the versioned archive. Every number below is a fit, on SPARC's 175 galaxies (Lelli, McGaugh and Schombert 2016), and should be read as provisional.*

## 1. The model in brief

Each galaxy is described in two steps.

1. **On its own leaf** the galaxy is the fundamental exponential source: stars with a true scale length `h`, gas with its scale length scaled alike, and the bulge as a point. The leaf has radius `ell`, the readout is gnomonic (`r = ell tan(chi)`), and the leaf's own gravity gives the orbital speed `v_leaf(r)`. There is no dark matter and no separate mass factor.
2. **A rescaling of the visible metric**, one function per galaxy, carries a point at pre-rescaling radius `r` to the observed radius `R`. The stretch `s(R) = R/r` multiplies the orbital speed, `v_obs(R) = s(R) v_leaf(r)`, and the same map carries the light, `Sigma_obs(R) = Sigma(r) (r/R)^2 (d ln r / d ln R)`. The rescaled image of the stars must still match the galaxy's measured 3.6 micron light about as well as a plain exponential disk does.

The rescaling depends on radius only: it is the same in every direction around the galaxy's center. The data used here are azimuthally averaged, so they cannot test an angle-dependent version.

## 2. What a profile contains

| Entry | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ell` | radius of the galaxy's leaf (kpc) |
| `h` | true scale length of the stars on the leaf (kpc), before rescaling |
| `Y_d`, `Y_b` | stellar mass-to-light ratios of disk and bulge at 3.6 micron (priors 0.5 and 0.7, 0.1 dex) |
| `a0`, `q1`, `q2` | the rescaling function: `ln r = ln R1 + a0 + q1 x (inner half) + q2 x (outer half)`, where the two halves split the measured range from `R1` to `RN` evenly in `ln R` |
| stretch in / mid / out | `R/r` at the first, middle and last radius, computed from the three numbers above |

So a profile has five fitted shape numbers (`ell`, `h`, `a0`, `q1`, `q2`) plus the mass-to-light ratios. The square of the stretch plays the part that the free mass factor played in earlier fits.

With three rescaling numbers the leaf's chi-square is 8,253 against NFW's 9,624, and its charged score 11,604 against 12,040. The control has the rescaling but no leaf; its total is dominated by one galaxy, UGC 2953, where it fails (chi-square near 10,000). Without that galaxy, at three numbers, the leaf has 7,406, the control 9,231 and NFW 9,010.

Cautions:

- The margin is modest and uneven. A handful of galaxies dominate in both directions (UGC 11914 and UGC 6787 against the leaf; IC 2574 and IC 4202 for it).
- The result depends on how tightly the image is held to the light. Ignoring the light, any rotation curve can be fitted exactly, with or without a leaf. Making the light dictate the map point by point, the fit fails badly. The tolerance used here sits between.
- Most of the fit is carried by the rescaling, not the leaf. After charging the leaf's extra number, the leaf is better than the control in 65 of 175 galaxies; in total it is about a fifth lower in chi-square once UGC 2953 is set aside.
- NFW was not given a rescaling of its own.
- Eleven galaxies have fewer than five measured light points; their images were held to an exponential of the catalogued scale length instead (marked † in the table).
- The fits use a simplex search from several starts; individual entries may not be global optima.

Reading:

- **The overall stretch follows what the galaxy is, not how we see it.** It is larger in low-mass, gas-rich, late-type, faint galaxies, and shows no link to distance or inclination. This is the same trend the free mass factor showed before.
- **The outer-over-inner stretch follows nothing here.** No measured quantity predicts it. That is what a deeper, galaxy-by-galaxy rule would look like from this side, and also what noise would look like; this file cannot tell them apart.
- **Data quality leaves marks** on the leaf radius and scale length (the velocity-error and extent columns), so those entries are partly set by how good each curve is.
