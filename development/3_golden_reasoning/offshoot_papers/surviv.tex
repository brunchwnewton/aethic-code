\begin{abstract}
We study the survival theory of a hazard rate whose noise is placed one integration further out than is standard: rather than diffusing $h$ or $\log h$, we diffuse the negative logarithmic derivative $\mu=-\frac{d}{dt}\log h$, so that the integrated driver is a Kolmogorov diffusion and the governing persistence exponent moves from $\tfrac12$ to $\tfrac14$. The hazard carries two channels, both read at the technostrength $x^+$, the positive part of the accumulated exponent: an external background muffled by the factor $\operatorname{sech}^2x^+$, and an internal channel that is a sum of units $\Gamma_j\sinh^2(x^+/\tau_j)$, each carrying a technoanatomy scalar $\tau_j>0$ whose logarithm is driven in turn, with the noise on its rate. The total hazard stays finite exactly when the strength's driver outruns the background and every unit's technoanatomy outruns the strength, so that no magnitude of accumulated capacity substitutes for technoanatomy, and no event of bounded duration perfectly separates the survivorship-conditioned law from the unconditioned one. First, a two-driver theorem: technoanatomy that stays bounded, or whose rate is critical, makes the viability event $\{\Lambda_\infty<\infty\}$ null for every driver; for strength drivers of bounded drift and anatomy rates independent of them, viability factors as the strength's driver-viability functional times the same functional of each unit's anatomy rate; a technoanatomy coupled to the strength, its scalar leading the strength and still improving, needs no bound on the driver, its factor being the same functional of the lead's rate; and in the solvable case the factors are two Gaussian sigmoids. Second, a conjectural transfer: where the internal channel is eventually dominated by the external one, the two-channel model's survival asymptotics are conjectured to coincide with those of the bare integrated-driver model with the credit doubled, up to a shift of initial data and bounded constants, conditional on a soft-wall reduction stated as an explicit Ansatz, supported numerically and not proved. Third, the survivorship interpolation $\PP(\cdot\mid T>t)$ is a monotone flow of upward tilts whose limit, in the critical regime, is the Doob $\hm$-transform: locally absolutely continuous with respect to the unconditioned law on survivors and globally singular. Fourth, the driver itself: continuous paths, stationary independent increments and the absence of a timescale leave the driftless Brownian rate, and a stretch of travel in one direction then obeys a Lindy law whose exponent is derived and not assumed, lasting a further $s$ at age $a$ with probability $(a/(a+s))^{1/2}$. Fifth, the pace of technoanatomy against strength is an output of the filtering and not a premise: with generic rates, independent and with nothing to hold a lead up, the conditioned law of every anatomy rate dominates the unconditioned one at every horizon, and among the histories that survive holding strength the lead of every unit is of order $t^{3/2}$, the scalar standing below the strength with probability $O(t^{-3/2}\log\log t)$ against one half unfiltered. Sixth, the strength is resolved into domains, each balanced by a technoanatomy of its own: the muffling of their sum is the product of their mufflings divided by an explicit symmetric factor, viability is $\prod_j(1-V_j+V_jA_j)-\prod_j(1-V_j)$, the pace holds domain by domain, and the amplitudes of a redundant decomposition into institutions are the weights of a quadrature, reached by a fixed point and independent of the decomposition up to its resolution. Seventh, the units are let share their loads: the internal hazard is the integral of $\sinh^2$ of a blend of the loads against a measure on the simplex of blends, a family that holds separate units, one merged unit, their mixtures and an expansion with one term for every set of units; every member obeys a single unit's law along the common direction, the second derivatives across it say which units are the same, and a rule on the derivative along the difference of two loads fixes the hazard for two units and leaves one number free for three or more. Monte Carlo experiments support the $t^{-1/4}$ exponent and the finite-window kinematic factor, and indicate that a unit whose technoanatomy is held at criticality adds one quarter to the exponent. One candidate empirical signature follows, a survivor hazard decaying as $1/(4t)$ under criticality, which does not by itself distinguish the model from static frailty.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Stochastic hazard rates are standard objects: the wrapper
$S_t=\exp(-\int_0^t h)$ with $h$ a diffusion is the reduced-form
framework of credit risk \cite{Lan04} and of stochastic mortality,
where the force of mortality is routinely modeled by a diffusion, the
mean-reverting Brownian Gompertz specification of Milevsky and
Promislow \cite{MP01} being a canonical instance. What distinguishes
the present model is not the wrapper but \emph{where the noise is
placed}: standard practice diffuses $h$ or $\log h$, whereas we diffuse
$\mu=-\tfrac{d}{dt}\log h$, one integration further out. The
consequences are structural rather than cosmetic: $\log h$ becomes
$C^1$, the state becomes a degenerate, $3/2$-self-similar hypoelliptic
diffusion, Kolmogorov's 1934 example \cite{Kol34,Hor67}, and the
governing persistence exponent moves from $\tfrac12$ to $\tfrac14$.

is a driver evolving in time and the object is a limit of path

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

\noindent\textit{Why two channels.} The model's split of the hazard into an external channel and an internal one, with the internal channel loaded by capacity relative to a technoanatomy that has to keep pace with it, records one observation that motivated it, the internal/external decomposition itself going back to \cite{GoldenScience2022}: once a civilization can destroy or redirect an asteroid on course to wipe out its planet, it has muffled an external hazard with a power that can equally be turned back on the biosphere as a weapon, so the same growth of capacity that answers the external channel opens the internal one, and maintaining the biosphere with stability, an internal rate low and robustly low, becomes a requirement that grows with capacity rather than one it discharges. \begin{definition}[Driftless model]\label{def:model}\footnote{The explicit reason I chose to use the logarithmic derivative of the
hazard rate for the Wiener noise in the first place was for better consistency
with the Lindy effect, which makes the system want to ``hold'' its intrinsic
parameters for a time. That is, in that my original statement of the Golden
Point already emphasizes the local capacity in a civilization for
self-preservation, (with this capacity intuitively being related to the
negative logarithmic derivative of the hazard rate, which after all remains a
positive constant in a biosphere whose hazard rate decays exponentially), then
applying the Lindy effect to this capacity itself has you expect to see each
next metaphorical second typically undergo a similar proportional shift in the
hazard rate as did the previous one, (since intuitively you would expect the
central tendency to continue this progression nonconfrontationally rather than
veer off of it suddenly). Hence, oscillations should probably be in this
capacity itself as opposed to in the hazard rate directly.}
% R886 (Opus 5.5, Oct 30 2026), at the author's direction: the author's footnote on the noise placement, synced from golden_reasoning.tex R880, verbatim.
Fix $\sigma>0$ and initial data $(\mu_0,D_0)\in\R\times\R$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
\mu_t=\mu_0+\sigma W_t,\qquad
D_t=D_0+\int_0^t\mu_s\,ds,\qquad
h_t=e^{-D_t},\qquad
\Lambda_t=\int_0^t h_u\,du .
\end{equation}
The credit $D$ and the hazard are one coordinate: the initial hazard
is $h_0:=e^{-D_0}$, and a multiplicative constant $q$ in the hazard
is the translation $D_0\mapsto D_0-\log q$ of the initial credit,
which is the content of Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge}. The hard wall of
Section~\ref{sec:persist} sits at $D=0$, where $e^{-D}$ passes
through one.
Let $E\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ be independent of $W$ and
$T:=\inf\{t\ge0:\Lambda_t\ge E\}$, so that
$\PP(T>t\mid\Fc^W_\infty)=e^{-\Lambda_t}$. The \emph{viability event}
is $\Imm:=\{\Lambda_\infty<\infty\}$; on $\Imm$ the conditional
probability of immortality, $e^{-\Lambda_\infty}$, is strictly
positive, and
$\PP(T=\infty)=\E[e^{-\Lambda_\infty}\one_\Imm]\le\PP(\Imm)$.
\end{definition}

The pair $(\mu,D)$ is the Kolmogorov diffusion,
$\Lc=\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\partial_\mu^2+\mu\,\partial_D$, Gaussian with
covariance
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\sigma^2t&\sigma^2t^2/2\\
\sigma^2t^2/2&\sigma^2t^3/3\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
and hypoelliptic by H\"ormander's condition at first order
\cite{Kol34,Hor67}. Three structural facts are immediate and load
bearing. There is no boundary in $h$: the logarithmic parametrization
makes $h>0$ automatic, and the whole problem concerns the growth of
the integrated driver. The credit $D$ is nondecreasing exactly while
$\mu\ge0$: accumulated credit is spent only through a negative rate.
And the initial hazard is exactly gauge:

\begin{lemma}[Gauge role of the initial credit; {[P]}]\label{lem:gauge}
Writing $\Lambda^{(D_0)}$ for the cumulative hazard from initial
credit $D_0$, $\Lambda^{(D_0)}_t=e^{-D_0}\Lambda^{(0)}_t$ pathwise;
hence $\Imm$ does not depend on $D_0$, while $\PP(T=\infty)$ is
continuous and nondecreasing in $D_0$, with
$\lim_{D_0\uparrow\infty}\PP(T=\infty)=\PP(\Imm)$ and
$\lim_{D_0\downarrow-\infty}\PP(T=\infty)=0$. The initial credit,
equivalently the initial hazard scale $h_0=e^{-D_0}$, can never affect
a dichotomy, only a value.
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[Nonparametric-Bayes reading]
Definition~\ref{def:model} places an integrated Wiener prior on
$-\log h$ --- the classical cubic smoothing-spline prior of Wahba
\cite{Wah78} --- so the results below may be read as the survival
asymptotics that canonical smoothness prior implies. A stronger
reading is available: writing $\mathcal D[f]:=-\frac{d}{dt}\log f$,
one has $\mathcal D[\bar F]=h$ and $\mathcal D[h]=\mu$, so the driver
is the second iterate of the operation that defines the hazard, and
the model occupies a rung of a ladder rather than a member of a
family. Noise at level one ($\log h$ Brownian) gives persistence
exponent $\tfrac12$; at level two, the present model, $\tfrac14$; at
level three the credit is twice-integrated Brownian motion and the
exponent is open \cite{AS15,AD13}. Level two is the last rung that is
both nontrivial and solvable.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Trend survivorship: the Lindy law of the rate; {[P]}]\label{prop:trend}
In the driftless model call a \emph{stretch} a maximal interval of time on
which the rate $\mu$ keeps one sign, so that the credit rises throughout
it or falls throughout it. Let a stretch in progress at time $t$ have
begun at the rate's last zero $g_t=\sup\{s\le t:\mu_s=0\}$, and call
$a=t-g_t>0$ its \emph{age}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item Given its age, the stretch lasts a further time $s$ with probability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trend}
\PP\bigl(\mu\text{ has no zero in }(t,t+s]\bigm|g_t=t-a\bigr)
=\Bigl(\frac{a}{a+s}\Bigr)^{1/2},
\end{equation}
exactly, for every $\sigma>0$, every $\mu_0$, and every $t$ and $s$.
\item The law \eqref{eq:trend} is that of a memoryless lifetime whose
rate $\lambda$ is unknown, weighted by $\lambda^{-1/2}d\lambda$ and
conditioned on the age attained:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trendmix}
\Bigl(\frac{a}{a+s}\Bigr)^{1/2}=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda s}\,\gamma_a(\lambda)\,d\lambda,
\qquad
\gamma_a(\lambda)=\Bigl(\frac{a}{\pi}\Bigr)^{1/2}\lambda^{-1/2}e^{-\lambda a}.
\end{equation}
The hazard of the travel reversing is $1/(2a)$, falling as the stretch
ages; the median further duration is $3a$; the mean further duration is
infinite.
\item The stretches are scale free as a population, before any
conditioning: under It\^o's excursion measure the length $\ell$ of a
stretch has density proportional to
\[
\ell^{-3/2}=\Gamma(\tfrac32)^{-1}\int_0^\infty\lambda^{-1/2}\,\lambda e^{-\lambda\ell}\,d\lambda,
\]
the same weight $\lambda^{-1/2}d\lambda$ over memoryless lifetimes.
\end{enumerate}
A stretch still in its first travel from $\mu_0\neq0$, with no zero yet at
time $t$, lasts a further $s$ with probability
$\operatorname{erf}\bigl(|\mu_0|/\sigma\sqrt{2(t+s)}\bigr)/\operatorname{erf}\bigl(|\mu_0|/\sigma\sqrt{2t}\bigr)$,
which tends to $(t/(t+s))^{1/2}$ once $t\gg\mu_0^2/\sigma^2$. The same
statements hold, with the same proof, of an anatomy rate and of a lead
rate when they are driftless Brownian: an improving technoanatomy keeps
improving, and a lead keeps widening, by the same law.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Dividing $\mu$ by $\sigma$ leaves its zeros in place, so take $\sigma=1$.
(i) Let $\mu_0=0$. By L\'evy's arcsine law the rate has no zero in an
interval $(u,v)$, $0<u<v$, with probability $(2/\pi)\arcsin\sqrt{u/v}$
\cite{RY99}. For $u<t<v$ the event $\{g_t\le u\}\cap\{\mu$ has no zero in
$(t,v]\}$ is the event that $\mu$ has no zero in $(u,v]$, so that,
differentiating in $u$,
\[
\PP\bigl(g_t\in du,\ \text{no zero in }(t,v]\bigr)=\frac{du}{\pi\sqrt{u(v-u)}},
\qquad
\PP(g_t\in du)=\frac{du}{\pi\sqrt{u(t-u)}},
\]
the second being the first at $v=t$. The ratio is $((t-u)/(v-u))^{1/2}$,
which is \eqref{eq:trend} with $a=t-u$ and $s=v-t$. If $\mu_0\neq0$, the
strong Markov property at the rate's first zero $r\le g_t$ restarts it
from zero, and the ratio, which depends on $u$, $t$ and $v$ only through
$t-u$ and $v-u$, is unchanged. (ii)
$\int_0^\infty\lambda^{-1/2}e^{-\lambda(a+s)}\,d\lambda=\Gamma(\tfrac12)(a+s)^{-1/2}$
with $\Gamma(\tfrac12)=\sqrt\pi$; $\gamma_a$ is the Gamma density of shape
$\tfrac12$ and rate $a$, of mean $1/(2a)$, which is also minus the
logarithmic derivative of \eqref{eq:trend} in $s$ at $s=0$;
$(a/(a+s))^{1/2}=\tfrac12$ at $s=3a$; and
$\int_0^\infty(a/(a+s))^{1/2}ds=\infty$. (iii) The excursion measure of
lengths is proportional to $\ell^{-3/2}d\ell$ \cite[Ch.~XII]{RY99}, and
$\int_0^\infty\lambda^{1/2}e^{-\lambda\ell}\,d\lambda=\Gamma(\tfrac32)\ell^{-3/2}$.
For the first travel, a Brownian motion from $m>0$ has no zero before $t$
with probability $\PP(|W_t|<m)=\operatorname{erf}(m/\sqrt{2t})$, by
reflection, and $\operatorname{erf}z\sim2z/\sqrt\pi$ as $z\to0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The rate that holds its travel is the generic one]\label{rem:genericrate}
The footnote to Definition~\ref{def:model} places the noise on the rate so
that the system holds its tendency for a time.
Proposition~\ref{prop:trend} is that holding made exact, and the weight
$\lambda^{-1/2}d\lambda$ of its item (ii) is derived, where a Lindy law
ordinarily takes its weight as a premise. The driftless Brownian rate is
in turn the only random rate meeting three requirements: continuous
paths; increments stationary and independent of the past, so that nothing
but the rate's present value bears on its future; and a law that carries
no timescale. The first two leave $\mu_0+ct+\sigma W_t$, a continuous
process with stationary independent increments being a Brownian motion
with drift, and $c$ and $\sigma$ both nonzero define the time
$\sigma^2/c^2$; so $c=0$, the rate of Definition~\ref{def:model}, or
$\sigma=0$ and no randomness. The event the law waits on is a change of
tendency, the credit beginning to fall where it rose, and the waiting is
the Lindy effect: a tendency that has held for an age $a$ has a median
further duration $3a$. One integration out, the same holding is read on
the level and not on the direction: the credit keeps above its starting
value through $t$ with probability of order $t^{-1/4}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:persist}), and the exponents $\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac14$
are those of Brownian motion and of its integral.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Trend survivorship with a linear drift; {[P]}]\label{cor:trenddrift}
Let the rate obey $d\mu_t=-\vartheta\mu_t\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t$ for a real
$\vartheta$. A stretch of age $a$, begun at a zero of the rate, lasts a
further $s$ with probability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trenddrift}
\Bigl(\frac{1-e^{-2\vartheta a}}{e^{2\vartheta s}-e^{-2\vartheta a}}\Bigr)^{1/2},
\end{equation}
which at $\vartheta=0$ is \eqref{eq:trend}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item For a damped rate, $\vartheta>0$, the law is \eqref{eq:trend} for
ages and durations short against $1/\vartheta$ and memoryless beyond, its
tail being $e^{-\vartheta s}(1-e^{-2\vartheta a})^{1/2}$: the rate has a
timescale, and the travel is held in proportion to its age only up to it.
\item For a self-reinforcing rate, $\vartheta<0$, the driver of
Corollary~\ref{cor:solvable}, the limit as $s\to\infty$ is
$(1-e^{-2|\vartheta|a})^{1/2}>0$: a tendency of age $a$ never reverses
with that probability, which tends to one as the stretch ages.
\end{enumerate}
The Lindy law is the boundary between the two, the case with no
timescale.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By the strong Markov property take the rate to start at a zero. Then
$\mu_r=e^{-\vartheta r}\beta(c(r))$ for a Brownian motion $\beta$ and the
clock $c(r)=\sigma^2(e^{2\vartheta r}-1)/(2\vartheta)$, so the zeros of
the rate are those of $\beta$ read on the clock, and \eqref{eq:trend}
holds in clock time: with $g$ the last zero before $t$, the stretch lasts
through $t+s$ with probability
$\bigl((c(t)-c(g))/(c(t+s)-c(g))\bigr)^{1/2}$, which is
\eqref{eq:trenddrift} with $a=t-g$. The two limits are read from it.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Two-channel model]\label{def:twochannel}\footnote{The
two-channel form and the viability criterion $\Lambda_\infty<\infty$
have a dated germ in the author's document of August 2022
\cite{GoldenScience2022}, where the criterion appears as $e^{a/b}<\infty$
under a two-channel ``Outlook''; the present formulation replaces the
germ and is cited for priority only.}
Fix $\sigma,\epsilon>0$ and $n,m\in\mathbb N$. With $\mu$, $x$ as in
\eqref{eq:model} (writing $x$ for the integrated driver), write
$x^+=\max(x,0)$ for the \emph{technostrength}, and let
$B_t=\sum_{i=1}^n e^{\epsilon W^{(i)}_t}$ for Brownian motions
$W^{(i)}$ independent of one another, of the driver $W$ and of the
anatomy rates below, an assumption the infinite-set lemma below requires
when the set is generated by the driver and the rates. The internal
channel carries $m$ units, the $j$th of amplitude $\Gamma_j>0$ and
\emph{technoanatomy scalar}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar}
\tau_{j,t}=e^{y_{j,t}},\qquad y_{j,t}=y_{j,0}+\int_0^t\eta_{j,s}\,ds,
\end{equation}
its \emph{anatomy rate} $\eta_j$ a regular diffusion. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hazard}
h^{\mathrm{tot}}_t
=\underbrace{B_t\operatorname{sech}^2x_t^+}_{\text{external}}
+\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^m\Gamma_j\sinh^2\!\Big(\frac{x_t^+}{\tau_{j,t}}\Big)}_{\text{internal}},
\end{equation}
and the unit's \emph{load} is $u_j=x^+/\tau_j$. Survival, $T$, and
$\Imm$ are as before with $h^{\mathrm{tot}}$ in place of $h$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Channel shapes; {[P]}]
\label{prop:dominance}
(i) The muffling factor is one at and below zero strength and falls
strictly above it, $\operatorname{sech}^2x^+=4e^{-2x}(1+O(e^{-2x}))$ as
$x\to\infty$: the external channel can be reduced without bound and is
never amplified. (ii) A unit vanishes at and below zero strength and
grows above it, as the square of its load while the load is small and as
$\tfrac14e^{2u}$ once it is large; at fixed scalars the internal channel
is unbounded above. (iii) Both channels are flat at the wall: with the
scalars fixed and the background frozen at $B$, $h(x)=B$ for $x\le0$,
$h'(0)=0$, and $h''(0^+)=2\big(\sum_j\Gamma_j\tau_j^{-2}-B\big)$, so that
acquiring strength lowers the hazard near the wall exactly when
$\sum_j\Gamma_j\tau_j^{-2}<B$. (iv) At fixed scalars and frozen
background, $h(x)\to\infty$ as $x\to\infty$ and $h_\ast=\inf_xh(x)>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Infinite sets defeat the driftless background; {[P]}]
\label{lem:infbackground}
Let $W$ be a standard Brownian motion, $\epsilon>0$, and let
$S\subset[0,\infty)$ be a measurable set, possibly random but
independent of $W$, with $\mathrm{Leb}(S)=\infty$ almost surely. Then
$\int_S e^{\epsilon W_u}\,du=\infty$ almost surely; consequently the
same holds with $B_u=\sum_{i=1}^ne^{\epsilon W^{(i)}_u}\ge
e^{\epsilon W^{(1)}_u}$ in place of $e^{\epsilon W_u}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conditioning on $S$, it suffices to treat deterministic $S$ of
infinite measure. For each $T$, finiteness of
$\int_{S\cap[T,\infty)}e^{\epsilon W_u}du$ is unchanged by the
multiplicative factor $e^{\epsilon W_T}$ --- it is decided by
$\int_{S\cap[T,\infty)}e^{\epsilon(W_u-W_T)}du$ --- and the integral
over $S\cap[0,T]$ is a.s.\ finite, so the event
$\{\int_Se^{\epsilon W_u}du=\infty\}$ lies in
$\sigma(W_u-W_T:u\ge T)$ for every $T$, hence in the tail
$\sigma$-field of the increments, and has probability $0$ or $1$ by
Kolmogorov's zero--one law. Were the probability $0$, then
$\int_Se^{\epsilon W_u}du<\infty$ a.s., and applying the same to $-W$,
also $\int_Se^{-\epsilon W_u}du<\infty$ a.s.; adding,
$2\,\mathrm{Leb}(S)\le\int_S(e^{\epsilon W_u}+e^{-\epsilon W_u})du
<\infty$, contradicting $\mathrm{Leb}(S)=\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Recurrent drivers; {[P]}]\label{prop:recurrent}
Suppose the driver of \S\ref{sec:model} is positive recurrent with
stationary law $\pi$ and $\int|\mu|\,\pi(d\mu)<\infty$; write
$\bar m:=\int\mu\,\pi(d\mu)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\alph*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item If $\bar m>0$ then $D_t/t\to\bar m$ a.s., $\Lambda_\infty<\infty$
a.s., and $\PP(\Imm)=1$.
\item If $\bar m<0$ then $\Lambda_\infty=\infty$ a.s.\ and $T<\infty$ a.s.
\item If $\bar m=0$ and the cycle integral defined in the proof is
nondegenerate (automatic for nondegenerate diffusions), then
$\Lambda_\infty=\infty$ a.s.: \emph{the critical case falls on the mortal
side.}
\end{enumerate}
For null-recurrent drivers no general statement is offered
\textbf{[O]}; the Brownian driver, the fundamental null-recurrent case, is
settled unconditionally by the driftless analysis of \S\ref{sec:persist}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a), (b): the ergodic theorem for positive recurrent one-dimensional
diffusions gives $D_t/t\to\bar m$ a.s.\ (see \cite[Ch.~X]{RY99}); if
$\bar m>0$ then eventually $D_t\ge D_0+\tfrac{\bar m}2t$ and
$\Lambda_\infty<\infty$; if $\bar m<0$ the integrand
$e^{-(D_t-D_0)}\to\infty$.

(c) Fix interior levels $a<b$ and regenerate at up-crossings: let
$R_0:=\inf\{t:\mu_t=b\}$ and inductively
$S_k:=\inf\{t>R_{k-1}:\mu_t=a\}$, $R_k:=\inf\{t>S_k:\mu_t=b\}$. By the
strong Markov property the cycles over $[R_{k-1},R_k)$ are i.i.d.; positive
recurrence gives $\E[L]<\infty$ for the cycle length
$L:=R_1-R_0$, and the cycle occupation identity yields, for the cycle
integral $\xi_k:=\int_{R_{k-1}}^{R_k}\mu_s\,ds$,
\[
\E[\xi_1]=\E[L]\int\mu\,d\pi=0,\qquad
\E|\xi_1|\le\E\Bigl[\int_{\text{cycle}}|\mu_s|ds\Bigr]
=\E[L]\int|\mu|\,d\pi<\infty .
\]
Thus $S_n:=D_{R_n}-D_{R_0}=\sum_{k\le n}\xi_k$ is a nondegenerate mean-zero
i.i.d.\ walk, so $\liminf_nS_n=-\infty$ a.s.\ by the classical
oscillation dichotomy for random walks (see, e.g., \cite{Dur19});
in particular the events $B_n:=\{D_{R_n}\le-1\}$ occur infinitely often
a.s. Choose $\delta>0$ and $M<\infty$ with
\[
q:=\PP_b\Bigl(L\ge\delta,\ \sup_{0\le u\le\delta}
\Bigl|\int_0^u\mu_s\,ds\Bigr|\le M\Bigr)>0,
\]
possible since $L>0$ a.s.\ and the supremum is finite a.s. Let
$G_n$ be the corresponding event for cycle $n{+}1$; by the strong Markov
property $\PP(G_n\mid\Fc_{R_n})=q$, so
$\sum_n\PP(B_n\cap G_n\mid\Fc_{R_n})=q\sum_n\one_{B_n}=\infty$ a.s., and
L\'evy's extension of the Borel--Cantelli lemma (see, e.g.,
\cite{Dur19}) gives
$B_n\cap G_n$ infinitely often a.s. On $B_n\cap G_n$, for
$u\in[0,\delta]$ one has $D_{R_n+u}\le-1+M$, hence
\[
\int_{R_n}^{R_n+\delta}e^{-D_s}\,ds\ \ge\ \delta\,e^{\,1-M-D_0}\,>\,0 ,
\]
and these windows are disjoint. Summing over infinitely many of them,
$\Lambda_\infty=\infty$ a.s.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Technoanatomy that does not keep growing is fatal; {[P]}]
\label{thm:trapped}
(i) If some unit's scalar is bounded, $\sup_t\tau_{j,t}<\infty$, then
$\Lambda_\infty=\infty$ almost surely, for the random background and
regardless of the driver --- pathwise in the driver and the anatomy
rates, almost surely in the background. (ii) The same holds if some
unit's anatomy rate is the driftless Brownian rate, or a nondegenerate
positive recurrent rate with nonpositive stationary mean. (iii) If the
scalars are fixed and the background is frozen, $B_t\equiv B>0$, then
$\PP(T>t)\le e^{-h_\ast t}$ with $h_\ast>0$ the floor of
Proposition~\ref{prop:dominance}(iv), whence $\E[T]<\infty$. For the
random background no positive floor exists ($\inf_tB_t=0$ a.s.), so the
exponential rate is frozen-background (annealed) only, and whether an
unconditional rate holds is open [O]; whether any power-law structure
survives in the random-background case is open [O], since almost-sure
death does not exclude polynomial survival tails and the exclusion is
proved only in the frozen-background setting of (iii).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On any driver path with $\Leb\{t:x_t\le M\}=\infty$ for some integer
$M\ge0$, the external term is at least $B_t\operatorname{sech}^2M$ on
that set, and Lemma~\ref{lem:infbackground} gives $\Lambda_\infty=\infty$
almost surely; on the remaining paths $\Leb\{t:x_t\le M\}<\infty$ for
every $M$. (i) If $\tau_{j,t}\le\bar\tau$, then on $\{t:x_t>1\}$, of
infinite measure, the unit's hazard is at least
$\Gamma_j\sinh^2(1/\bar\tau)$. (ii) A driftless rate makes
$\{t:y_{j,t}<y_{j,0}\}$ of logarithmic density one half, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:half} applied to $y_j$, hence of infinite measure, and so
is its intersection with $\{t:x_t\ge e^{y_{j,0}}\}$, on which the load is
at least one and the unit's hazard at least $\Gamma_j\sinh^21$. A
positive recurrent rate with negative mean sends $y_j$ to $-\infty$, which
is case (i); with zero mean, the cycle argument in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:recurrent}(c), applied to $y_j$ in place of the
credit, gives infinitely many disjoint windows of a fixed length on which
$y_j$ is bounded above by a constant $M'$, and the load there is at least
one once $x_t\ge e^{M'}$. (iii) $\Lambda_t\ge h_\ast t$ and
$\PP(T>t)=\E[e^{-\Lambda_t}]\le e^{-h_\ast t}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two drivers; {[P]}]\label{thm:twocond}
Let the driver be a regular diffusion on $\R$ with scale function $s$.
Define the \emph{driver-viability functional}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vdrv}
V_{\mathrm{drv}}(\mu_0)=
\begin{cases}
\PP_{\mu_0}(\mu_t\to+\infty)=\dfrac{s(\mu_0)-s(-\infty)}{s(+\infty)-s(-\infty)},
& \text{transient driver,}\\[8pt]
1, & \text{positive recurrent, }\bar m>0,\\[2pt]
0, & \text{positive recurrent, }\bar m\le0,\\[2pt]
0, & \text{Brownian driver,}\\[2pt]
\text{open [O]}, & \text{general null recurrence,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $\bar m=\int\mu\,d\pi$ the stationary mean where it exists, the
transient formula read with its boundary conventions ($s(+\infty)=\infty$
and $s(-\infty)$ finite giving $0$; $s$ bounded above with
$s(-\infty)=-\infty$ giving $1$).
Write $V_{\mathrm{anat}}$ for the same functional of an anatomy rate.
(i) On the event that the credit grows at least linearly,
$\liminf_tx_t/t>0$, which carries the driver's escape and its positive
recurrence with positive mean, $\Lambda_\infty<\infty$ exactly when
$\int_0^\infty\sinh^2(x_t^+/\tau_{j,t})\,dt<\infty$ for every unit, and,
where the loads stay bounded, exactly when every unit's load is
square-integrable in time: the weakest unit decides. Off that event
$\Lambda_\infty=\infty$, within the scope clause of \eqref{eq:vdrv}. (ii)
If the anatomy rates are moreover independent of the driver and of one
another, and the driver has bounded drift and bounded noise coefficient,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twocond}
\PP\bigl(\Lambda_\infty<\infty\bigr)
=\underbrace{V_{\mathrm{drv}}(\mu_0)}_{\text{strength}}
\cdot
\underbrace{\prod_{j=1}^mV_{\mathrm{anat}}(\eta_{j,0})}_{\text{technoanatomy}}.
\end{equation}
The factors are logically distinct necessary conditions, carried by
independent drivers: the first is the strength outrunning the background,
the others are each unit's technoanatomy outrunning the strength, and no
magnitude of accumulated capacity substitutes for them. In the positive
recurrent case a driver does not escape; its time integral acquires
positive linear drift, which is why the functional is stated piecewise
rather than as a single scale-function ratio.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) Where $x_t\ge ct$ eventually, $c>0$, the external term is at most
$4B_te^{-2ct}$ eventually, which the background's subexponential growth
leaves integrable; viability is then the integrability of the internal
channel, unit by unit, and where a load stays below $U$,
$u^2\le\sinh^2u\le(\sinh U/U)^2u^2$. Off that event: on
$\{\mu_t\to-\infty\}$ the technostrength is eventually zero, the hazard is
$B_t$, and $\Lambda_\infty=\infty$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:infbackground}; a
positive recurrent driver with $\bar m<0$ sends the credit to $-\infty$ in
the same way, and one with $\bar m=0$ (nondegenerate) has the windows of
the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:recurrent}(c), of infinite total
measure, on which the technostrength stays bounded, so that
Lemma~\ref{lem:infbackground} applies; and the Brownian driver puts the
credit below its initial value on a set of logarithmic density one half
(Theorem~\ref{thm:half}), with the same conclusion. General null
recurrence is left open, which is the statement's scope clause. (ii) A
driver of bounded drift $K$ and noise coefficient at most $\bar\sigma$ has
$|\mu_t|\le|\mu_0|+Kt+|M_t|$, with $M$ a continuous martingale of quadratic
variation at most $\bar\sigma^2t$, so $|M_t|=O(\sqrt{t\log\log t})$ and
$|x_t|=O(t^2)$ almost surely. On a unit's escape event
$\eta_{j,t}\to\infty$, so $y_{j,t}/t\to\infty$; with a positive stationary
mean $\bar\eta_j$, $y_{j,t}\ge\tfrac12\bar\eta_jt$ eventually. Either way
$x_t^2e^{-2y_{j,t}}$ is integrable and the load tends to zero, so the unit
is integrable; on each unit's complementary events
Theorem~\ref{thm:trapped} applies, and independence of the drivers gives
the product. The hitting probabilities are the classical scale-function
formula \cite{KT81,RY99}. Throughout, the background $B$ is random and is
never fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Technoanatomy that leads the strength; {[P]}]\label{cor:lead}
Let a unit's scalar be coupled to the strength in place of
\eqref{eq:scalar}: on $\{x_t>0\}$, $\tau_{j,t}=x_t\,e^{\ell_{j,t}}$, with
\emph{lead} $\ell_{j,t}=\ell_{j,0}+\int_0^t\zeta_{j,s}\,ds$ and lead rate
$\zeta_j$ a regular diffusion, so that the unit's load is $e^{-\ell_j}$
wherever the strength is positive and its gain is
$\mathcal D[h^{\mathrm{unit}}]=2u\coth(u)\,\zeta_j$. On the event that the
credit grows at least linearly the unit is integrable exactly when
$\int_0^\infty\sinh^2(e^{-\ell_{j,t}})\,dt<\infty$, which, where the lead is
bounded below, is the integrability of $e^{-2\ell_j}$; no bound on the
driver's growth enters. If every unit is coupled in this way, with lead
rates independent of the background, of the driver and of one another,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lead}
\PP\bigl(\Lambda_\infty<\infty\bigr)
=V_{\mathrm{drv}}(\mu_0)\prod_{j=1}^mV_{\mathrm{lead}}(\zeta_{j,0}),
\end{equation}
with $V_{\mathrm{lead}}$ the functional \eqref{eq:vdrv} of a lead rate,
under the scope clause of Theorem~\ref{thm:twocond}. A scalar standing at
least this far ahead does at least as well, the hazard being
nonincreasing in each scalar; and where the lead outgrows the strength by
more than the background's fluctuations,
$\ell_{j,t}-x_t+\tfrac12\log B_t\to\infty$, the unit is eventually
dominated by the external channel. The coupled unit does not vanish as
the strength falls to zero from above, its load being $e^{-\ell_j}$ at
every positive strength: the coupled form describes a strength clear of
the wall.

\begin{corollary}[A technoanatomy locked to its strength: the three signs; {[P]}]\label{cor:locked}
Write $\rho_j$ for a unit's \emph{gain}, its hazard reduction per unit of
strength gained along the history,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain}
\rho_j:=-\frac{d\log h^{\mathrm{unit}}_j}{dx}
=\frac{\mathcal D[h^{\mathrm{unit}}_j]}{\mu}
=2u_j\coth(u_j)\,\Bigl(\frac{\eta_j}{\mu}-\frac1x\Bigr)
\end{equation}
on $\{x>0,\ \mu\neq0\}$, which in the lead form of
Corollary~\ref{cor:lead} is $2u_j\coth(u_j)\,\zeta_j/\mu$. Let the lead be
\emph{locked}, a function $\ell(x)$ of the strength alone, so that the
gain is $2u\coth(u)\,\ell'(x)$, at small load twice the lead gained per
unit of strength.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item If $\ell=\ell_0+\tfrac12\rho x$ with a constant $\rho>0$, the unit's
hazard on $\{x>0\}$ is
\[
\Gamma\sinh^2\!\bigl(e^{-\ell_0-\rho x/2}\bigr)
=\Gamma e^{-2\ell_0}\,e^{-\rho x}\bigl(1+O(e^{-2\ell})\bigr),
\]
an exponential in the strength whose gain tends to $\rho$, and the unit
is integrable wherever the credit grows at least linearly.
\item If the lead is held, $\ell\equiv\ell_0$, so that the scalar grows in
proportion to the strength, the unit's hazard is the constant
$\Gamma\sinh^2(e^{-\ell_0})$ wherever the strength is positive, its gain
is zero, and viability is null.
\item If the scalar is held, $\tau\equiv\bar\tau$, the unit's hazard is
$\Gamma\sinh^2(x/\bar\tau)\sim\tfrac14\Gamma e^{2x/\bar\tau}$, its gain
tends to $-2/\bar\tau$, and viability is null
(Theorem~\ref{thm:trapped}); a lead that falls as the strength grows,
$\rho<0$ in (i), is fatal in the same way.
\end{enumerate}
Hence, for a technoanatomy locked to its strength at a constant gain
$\rho$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:locked}
\PP\bigl(\Lambda_\infty<\infty\bigr)=\one\{\rho>0\}\,V_{\mathrm{drv}}(\mu_0)
\end{equation}
under the scope clause of Theorem~\ref{thm:twocond}: the lead having no
rate of its own, the technoanatomy factor of \eqref{eq:lead} degenerates
to the sign of the gain. The correspondence is exact as well as
asymptotic: on $\{x>0\}$ an internal hazard $\Gamma'e^{-\rho x}$, for any
real $\rho$, is the unit of amplitude $\Gamma$ carrying the load
$u=\operatorname{arsinh}\bigl((\Gamma'/\Gamma)^{1/2}e^{-\rho x/2}\bigr)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The identity \eqref{eq:gain} is the unit's gain of
Remark~\ref{rem:gains} divided by $dx/dt=\mu$; in the lead form
$u=e^{-\ell}$ and $\zeta=\ell'(x)\mu$, and $u\coth u\to1$ as $u\to0$. (i)
$\sinh^2v=v^2(1+O(v^2))$, and where $x_t\ge ct$ the unit's hazard is at
most a multiple of $e^{-\rho ct}$. (ii), (iii): on
$\{\liminf_tx_t/t>0\}$ the strength is eventually positive and the unit's
hazard is bounded below, by a constant in (ii) and as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:trapped}(i) in (iii), the load of a falling lead being
unbounded; off that event Theorem~\ref{thm:twocond}(i) gives
$\Lambda_\infty=\infty$. With (i) and the external term's integrability
on that event, this is \eqref{eq:locked}. The last claim solves
$\Gamma\sinh^2u=\Gamma'e^{-\rho x}$ for $u$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The solvable case: two sigmoids; {[P]}]\label{cor:solvable}
For the self-reinforcing driver $d\mu_t=\nu\mu_t\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t$,
$\nu>0$ --- the member of the driver family whose drift is
proportional to its state ---
$s'(v)=e^{-\nu v^2/\sigma^2}$ is integrable at both ends, and for a single
unit whose anatomy rate is self-reinforcing as well,
$d\eta_t=\lambda\eta_t\,dt+s_a\,dW^{a}_t$ with $\lambda>0$ and $W^a$
independent of $W$ and of the background,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmoid}
\PP\bigl(\Lambda_\infty<\infty\bigr)
=\Phi\!\Bigl(\frac{\mu_0\sqrt{2\nu}}{\sigma}\Bigr)\cdot\Phi\!\Bigl(\frac{\eta_0\sqrt{2\lambda}}{s_a}\Bigr):
\end{equation}
a smooth Gaussian sigmoid of width $\sigma/\sqrt{2\nu}$ in the strength's
initial rate, multiplied by another of width $s_a/\sqrt{2\lambda}$ in the
initial rate of technoanatomy. Each factor is an escape probability. On
the joint escape event $|x_t|=O(e^{\nu t})$ while $y_t$ grows as a positive
multiple of $e^{\lambda t}$, so the load decays faster than any
exponential; off it, Theorem~\ref{thm:trapped} or the external channel
applies. The same two sigmoids hold in the lead form of
Corollary~\ref{cor:lead} with a self-reinforcing lead rate, the second
factor then read in the lead rate's parameters. With a technoanatomy that
falls behind, feedback accelerates failure: $\mu_t\to+\infty$ drives the
unit's hazard up superexponentially, so a system whose technoanatomy lags
is strictly worse off with feedback than without.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Overload, and the open directions; {[S]}]\label{rem:fluct}
An overload of duration $\delta$ at load $u$ contributes
$\asymp\delta\,\Gamma\sinh^2u$ to $\Lambda$, so survivability requires
$\delta\lesssim\Gamma^{-1}\operatorname{csch}^2u$: the affordable duration
of overload decays exponentially in the load. Overload recurring
arbitrarily late need not be fatal: load $n$ on the intervals
$[n,n+e^{-3n}]$ costs $\Gamma\sum_ne^{-3n}\sinh^2n<\infty$. What is fatal is
a load bounded below on a set of infinite measure, which is
Corollary~\ref{cor:overlap}. Theorem~\ref{thm:twocond} gives viability as
a two-transience condition, one transience for the strength and one for
each unit's technoanatomy, and the lead form of Corollary~\ref{cor:lead}
covers a technoanatomy coupled to the strength. Correlation between the
driver and an anatomy rate in general, and general null recurrence of a
rate, are open [O].
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Overload on a set of infinite measure is fatal;
{[P]}]\label{cor:overlap}
If there exist $a>0$ and a unit $j$ with
$\Leb\{t:x_t^+/\tau_{j,t}\ge a\}=\infty$ almost surely, then
$\Lambda_\infty=\infty$ almost surely, since on that set the unit's
hazard is at least $\Gamma_j\sinh^2a$. What probability of viability a
general coupling of the scalar to the strength yields is open [O].
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Adverse credit occupies half of logarithmic time in the
critical regime; {[P]}]\label{thm:half}
For the driftless driver, almost surely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:half}
\lim_{S\to\infty}\frac1S\,\Leb\bigl\{s\in[0,S]:D_{e^{s}}-D_0<0\bigr\}=\frac12,
\end{equation}
so the set of times at which the credit sits below its initial value
has logarithmic density exactly one half, for every initial datum. The
statement is confined to the critical recurrent regime and fails for
any driver whose credit is transient.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$Z_s:=(D_{e^{s}}-D_0)/(\sigma e^{3s/2})$ is a stationary, centered,
Gaussian, ergodic process, by the Lamperti transform of the integrated
Brownian motion under its $3/2$-self-similarity, so Birkhoff's ergodic
theorem gives logarithmic density $\PP(Z_0<0)=\tfrac12$ by symmetry.
\end{proof} For general $\mu_0$ the rescaled increment is $(D_{e^s}-D_0)/(\sigma e^{3s/2}) = (\mu_0/\sigma)\,e^{-s/2} + e^{-3s/2}\int_0^{e^s}W_u\,du$, so the displayed process is exactly stationary and centered only when $\mu_0 = 0$; the proof applies ergodicity to the stationary component and then uses that the deterministic shift $(\mu_0/\sigma)e^{-s/2}$ vanishes, which leaves the limiting sign occupation unchanged, so the conclusion holds for every $\mu_0$.

reads it as the statement that adverse strength is a generic condition
in the critical regime and not evidence of a technoanatomy falling behind.

\section{Persistence and the transfer}\label{sec:persist}

\begin{theorem}[Persistence of the hard-wall functional; {[C]}]
\label{thm:persist}
Let $\tau:=\inf\{t\ge0:D_t\le0\}$ for the driftless model started at
$\mu_0\ge0$, $D_0>0$. Then
$\PP_{(\mu,d)}(\tau>t)\sim c\,\hm(\mu,d)\,t^{-1/4}$ as $t\to\infty$,
where $\hm$ is the persistence-harmonic function of the killed
Kolmogorov semigroup, with boundary behavior
$\hm(\mu,0^+)\propto\mu^{1/2}$ and $\hm(0^+,d)\propto d^{1/6}$, and
$\E_x[\hm(X_s)\one_{\{\tau>s\}}]=\hm(x)$. The exponent is Sinai's
\cite{Sin92}; the refined form and the harmonic function are
McKean, Goldman, Lachal, Isozaki--Watanabe, and
Groeneboom--Jongbloed--Wellner
\cite{McK63,Gol71,Lac91,IW94,GJW99}; universality is
Denisov--Wachtel \cite{DW15}, surveyed in \cite{AS15,BMS13}.
\end{theorem}

The soft-killing functional replaces the hard wall by the killing rate
$e^{-D}$, exponentially negligible for $D\gg0$ and overwhelming for
$D\ll0$: an absorbing wall of thickness $O(1)$ in $D$ at the level
$D=0$ where the hard wall $\tau=\inf\{t:D_t\le0\}$ sits, negligible
against excursions of size $t^{3/2}$. Killing approximates absorption
when the credit crosses downward through zero, and the sign convention
is fixed here once so that it cannot drift. The reduction is stated as two hypotheses of increasing strength,
because the exponent and the conditioned law require different
things: the tail asymptotics rest on the weaker and the $Q$-process
on the stronger.

\begin{ansatz}[A: soft killing preserves the exponent]\label{ans:soft}
For every admissible initial datum there exist
$0<c\le C<\infty$ and $t_0$ with
$c\,\PP(\tau>t)\le\PP(T>t)\le C\,\PP(\tau>t)$ for $t\ge t_0$, after an
$O(1)$ adjustment of the initial credit. Equivalently
$\log\PP(T>t)=\log\PP(\tau>t)+O(1)$.
\end{ansatz}

\begin{ansatz}[B: soft survival has a ratio asymptotic]\label{ans:ratio}
Writing $K_tf(x)=\E_x[e^{-\int_0^tq(X_u)du}f(X_t)]$ for the
Feynman--Kac semigroup with $q(\mu,D)=e^{-D}$, there is a positive
function $g$ on the state space and a constant $c_{\mathrm{soft}}$ with
$K_t1(x)\sim c_{\mathrm{soft}}\,g(x)\,t^{-1/4}$ locally uniformly in
$x$; formally $g$ is the zero-energy positive solution of
$(\Lc-q)g=0$ with the growth conditions the soft wall imposes.
\end{ansatz}

\noindent Ansatz~\ref{ans:soft} does not imply Ansatz~\ref{ans:ratio}:
comparability fixes $\log\PP_x(T>t)$ to $O(1)$ but leaves the hidden
constant free to depend on $x$, so that $\PP_x(T>t)\sim c(x)\hm(x)t^{-1/4}$
with $c$ non-constant is compatible with A and incompatible with the
conclusion that the soft conditioned law is the hard-wall
$\hm$-transform. The question the two hypotheses leave open, which
the numerical agenda of Section~\ref{sec:numerics} targets, is not whether $g = C\,\hm(\cdot + \delta)$ for some effective wall translation $\delta$, which exact proportionality the operators obstruct, since $\mathcal{L}\hm = 0$ makes a translated $\hm$ still $\mathcal{L}$-harmonic while positive $g$ satisfies $\mathcal{L} g = q g > 0$, but whether $g$ approaches a translated $\hm$ in an appropriately specified asymptotic or scaling limit, which is the question the numerics target.

One half is rigorous modulo a cited input: on the event
$\{D_u\ge2\log\frac{2+u}{2}\ \forall u\le t\}$ the cumulative hazard
is bounded, and a logarithmic moving boundary does not change the
persistence exponent of an integrated process \cite{AS15,DW15}; the
upper half reduces to the plausible, unproved statement that
persistence with a bounded occupation tolerance has the same exponent
as strict persistence. The nearest standing literature cuts against
assuming this lightly: persistence with \emph{partial survival}, each crossing killing with probability $p$, carries exponents
varying continuously with $p$ \cite{MB98}, for the random acceleration
process among others \cite{Bur14}, so softening a wall can shift an
exponent. What distinguishes the present softening is its structure:
killing is rate-based and confined to a boundary layer of width $O(1)$
in the credit against excursions of scale $t^{3/2}$, a vanishing
fraction of the range, where partial survival taxes every crossing
at fixed price at every scale. Everything tagged [A] depends on exactly
this gap, and Section~\ref{sec:numerics} tests it directly; the
measured constancy of the soft-to-hard ratio is evidence the
boundary-layer case sits in the exponent-preserving class the
moving-boundary results delimit from the rigorous side.

\begin{remark}[The exponent with critical technoanatomy; {[S]}, proof {[O]}]\label{rem:addexp}
Proposition~\ref{thm:transfer} concerns a technoanatomy that has escaped
ahead. A unit whose anatomy rate, or whose lead rate, is itself the
driftless Brownian rate is never dominated: by
Theorem~\ref{thm:trapped}(ii) and Corollary~\ref{cor:lead} it makes
Departure certain, and it changes the exponent. Survival to $t$ then asks
two things of two independent integrated Brownian motions: of the credit,
that it keep above the wall; and of $\log\tau$, that it keep ahead of
$\log x^+$, a level that moves only logarithmically in $t$, or of the
lead, that it keep above a fixed level. Each has persistence exponent
$\tfrac14$, a logarithmic moving boundary not changing it
\cite{AS15,DW15}, and the scaling expectation at frozen background is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:addexp}
\PP(T>t)\asymp t^{-(1+m)/4}
\end{equation}
for $m$ units with independent critical rates: one quarter for the
strength and one for each technoanatomy held at criticality, the hazard
of survivors being $(1+m)/(4t)$ in place of $1/(4t)$.
Section~\ref{sec:numerics} finds slopes $-0.51$ and $-0.50$ for one unit
in the two forms, against $-0.25$ for the external channel alone; larger
$m$ is untested, and a proof, with the case of correlated rates, is open
[O].
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Two walls: the pace as an output of conditioning; {[S]}]\label{rem:pace}
In the coordinates of strength and lead the hazard on $\{x>0\}$ is
$B_t\operatorname{sech}^2x+\sum_j\Gamma_j\sinh^2(e^{-\ell_j})$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:leadcoord}): the external channel is a soft wall
for the strength, at zero, and each unit is a soft wall for its own lead,
at the level $-\log\operatorname{arsinh}\Gamma_j^{-1/2}$ where its hazard
is of order one. Survival is the joint persistence of the strength above
the first wall and of every lead above its own, which is the content of
Remark~\ref{rem:addexp}. Let the rates be the generic ones of
Remark~\ref{rem:genericrate}, driftless and independent, so that nothing
in the unconditioned law holds a lead up: $\log\tau_j$ is symmetric about
its start while $\log x^+$ grows, and the typical lead falls.
Conditioning on survival then acts on each lead as it acts on the
strength. On the scaling picture the conditioned law is that of two
integrated Brownian motions each kept above its wall: the leads of
survivors grow as $t^{3/2}$, as their strength does; an anatomy rate whose
lead is near its wall receives the upward drift of
Section~\ref{sec:cond}, as the driver does near its own; and the loads of
survivors tend to zero. The pace of technoanatomy against strength, its
standing far ahead and still gaining, is thus what the generic law looks
like after the filtering, and not a premise laid on it. While a lead is
near its wall the two rates of survivors are moreover positively
correlated, strength gained being survivable only with technoanatomy
gained, and the coupling relaxes once the lead has run ahead.
Section~\ref{sec:numerics} reports the experiment.
Theorem~\ref{thm:pace} proves this of the leads, for the histories that
survive holding strength: their leads are of order $t^{3/2}$ at the
horizon, their scalars stand below their strength with probability
$O(t^{-3/2}\log\log t)$ against one half unfiltered, and the conditioned
law of every anatomy rate dominates the generic one. At scaling level
there remain the exponent \eqref{eq:addexp}, the drift and the
correlation of the rates near a wall, and, short of the doubling of
Theorem~\ref{thm:pace}(v), that survivors hold strength at all. On the
last the background matters. With the driftless background of
Definition~\ref{def:twochannel} a history can also survive holding no
strength, its units unloaded, while the background stays quiet, with
probability of order $t^{-1/4-n/2}$ on the same picture, against
$t^{-(1+m)/4}$ for the route through strength and leads: the second route
outweighs the first when $m<2n$, and the conditioning on $H_t$ sets the
first aside.
\end{remark}

Let $\beta$ be a Brownian motion on $[0,t]$, of any volatility and from a
fixed start, and let $F$ and $G$ be bounded measurable functionals of its
path, each nondecreasing: $F(\omega)\le F(\omega')$ whenever
$\omega_s\le\omega'_s$ for every $s\le t$. Then
$\E[F(\beta)G(\beta)]\ge\E[F(\beta)]\,\E[G(\beta)]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For independent real variables $X_1,\dots,X_k$ and bounded functions $f$
and $g$ nondecreasing in each argument, $\E[fg]\ge\E[f]\,\E[g]$, which is
the association of independent variables \cite{EPW67}. For $k=1$,
$(f(X)-f(X'))(g(X)-g(X'))\ge0$ for an independent copy $X'$ of $X$, and
the expectation of the product is $2\E[fg]-2\E[f]\E[g]$. For the step,
conditioning on $X_k$ gives $\E[fg\mid X_k]\ge\varphi(X_k)\psi(X_k)$ with
$\varphi(z)=\E f(X_1,\dots,X_{k-1},z)$ and $\psi$ likewise, both
nondecreasing, to which the case $k=1$ applies. The values of $\beta$ at
the points $it2^{-N}$, $i\le2^N$, are sums of independent increments, and
given those values $\beta$ is their linear interpolation plus Brownian
bridges independent of them. Hence
$F_N:=\E[F(\beta)\mid\beta_{it2^{-N}},\,i\le2^N]$ is a nondecreasing
function of the increments, as is $G_N$, and
$\E[F_NG_N]\ge\E[F_N]\,\E[G_N]=\E[F]\,\E[G]$. The path being continuous,
those values generate its $\sigma$-field as $N\to\infty$, so that
$F_N\to F$ and $G_N\to G$ in $L^2$ by martingale convergence, and
$\E[F_NG_N]\to\E[FG]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The pace of survivors; {[P]}]\label{thm:pace}
In the two-channel model let the rates be the generic ones,
$\mu=\mu_0+\sigma W$ and $\eta_j=\eta_{j,0}+s_jW^j$ with $s_j>0$, the
Brownian motions $W,W^1,\dots,W^m$ independent of one another and of the
background, which is that of Definition~\ref{def:twochannel} or is frozen
at a constant $B>0$. Write $\ell_j=\log(\tau_j/x)$ for a unit's lead on
$\{x>0\}$, and
\[
H_t:=\{T>t,\ x_t\ge1\}
\]
for the histories that survive to $t$ and hold strength there. Fix a
unit $j$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{The filtering only raises a technoanatomy.} For every $t$,
every bounded nondecreasing functional $F$ of the path of $\eta_j$ on a
bounded interval, and every event $A$ determined by the driver, the
background and the other units' rates with $\PP(T>t,\,A)>0$,
\[
\E\bigl[F\bigm|T>t,\ A\bigr]\;\ge\;\E[F].
\]
Given survival to $t$, or given $H_t$, the law of the path of the anatomy
rate, and with it that of $\log\tau_j$, dominates its unconditioned law
stochastically, at every horizon.
\item \emph{What holding strength costs, and where the time goes.} There
is $c>0$ with $\PP(H_t)\ge c\,t^{-(1+m)/2}$ for $t\ge1$. Consequently, for
$0<\delta<\tfrac12$, the time spent holding strength with a lead no
longer than $(\tfrac12-\delta)\log t$,
\[
O_t:=\Leb\bigl\{s\le t:\ x_s>0,\ \ell_{j,s}\le(\tfrac12-\delta)\log t\bigr\},
\]
satisfies
\[
\PP\bigl(O_t>t^{1-\delta}\bigm|T>t\bigr)\;\le\;
c^{-1}\,t^{(1+m)/2}\exp\bigl(-\Gamma_jt^{\delta}\bigr)\;\longrightarrow\;0,
\]
and the same given $H_t$.
\item \emph{The lead at the horizon.} There are $C<\infty$ and $t_0$ such
that, for $t\ge t_0$ and every $w\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pace}
\PP\bigl(\ell_{j,t}\le w\bigm|H_t\bigr)\;\le\;C\,(w+\log\log t)\,t^{-3/2}.
\end{equation}
The scalar stands at or below the strength with conditional probability
$O(t^{-3/2}\log\log t)$, and for every $\varepsilon>0$
\[
\PP\bigl(t^{3/2-\varepsilon}\le\ell_{j,t}\le t^{3/2+\varepsilon}\bigm|H_t\bigr)\;\longrightarrow\;1:
\]
the lead of a survivor holding strength is of order $t^{3/2}$ to within
any power of $t$, and its load is then at most
$\exp(-t^{3/2-\varepsilon})$.
\item \emph{Without the filtering.} For any $w_t=o(t^{3/2})$,
$\PP(\ell_{j,t}\le w_t\mid x_t\ge1)\to\tfrac12$, and
$\E[O_t]/t\to\tfrac14$.
\item \emph{The strength of survivors, at frozen background.} If
$B_t\equiv B$, there are $C'<\infty$ and $t_0'$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pacestrength}
\PP\bigl(x_t<1\bigm|T>t\bigr)\;\le\;C'\,t^{-1}\log^3t\;
\frac{\PP(T>t/2)}{\PP(T>t)},\qquad t\ge t_0'.
\end{equation}
By (ii) the ratio on the right is at most $4^{1+m}$ at more than half of
the horizons $t=2^k$, $k\le K$, once $K$ is large, and it is bounded at
every horizon if \eqref{eq:addexp} holds. Where it is bounded, survivors
hold strength with probability $1-O(t^{-1}\log^3t)$, and \eqref{eq:pace}
holds given survival alone, with a further term of that order.
\end{enumerate}
The statements hold with the same proof in the lead form of
Corollary~\ref{cor:lead} with driftless Brownian lead rates, the lead
rate in place of the anatomy rate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal G$ be the $\sigma$-field of the driver, the background and
the rates of the units other than $j$; the path of $\eta_j$ is
independent of it. Given all the paths, survival to $t$ has probability
$e^{-\Lambda_t}=R_t\,U_{0,t}$, with
\begin{gather*}
R_t:=\exp\Bigl(-\int_0^t\Bigl[B_s\operatorname{sech}^2x_s^+
+\sum_{i\neq j}\Gamma_i\sinh^2\frac{x_s^+}{\tau_{i,s}}\Bigr]ds\Bigr),\\
U_{a,b}:=\exp\Bigl(-\Gamma_j\int_a^b\sinh^2\bigl(x_s^+e^{-y_{j,s}}\bigr)\,ds\Bigr),
\end{gather*}
and $R_t$ is $\mathcal G$-measurable. For a fixed path of $x$ each
$U_{a,b}$ is a nondecreasing functional of the path of $\eta_j$: raising
$\eta_j$ raises $y_j$ and lowers the load.

(i) By Lemma~\ref{lem:assoc} applied given $\mathcal G$,
$\E[F\,U_{0,t}\mid\mathcal G]\ge\E[F]\,\E[U_{0,t}\mid\mathcal G]$.
Multiplying by $R_t\one_A$ and taking expectations,
$\E[F;\,T>t,\,A]\ge\E[F]\,\PP(T>t,\,A)$.

(ii) Let $E$ be the event that, for constants $X$, $A$, $Y$, $Y'$ chosen
below, (a) $\sup_{s\le1}|x_s|\le X$, $x_1\ge1$, $\mu_1\in[2,3]$, and for
every unit $i$, $\inf_{s\le1}y_{i,s}\ge-Y$, $y_{i,1}\ge Y'$ and
$\eta_{i,1}\ge4$; (b) $1\le\mu_u\le A+u$ for $1\le u\le t$; (c)
$\eta_{i,u}\ge3$ for $1\le u\le t$ and every unit $i$; (d)
$W^{(i)}_u\le1+u/\epsilon$ for every $u\ge0$ and $i\le n$. On $E$, for
$1\le s\le t$,
\[
s\;\le\;x_s\;\le\;X+As+\tfrac12s^2,\qquad y_{i,s}\;\ge\;Y'+3(s-1).
\]
By (d), $B_s\le ne^{\epsilon+s}$, so that the external term is at most
$ne^{\epsilon+1}$ before time one and, as
$\operatorname{sech}^2x\le4e^{-2x}$, at most $4ne^{\epsilon-s}$ after it;
for a frozen background (d) is void and $B$ stands in place of
$ne^{\epsilon+s}$. With
$Y':=\log\sup_{s\ge1}(X+As+\tfrac12s^2)e^{-(s-1)}$ every load is at most
$e^{-2(s-1)}$ after time one, and at most $Xe^{Y}$ before it. As
$\sinh^2u\le2u^2$ for $u\le1$, on $E$
\[
\Lambda_t\;\le\;C_\Lambda:=ne^{\epsilon+1}+4ne^{\epsilon}
+\sum_i\Gamma_i\bigl(\sinh^2(Xe^{Y})+\tfrac12\bigr)
\]
and $x_t\ge1$, so that $\PP(H_t)\ge e^{-C_\Lambda}\,\PP(E)$. The event (d)
has probability $(1-e^{-2/\epsilon})^n$, $\sup_u(W_u-u/\epsilon)$ being
exponential of rate $2/\epsilon$, and is independent of the rest. Given
$\Fc_1$, the event (b) and the $m$ events of (c) are independent. By
reflection each of the latter has probability at least
$\operatorname{erf}(1/s_i\sqrt{2t})$, and $\mu$ stays above one through
$t$ with probability at least $\operatorname{erf}(1/\sigma\sqrt{2t})$.
Let $\varrho$ be the first time after one at which $\mu_u=A+u$. From
$\mu_1\in[2,3]$ the line starts $a\in[A-2,A-1]$ above the rate, so that
$\PP(\varrho<\infty\mid\Fc_1)=e^{-2a/\sigma^2}$ and
$\E[\varrho-1;\,\varrho<\infty\mid\Fc_1]=a\,e^{-2a/\sigma^2}$; and by the
strong Markov property at $\varrho$ and reflection again, the rate stays
above one from $\varrho$ through $t$ with probability at most
$(2/\pi)^{1/2}(A+\varrho-1)/\sigma\sqrt{t-\varrho}$. Splitting at
$\varrho=t/2$,
\[
\PP\bigl(\text{(b)}\bigm|\Fc_1\bigr)\;\ge\;
\operatorname{erf}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2t}}\Bigr)
-\frac{4A}{\sigma\sqrt{\pi t}}\,e^{-2(A-2)/\sigma^2}
-e^{-(t-2)/4\sigma^2},
\]
the last term bounding $\PP(t/2<\varrho<\infty\mid\Fc_1)$. Since
$\operatorname{erf}z\ge\operatorname{erf}(1)\,z$ on $[0,1]$, a choice of
$A$ with $(4A/\sqrt\pi)\,e^{-2(A-2)/\sigma^2}\le\tfrac14$ leaves (b) a
conditional probability at least $c_1t^{-1/2}$ for $t\ge t_1$, and for
$t<t_1$ at least its value at $t_1$, the event decreasing in $t$.
Finally (a) contains a neighbourhood, in the supremum norm, of a path of
$(W,W^1,\dots,W^m)$ on $[0,1]$, the constants being chosen in this order:
a path of $W$ carrying $\mu$ into $(2,3)$ and $x$ above one at time one,
which fixes $X$; then $A$, which depends on $\sigma$ alone; then $Y'$;
then paths of the $W^i$ carrying $\eta_i$ above $4$ and $y_i$ above $Y'$
at time one, which fixes $Y$. Wiener measure charges every such
neighbourhood. Hence $\PP(E)\ge c'\,t^{-(1+m)/2}$ with $c'>0$, and the
bound holds with $c=c'e^{-C_\Lambda}$. For the occupation time, where
$x_s>0$ and $\ell_{j,s}\le L$ the unit's hazard is at least
$\Gamma_j\sinh^2(e^{-L})\ge\Gamma_je^{-2L}$; with
$L=(\tfrac12-\delta)\log t$ this gives
$\Lambda_t\ge\Gamma_jt^{-1+2\delta}O_t$ and
$\PP(T>t,\,O_t>t^{1-\delta})\le\exp(-\Gamma_jt^{\delta})$, to be divided
by $\PP(T>t)\ge\PP(H_t)$.

(iii) Put $M_t:=\log(6\sqrt e\,\log t)$, and let $Q_t$ be the event that
$|\mu|$ and $|\eta_j|$ stay below $t$ on $[0,t]$, so that
$\PP(Q_t^c)\le8e^{-t/4\bar\sigma^2}$ for large $t$, with
$\bar\sigma=\max(\sigma,s_j)$. \emph{Deep leads.} On
$Q_t\cap\{x_t\ge1,\ \ell_{j,t}<-M_t\}$, for $t-\tfrac1{2t}\le s\le t$ the
credit and $y_j$ have each moved by at most $\tfrac12$, so that
$x_s\ge\tfrac12x_t$ and the load $x_se^{-y_{j,s}}$ exceeds
$e^{M_t}/2\sqrt e=3\log t$. As $\sinh^2z\ge\tfrac18e^{2z}$ for $z\ge1$,
$\Lambda_t\ge\Gamma_jt^5/16$ there, and
\[
\PP\bigl(T>t,\ x_t\ge1,\ \ell_{j,t}<-M_t\bigr)\;\le\;e^{-\Gamma_jt^5/16}+\PP(Q_t^c).
\]
\emph{The band.} Given $\mathcal G$ and the path of $\eta_j$ on
$[0,t/2]$, $y_{j,t}$ is Gaussian of variance $v_t=s_j^2t^3/24$, and on
$\{x_t\ge1\}$ the event $\{-M_t\le\ell_{j,t}\le w\}$ asks it to fall in an
interval of length $w+M_t$ whose ends are $\mathcal G$-measurable, which
has conditional probability at most $(w+M_t)(2\pi v_t)^{-1/2}$. Since
$U_{0,t}\le U_{0,t/2}$,
\[
\PP\bigl(T>t,\ x_t\ge1,\ -M_t\le\ell_{j,t}\le w\bigr)\;\le\;
\frac{w+M_t}{\sqrt{2\pi v_t}}\,K_t,\qquad
K_t:=\E\bigl[R_t\one_{\{x_t\ge1\}}\,\E[U_{0,t/2}\mid\mathcal G]\bigr].
\]
\emph{The doubling.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:assoc} applied given $\mathcal G$,
$\E[U_{0,t}\mid\mathcal G]\ge\E[U_{0,t/2}\mid\mathcal G]\,
\E[U_{t/2,t}\mid\mathcal G]$. Where $|\mu|\le t$ on $[0,t]$,
$\sup_{s\le t}x_s\le|x_0|+t^2\le e^t$ for large $t$; if moreover
$y_{j,s}\ge2t$ on $[t/2,t]$, the load there is at most $e^{-t}$ and
$U_{t/2,t}\ge e^{-\Gamma_j}$. By Brownian scaling,
$y_{j,rt}=y_{j,0}+\eta_{j,0}rt+s_jt^{3/2}\int_0^r\widetilde W_u\,du$ for a
standard Brownian motion $\widetilde W$, and
$\PP(\inf_{t/2\le s\le t}y_{j,s}\ge2t)$ has limit inferior at least
\[
p_*:=\PP\Bigl(\inf_{1/2\le r\le1}\int_0^r\widetilde W_u\,du>0\Bigr)>0.
\]
Hence, for large $t$,
\[
\PP(H_t)=\E\bigl[R_t\one_{\{x_t\ge1\}}\,\E[U_{0,t}\mid\mathcal G]\bigr]
\;\ge\;\tfrac12p_*e^{-\Gamma_j}\Bigl(K_t-\PP\bigl(\sup_{s\le t}|\mu_s|>t\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
and as $K_t\ge\PP(H_t)\ge ct^{-(1+m)/2}$ while the probability subtracted
is exponentially small, $\PP(H_t)\ge\tfrac14p_*e^{-\Gamma_j}K_t$. Adding
the deep leads to the band and dividing by $\PP(H_t)$ gives
\eqref{eq:pace}, $M_t$ being at most $2\log\log t$ and the deep term, over
$\PP(H_t)$, smaller than any power of $t$. For the upper side, on
$\{x_t\ge1\}$ the lead is at most $y_{j,t}$, a Gaussian of variance
$s_j^2t^3/3$, so that
$\PP(\ell_{j,t}\ge t^{3/2+\varepsilon},\ x_t\ge1)\le e^{-c''t^{2\varepsilon}}$
for a $c''>0$, which is negligible against $\PP(H_t)$.

(iv) Unconditioned, $t^{-3/2}y_{j,t}$ is Gaussian with mean $O(t^{-1/2})$
and variance $s_j^2/3$, independent of $x$, while $t^{-3/2}\log x_t\to0$
in probability on $\{x_t\ge1\}$, an event of probability tending to
$\tfrac12$; so $\PP(y_{j,t}\le\log x_t+w_t\mid x_t\ge1)\to\tfrac12$. In the
same way, for each $r\in(0,1]$,
$\PP(x_{rt}>0,\ \ell_{j,rt}\le(\tfrac12-\delta)\log t)\to\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12$,
and $\E[O_t]/t$ is the integral of this probability over $r$.

(v) Let $B_t\equiv B$ and $M'_t:=B^{-1}\sqrt t\,\log^3t$. Where $|\mu|$
stays below $\sqrt t\log t$ on $[0,t]$, an event whose complement has
probability at most $4e^{-\log^2t/4\sigma^2}$ for large $t$, a credit
below $-M'_t$ at $t$ has been negative for a time $M'_t/\sqrt t\log t$,
during which the hazard is $B$; so
$\PP(T>t,\ x_t<-M'_t)\le e^{-\log^2t}+4e^{-\log^2t/4\sigma^2}$. Given
$\Fc_{t/2}$, $x_t$ is Gaussian of variance $\sigma^2t^3/24$, so that
$\PP(-M'_t\le x_t<1\mid\Fc_{t/2})\le(1+M'_t)(12/\pi\sigma^2t^3)^{1/2}$, and
as $e^{-\Lambda_t}\le e^{-\Lambda_{t/2}}$,
\[
\PP\bigl(T>t,\ -M'_t\le x_t<1\bigr)\;\le\;
(1+M'_t)\Bigl(\frac{12}{\pi\sigma^2t^3}\Bigr)^{1/2}\PP(T>t/2).
\]
Dividing by $\PP(T>t)\ge ct^{-(1+m)/2}$ gives \eqref{eq:pacestrength}.
Were the ratio $\PP(T>2^{k-1})/\PP(T>2^k)$, which is at least one, to
exceed $4^{1+m}$ at half of the $k\le K$, the product of the ratios over
$k\le K$, which is $\PP(T>1)/\PP(T>2^K)$, would exceed $2^{(1+m)K}$,
against (ii) once $K$ is large.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The credit of survivors, with no Ansatz; {[P]}]\label{cor:strengthscale}
Consider the driftless model of Definition~\ref{def:model}, with credit
$D$, or the two-channel model with the external channel alone at frozen
background, $m=0$ and $B_t\equiv B$, with credit $x$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item Survival doubles at bounded cost: there are $c_0>0$ and $t_0$ with
$\PP(T>2t)\ge c_0\,\PP(T>t)$ for $t\ge t_0$. Hence
$\PP(T>t)\ge c\,t^{-\kappa}$ with $\kappa=\log_2(1/c_0)$, and the hazard of
survivors, $-\frac{d}{dt}\log\PP(T>t)$, integrates to at most
$\log(1/c_0)$ over every interval $[t,2t]$.
\item There are $C<\infty$ and $t_0$ with, for $t\ge t_0$ and $w\ge0$,
\[
\PP\bigl(D_t\le w\bigm|T>t\bigr)\;\le\;C\,(w+\log t)\,t^{-3/2}
\]
in the driftless model, and the same for $x_t$ with $\sqrt t\log^3t$ in
place of $\log t$ in the other. The credit of a survivor is nonpositive
with probability $O(t^{-3/2}\log t)$, respectively $O(t^{-1}\log^3t)$, and
for every $\varepsilon>0$ it lies between $t^{3/2-\varepsilon}$ and
$t^{3/2+\varepsilon}$ with conditional probability tending to one.
\end{enumerate}
Neither item uses Ansatz~\ref{ans:soft} or Ansatz~\ref{ans:ratio}. The
scale $t^{3/2}$ of a survivor's credit, and a hazard of survivors of order
at most $1/t$ on average over each doubling of the horizon, are thereby
proved; the exponent $\tfrac14$ and the limit law of
Section~\ref{sec:cond} remain under their hypotheses.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For the driftless model take the hazard to be $e^{-D}$, a constant factor
being a shift of the credit. In both models the hazard is nonincreasing in
the credit, so that $U_{a,b}:=\exp(-\int_a^bh_s\,ds)$ is a nondecreasing
functional of the path of $\mu$, and $e^{-\Lambda_t}=U_{0,t}$. (i) By
Lemma~\ref{lem:assoc},
$\PP(T>2t)=\E[U_{0,t}U_{t,2t}]\ge\PP(T>t)\,\E[U_{t,2t}]$. Where the credit
stays above $\log t$ on $[t,2t]$, $\int_t^{2t}e^{-D_s}ds\le1$ and
$\int_t^{2t}B\operatorname{sech}^2x_s\,ds\le4B/t$; and by the scaling of
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pace}(iii) that event has probability with
limit inferior at least $p_*$. Iterating the doubling from $t_0$ gives the
polynomial bound, and $\log(\PP(T>t)/\PP(T>2t))$ is the integral over
$[t,2t]$ of the hazard of survivors. (ii) In the driftless model, where
$|\mu|$ stays below $t$ on $[0,t]$ a credit below $-1-2\log t$ at $t$ has
stood below $-2\log t$ since $t-1/t$, the hazard there exceeding $t^2$; so
$\PP(T>t,\ D_t<-1-2\log t)\le e^{-t}+4e^{-t/4\sigma^2}$ for large $t$.
Given $\Fc_{t/2}$, $D_t$ is Gaussian of variance $\sigma^2t^3/24$, whence
\[
\PP\bigl(T>t,\ -1-2\log t\le D_t\le w\bigr)\;\le\;
(w+1+2\log t)\Bigl(\frac{12}{\pi\sigma^2t^3}\Bigr)^{1/2}\PP(T>t/2),
\]
and $\PP(T>t/2)\le c_0^{-1}\PP(T>t)$ by (i). For the external channel the
deep estimate is that of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pace}(v). The upper
side is the Gaussian tail of the credit at $t$ against the polynomial
bound of (i).
\end{proof}

\section{Domains: the techno-system as a product}\label{sec:domains}

The two-channel model loads every unit with one strength. Here each unit
has a strength of its own, so that each advantage is balanced by its own
technoanatomy and each takes its part in the muffling; the system is then
a product over its domains, exactly in the internal channel and up to one
symmetric factor in the external one.

\begin{definition}[The model with domains]\label{def:domains}
A \emph{domain} $j\in\{1,\dots,m\}$ carries a credit
$x_{j,t}=x_{j,0}+\int_0^t\mu_{j,s}\,ds$ with a rate $\mu_j$ of its own, a
strength $x_j^+$, and a scalar $\tau_j=e^{y_j}$ moved by an anatomy rate
$\eta_j$ as in \eqref{eq:scalar}. The \emph{total strength} is
$X=\sum_jx_j^+$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domains}
h=B_t\operatorname{sech}^2X+\sum_{j=1}^m\Gamma_j\sinh^2\frac{x_j^+}{\tau_j}.
\end{equation}
A domain's load is $x_j^+/\tau_j$ and its lead
$\ell_j=\log(\tau_j/x_j^+)$ on $\{x_j>0\}$. A domain with $x_j\ge1$ is
\emph{developed}; one with $x_j\le0$ is \emph{idle}, its unit vanishing
and its scalar bearing on nothing. Definition~\ref{def:twochannel} is the
case of fixed shares, $x_j=s_jx$ with $s_j>0$ and $\sum_js_j=1$, its
scalars being $\tau_j/s_j$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The muffling of a sum; {[P]}]\label{prop:addition}
For real $x_1,\dots,x_m$ and $t_j=\tanh x_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:addition}
\operatorname{sech}^2\Bigl(\sum_jx_j\Bigr)=\frac{\prod_j\operatorname{sech}^2x_j}{E_m^2},
\qquad
E_m=\tfrac12\Bigl[\prod_j(1+t_j)+\prod_j(1-t_j)\Bigr]
=\sum_{|S|\ \mathrm{even}}\ \prod_{j\in S}t_j,
\end{equation}
the sum being over the subsets $S$ of $\{1,\dots,m\}$ of even size. For
$x_j\ge0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item $1\le E_m\le2^{m-1}$, with $E_m=1$ exactly when at most one $x_j$ is
positive and $E_m\to2^{m-1}$ as every $x_j\to\infty$; a domain at zero
strength drops out of both sides.
\item $4^{1-m}\prod_j\operatorname{sech}^2x_j^+\le\operatorname{sech}^2X\le\prod_j\operatorname{sech}^2x_j^+$:
the external channel of \eqref{eq:domains} is the product of the domains'
mufflings divided by a factor that is one while a single domain is
developed and tends to $4^{m-1}$ when all are, where it is
$4\prod_j(\tfrac14\operatorname{sech}^2x_j)$.
\item The product without the divisor is not stable under refinement: a
total $x$ held in $m$ equal parts gives
$\operatorname{sech}^{2m}(x/m)\ge e^{-x^2/m}\to1$ as $m\to\infty$, while
\eqref{eq:domains} depends on the parts only through their sum.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\cosh(\sum_jx_j)=\tfrac12(\prod_je^{x_j}+\prod_je^{-x_j})$ and
$e^{\pm x_j}=\cosh x_j\,(1\pm t_j)$; in the expansion of the two products
the terms of odd degree cancel. (i) Each of the $2^{m-1}$ even products
lies in $[0,1]$, the empty one is $1$, and a nonempty one is positive
only if two of the $t_j$ are. (ii) is (i) in \eqref{eq:addition}. (iii)
$\log\cosh v\le\tfrac12v^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Viability with domains; {[P]}]\label{thm:domains}
In the model with domains let the $2m$ rates be independent of one
another and of the background.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item $\Lambda_\infty<\infty$ exactly when
$\int_0^\infty B_t\operatorname{sech}^2X_t\,dt<\infty$ and
$\int_0^\infty\sinh^2(x_{j,t}^+/\tau_{j,t})\,dt<\infty$ for every domain.
The first holds where the total strength grows at least linearly. The
second holds for a domain whose credit tends to $-\infty$, whatever its
scalar does; for a domain whose credit grows at least linearly it is the
condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:twocond}(i) on that domain's unit. A
bounded scalar is fatal in a domain whose credit grows at least linearly
and harmless in one whose credit tends to $-\infty$.
\item Let every rate be transient, or positive recurrent with nonzero
stationary mean, with bounded drift and noise coefficient, and write
$V_j=V_{\mathrm{drv}}(\mu_{j,0})$ and $A_j=V_{\mathrm{anat}}(\eta_{j,0})$
for the functionals \eqref{eq:vdrv} of the two rates of domain $j$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domainsprod}
\PP\bigl(\Lambda_\infty<\infty\bigr)
=\prod_{j=1}^m\bigl(1-V_j+V_jA_j\bigr)-\prod_{j=1}^m\bigl(1-V_j\bigr).
\end{equation}
Each domain is left idle, or is developed with its technoanatomy keeping
ahead, and one at least is developed. For $m=1$ this is
\eqref{eq:twocond}.
\item If the rate of every domain is the driftless Brownian rate,
$\Lambda_\infty=\infty$ almost surely, whatever the anatomy rates.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The terms of \eqref{eq:domains} are nonnegative, so $\Lambda_\infty$
is finite exactly when each integral is. Where $X_t\ge ct$ eventually the
external term is at most $4B_te^{-2ct}$, integrable as in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:twocond}(i). A domain whose credit tends to $-\infty$ has
$x_j^+=0$ from some time on, and before it the integrand is continuous on
a bounded interval. If $x_{j,t}\ge ct$ eventually and
$\tau_j\le\bar\tau$, the unit's hazard is eventually at least
$\Gamma_j\sinh^2(1/\bar\tau)$. (ii) Under the hypothesis each credit grows
at least linearly, on an event $G_j$ of probability $V_j$, or tends to
$-\infty$, and these events are independent. On $\bigcap_jG_j^c$ the total
strength is eventually zero, the hazard is $B_t$ from then on, and
$\Lambda_\infty=\infty$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:infbackground}. On the
complement the total strength grows at least linearly and by (i)
viability is the integrability of the units of the domains in $G_j$. For
such a domain $|x_{j,t}|=O(t^2)$ as in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:twocond}(ii); on the escape of its anatomy rate upward,
or with a positive stationary mean, $y_{j,t}\ge ct$ eventually and
$x_j^2e^{-2y_j}$ is integrable; otherwise $y_{j,t}\to-\infty$, the scalar
is bounded, and the unit is fatal by (i). A history is therefore viable
exactly when some domain is in $G_j$ and every domain in $G_j$ has its
anatomy rate escape upward: the event that each domain is outside $G_j$
or is in it with the escape, of probability the first product, less the
event that every domain is outside, of probability the second. (iii) The
processes $(x_{j,e^s}-x_{j,0})/\sigma_je^{3s/2}$ are independent, and each
is, up to a term that tends to zero, the stationary Gaussian process of
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:half}, whose covariance tends to zero; the
vector of them is mixing, and Birkhoff's theorem gives the set of times
at which every credit is below its initial value the logarithmic density
$2^{-m}$. On that set, of infinite measure and independent of the
background, $X\le\sum_jx_{j,0}^+$ and the external term is at least
$B_t\operatorname{sech}^2(\sum_jx_{j,0}^+)$;
Lemma~\ref{lem:infbackground} concludes.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The pace, domain by domain; {[P]}]\label{thm:pacedomains}
In the model with domains let every rate be driftless Brownian,
$\mu_j=\mu_{j,0}+\sigma_jW^j$ and $\eta_j=\eta_{j,0}+s_j\widetilde W^j$
with $\sigma_j,s_j>0$, the $2m$ Brownian motions independent of one
another and of the background, which is as in Theorem~\ref{thm:pace}.
Write $H^j_t:=\{T>t,\ x_{j,t}\ge1\}$ for the histories that survive to $t$
with domain $j$ developed. The statements of Theorem~\ref{thm:pace} hold
for each domain, with $x_j$ in place of $x$ and $H^j_t$ in place of $H_t$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item $\E[F\mid T>t,\,A]\ge\E[F]$ for $F$ bounded and nondecreasing in the
path of $\eta_j$ and $A$ determined by the background and the other
$2m-1$ rates; and the same for $F$ bounded and nondecreasing in the paths
of all the anatomy rates together and $A$ determined by the background
and the domains' rates. The filtering raises the technoanatomies jointly.
\item $\PP(H^j_t)\ge c\,t^{-m}$ for $t\ge1$, and the occupation bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:pace}(ii) holds with $c^{-1}t^{m}$ in place of
$c^{-1}t^{(1+m)/2}$.
\item $\PP(\ell_{j,t}\le w\mid H^j_t)\le C\,(w+\log\log t)\,t^{-3/2}$ for
$t\ge t_0$ and $w\ge0$, with the consequences drawn there.
\item Without the filtering the limits of Theorem~\ref{thm:pace}(iv)
hold.
\item At frozen background,
$\PP(X_t<1\mid T>t)\le C'\,t^{-1}\log^3t\;\PP(T>t/2)/\PP(T>t)$, the ratio
being at most $4^{2m}$ at more than half of the horizons $2^k$, $k\le K$,
once $K$ is large.
\end{enumerate}
An idle domain is under no such constraint: given survival to $t$ and
that $x_{j,s}\le0$ for all $s\le t$, the path of $\eta_j$ keeps its
unconditioned law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:assoc} holds, with the same proof, for bounded functionals
of finitely many independent Brownian paths that are nondecreasing in
each of them, the dyadic increments of all the paths together being
independent variables. The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pace} then applies
with these changes. $\mathcal G$ is the $\sigma$-field of the background
and of all the rates other than $\eta_j$; $R_t$ collects the external term
and the other domains' units; and
$U_{a,b}=\exp(-\Gamma_j\int_a^b\sinh^2(x_{j,s}^+e^{-y_{j,s}})\,ds)$. (i) As
there; for the joint statement, given the background and the domains'
rates the survival weight is a product over the domains of factors each
nondecreasing in the path of its own anatomy rate and constant in the
others. (ii) In the event $E$ the conditions of (a) and (b) on $\mu$ are
imposed on every $\mu_i$, with the same constants. Then $x_{i,s}\ge s$ for
every $i$ and $X_s\ge s$ after time one, the bounds on $\Lambda_t$ hold,
and (b) costs a factor $c_1t^{-1/2}$ for each of the $m$ domains' rates as
(c) does for each of the $m$ anatomy rates. (iii) and (iv) Verbatim, with
$x_j$ for $x$ and $Q_t$ taken for $\mu_j$ and $\eta_j$. (v) On
$\{X_t<1\}$ every credit is below one. Either every credit is below
$-M'_t$, and where every $|\mu_i|$ has stayed below $\sqrt t\log t$ all
the credits have then been negative for a time $M'_t/\sqrt t\log t$,
during which the hazard is $B$; or some credit lies in $[-M'_t,1)$, which
given $\Fc_{t/2}$ has probability at most
$m(1+M'_t)(12/\pi\sigma_\ast^2t^3)^{1/2}$, $\sigma_\ast$ the least of the
$\sigma_i$. The ratios are counted as there, with the bound of (ii). For
the last statement, where $x_{j,s}\le0$ for all $s\le t$ the factor
$U_{0,t}$ is one; that event and $R_t$ are $\mathcal G$-measurable and
$\eta_j$ is independent of $\mathcal G$, so that
$\E[F;\,T>t,\ x_{j,s}\le0\ \forall s\le t]=\E[F]\,\PP(T>t,\ x_{j,s}\le0\ \forall s\le t)$
for every bounded functional $F$ of the path of $\eta_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Institutions as labels]\label{def:labels}
Let the system have a \emph{content}: a measure space $(\Omega,\nu)$ of
finite mass. An \emph{institution} is a label, a measurable part
$A_j\subset\Omega$ of mass $m_j=\nu(A_j)>0$, $j=1,\dots,m$. Labels may
overlap: $M_{jk}=\nu(A_j\cap A_k)$, and $\rho_{jk}=M_{jk}/m_j$ is the
share of $j$'s content that $k$ also reads, $r_j=\sum_{k\neq j}\rho_{jk}$
the \emph{total overlap} of $j$. The \emph{reading} by $j$ of a density
$f\in L^2(\nu)$ is $F_j=\int_{A_j}f\,d\nu$. The labels \emph{resolve} the
functions in the span $\mathcal S$ of the indicators $\one_{A_j}$, and
their \emph{resolution} is the distance
$\varepsilon_{\mathcal S}=\operatorname{dist}_{L^2(\nu)}(1,\mathcal S)$
from the constant to that span.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The weights of a redundant decomposition; {[P]}]\label{prop:weights}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item If $\bar r:=\max_jr_j<1$, the equations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
w_j=1-\sum_{k\neq j}\rho_{jk}\,w_k,\qquad j=1,\dots,m,
\end{equation}
have a unique solution. It lies in $[1-\bar r,1]^m$; it is the limit of
the iterates started from $w=1$, the case of labels that do not overlap,
with error at most $\bar r^{\,n+1}$ after $n$ steps; and it is the
alternating series $\sum_{n\ge0}(-R)^n\mathbf 1$, $R=(\rho_{jk})_{j\neq k}$:
the first step damps each label by its total overlap, and each later one
corrects the step before.
\item These weights minimize $\|\sum_jw_j\one_{A_j}-1\|_{L^2(\nu)}$, the
minimum being $\varepsilon_{\mathcal S}$, and for every density $f$
\[
\Bigl|\sum_jw_jF_j-\int f\,d\nu\Bigr|\;\le\;
\varepsilon_{\mathcal S}\,\operatorname{dist}_{L^2(\nu)}(f,\mathcal S).
\]
A density that the labels resolve is integrated exactly by its weighted
readings, and every density is when $\varepsilon_{\mathcal S}=0$, that is,
when the labels admit a partition of unity.
\item No decomposition is preferred: two families of labels with the same
span, each with the weights solving its own normal equations, give the
same weighted sum of readings for every density.
\item Where a total overlap reaches one the weights may leave $(0,1]$:
for two labels and a third that is their common part,
$1=\one_{A_1}+\one_{A_2}-\one_{A_1\cap A_2}$ on $A_1\cup A_2$, and the
third weight is $-1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\Phi(w)=\mathbf 1-Rw$ maps $[0,1]^m$ into $[1-\bar r,1]^m$, $R$ having
nonnegative entries and row sums at most $\bar r$, and
$\|\Phi(w)-\Phi(w')\|_\infty\le\bar r\,\|w-w'\|_\infty$. The fixed point is
unique, $\|\mathbf 1-w\|_\infty=\|Rw\|_\infty\le\bar r$, and the iterates
of $\Phi$ from $\mathbf 1$ are the partial sums of the series. (ii)
Multiplied by $m_j$, \eqref{eq:weights} reads $Mw=(m_j)_j$, the normal
equations of the least-squares problem, $M$ being the Gram matrix of the
indicators. The residual $g=\sum_jw_j\one_{A_j}-1$ is orthogonal to
$\mathcal S$ and has norm $\varepsilon_{\mathcal S}$, and
$\sum_jw_jF_j-\int f\,d\nu=\langle g,f\rangle=\langle g,f-\pi f\rangle$,
$\pi$ the orthogonal projection on $\mathcal S$. (iii) In either family
the weighted sum of readings is $\langle\pi1,f\rangle$. (iv) By
inspection.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The hazard as an integral; the amplitudes as weights]\label{def:quadrature}
Let the content carry an amplitude density $\gamma\ge0$ and a load
$u\ge0$, the internal hazard being $\int\gamma\sinh^2u\,d\nu$. Institution
$j$ reads the amplitude $\hat\Gamma_j=\int_{A_j}\gamma\,d\nu$ and the load
$u_j$ given by
$\hat\Gamma_j\sinh^2u_j=\int_{A_j}\gamma\sinh^2u\,d\nu$, the mean of the
load over its content with generator $\sinh^2$. The decomposition into
the institutions is the quadrature
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadrature}
\int\gamma\sinh^2u\,d\nu\;\approx\;\sum_j\Gamma_j\sinh^2u_j,
\qquad\Gamma_j:=w_j\,\hat\Gamma_j,
\end{equation}
with the weights of \eqref{eq:weights}; by
Proposition~\ref{prop:weights}(ii) its error is at most
$\varepsilon_{\mathcal S}\operatorname{dist}(\gamma\sinh^2u,\mathcal S)$.
In the same way a strength density $\xi\ge0$ is read as
$x_j^+=\int_{A_j}\xi\,d\nu$, and the external channel is taken at the
total $\sum_jw_jx_j^+$. With labels that do not overlap every weight is
one and \eqref{eq:domains} is recovered; for a label read twice the
equations determine only the sum of its two weights, which is one.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Listening; {[P]}]\label{prop:listening}
Let $P$ be an $m\times m$ matrix with nonnegative entries and rows
summing to one, and $0\le\kappa<1$. Let each scalar read its own content
$b_j$ and the others' readings, in the shares $1-\kappa$ and $\kappa$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:listening}
y_j=(1-\kappa)\,b_j+\kappa\sum_kP_{jk}\,y_k,\qquad y_j=\log\tau_j.
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item $y=Kb$ with
$K=(1-\kappa)(I-\kappa P)^{-1}=(1-\kappa)\sum_{n\ge0}\kappa^nP^n$. $K$ has
nonnegative entries, rows summing to one, and $K_{jj}\ge1-\kappa$: each
scalar $\tau_j=\prod_k\hat\tau_k^{K_{jk}}$ is a weighted geometric mean of
the contents $\hat\tau_k=e^{b_k}$, and $K_{jk}/(1-\kappa)$ is the expected
number of visits to $k$ of the walk with transition matrix $P$ started at
$j$ and stopped with probability $1-\kappa$ at each step.
\item $\min_kb_k\le y_j\le\max_kb_k$. No scalar exceeds the greatest
content, and with all contents equal every reading equals them.
\item $y_j\ge K_{jj}b_j+(1-K_{jj})\min_{k\neq j}b_k$: as an institution's
own content falls with the others held, its scalar falls only as the
power $K_{jj}$ of it.
\item Let the contents move by independent driftless Brownian rates,
independent of the background and of the domains' rates. Then every
anatomy rate is a combination of them with nonnegative coefficients, any
two anatomy rates are nonnegatively correlated, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:pacedomains}(i)--(iv) hold, with the functionals of (i)
nondecreasing in the paths of the contents' rates and the constant of
(iii) divided by $K_{jj}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\kappa P$ has row sums $\kappa<1$, so the series converges and inverts
$I-\kappa P$; its terms are nonnegative, the rows of $P^n$ sum to one and
$(1-\kappa)\sum_n\kappa^n=1$; the term $n=0$ gives $K_{jj}\ge1-\kappa$; and
$(P^n)_{jk}$ is the probability that the walk is at $k$ after $n$ steps.
(ii) and (iii) Each $y_j$ is an average of the $b_k$ with weights
$K_{jk}$. (iv) $\eta=K\beta$ for the contents' rates $\beta$. Take
$\mathcal G$ to be the $\sigma$-field of the background and the domains'
rates. Given $\mathcal G$ the survival weight is a product over the
domains of factors nondecreasing in the path of $y_i$, hence in the paths
of all the $\beta_k$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:assoc} for several paths gives
(i) and the doubling. The second factor of the doubling is at least
$\exp(-\sum_i\Gamma_i)$ where every content stays above $2t$ on
$[t/2,t]$, the rows of $K$ summing to one. Given $\mathcal G$ and the
contents' paths to $t/2$, $y_{j,t}$ is Gaussian of variance at least
$K_{jj}^2s_j^2t^3/24$. In (ii) every content's rate is kept above $3$
after time one, and in the deep estimate every one is kept below $t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[One tendency read in parts; {[P]}]\label{prop:readings}
Let $\mathcal W$ be a Brownian motion over the content, a centered
Gaussian family $\mathcal W_t(A)$, $A\subset\Omega$ measurable, with
$\E[\mathcal W_s(A)\mathcal W_t(A')]=(s\wedge t)\,\nu(A\cap A')$, and let
institution $j$ read the rate $\mu_j=\mu_{j,0}+\sigma\,\mathcal W(A_j)/m_j$,
the tendency averaged over its content.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item Each $\mu_j$ is a driftless Brownian rate, of volatility
$\sigma/\sqrt{m_j}$. The rates of institutions that do not overlap are
independent, and those of $j$ and $k$ have correlation
$r_{jk}=M_{jk}/\sqrt{m_jm_k}=\sqrt{\rho_{jk}\rho_{kj}}$.
\item Given that the rate of $j$ has moved by $\delta$ over an interval,
the movement of the rate of $k$ over it is Gaussian with mean
$\rho_{kj}\,\delta$ and with the share $1-r_{jk}^2$ of its unconditioned
variance: a movement of one institution shifts the law of another's and
determines nothing in it while $r_{jk}<1$.
\item The rates are combinations, with nonnegative coefficients, of the
independent Brownian motions $\mathcal W(C)$ over the atoms $C$ of the
partition that the labels generate, so that Lemma~\ref{lem:assoc} for
several paths applies to functionals nondecreasing in them.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Over an interval of length $\Delta$ the movements have variances
$\sigma^2\Delta/m_j$ and covariance $\sigma^2\Delta\,M_{jk}/m_jm_k$; the
regression coefficient of $k$ on $j$ is $M_{jk}/m_k=\rho_{kj}$ and the
residual variance is the share $1-r_{jk}^2$. $\mathcal W(A_j)$ is the sum
of the $\mathcal W(C)$ over the atoms in $A_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is modelled and what is proved; {[O]} for the exponent]\label{rem:domainsscope}
The content, the reading of the rates as parts of one tendency and the
listening law are proposals about the system; the statements above are
what follows from each. The survival exponent of the model with domains
is open [O]. A unit is kept safe along either of two routes, its domain
idle or its lead long, and a history may pass from one to the other, so
that the count of Remark~\ref{rem:addexp}, a quarter for the external
wall and a quarter for each unit, bounds the exponent from above and need
not equal it. With two domains at frozen background the local exponent of
survival in the run of Section~\ref{sec:exhibit} is $0.65$ between $T=4$
and $T=8$ and $0.57$ between $T=8$ and $T=16$, against $\tfrac34$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Shared loads: between separate units and one}\label{sec:sharing}

In \eqref{eq:domains} each unit bears its own load and nothing of
another's. A single unit at a pooled load is the opposite case. This
subsection gives the family between the two, in which the content of the
system bears blends of the loads; shows that the forms proposed for the
ground between are members of it; and says what a rule on a derivative
can fix and what it cannot.

\begin{definition}[Shared loads]\label{def:sharing}
Write $u_j=x_j^+/\tau_j$ for the load of domain $j$, $u=(u_1,\dots,u_m)$,
$\partial_j=\partial/\partial u_j$, and
$\Delta=\{\pi\in\R^m:\ \pi_j\ge0,\ \sum_j\pi_j=1\}$ for the simplex of
\emph{blends}. Let the content of Definition~\ref{def:quadrature} bear at
each point a blend of the loads, $u(\omega)=\sum_j\pi_j(\omega)\,u_j$ with
$\pi(\omega)\in\Delta$. The image of $\gamma\,d\nu$ under $\pi$ is a
finite measure $\mu$ on $\Delta$, the \emph{sharing measure}, and the
internal hazard is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharing}
h_\mu(u)=\int_\Delta\sinh^2\langle\pi,u\rangle\,\mu(d\pi).
\end{equation}
Its mass $\Gamma=\mu(\Delta)$ is the total amplitude,
$\Gamma_j=\int\pi_j\,d\mu$ the amplitude of $j$, and
$\bar u=\sum_j\Gamma_ju_j/\Gamma$ the \emph{pooled load}. For a pair $i,j$
write $\partial_q=\tfrac12(\partial_i+\partial_j)$ and
$\partial_d=\tfrac12(\partial_j-\partial_i)$ for the derivatives along the
pair's sum and along its difference, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distinct}
\kappa_{ij}(u)=\frac{\partial_d^2h_\mu(u)}{\partial_q^2h_\mu(u)}
\end{equation}
for the \emph{distinctness} of the pair at the loads $u$. The content is
integrated once in \eqref{eq:sharing}, so that no weights of the kind of
\eqref{eq:weights} enter.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The forms it contains; {[P]}]\label{prop:forms}
Each of the following is $h_\mu$ for the measure named, $\delta_\pi$
being the unit mass at the blend $\pi$ and $e_j$ the $j$th vertex of
$\Delta$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Separate units.} $\mu=\sum_j\Gamma_j\delta_{e_j}$ gives
$\sum_j\Gamma_j\sinh^2u_j$, the internal channel of \eqref{eq:domains}.
\item \emph{One merged unit.} $\mu=\Gamma\delta_{\pi^\ast}$ with
$\pi^\ast_j=\Gamma_j/\Gamma$ gives $\Gamma\sinh^2\bar u$.
\item \emph{The mixture.} For $0\le\varphi\le1$,
$\mu=\varphi\sum_j\Gamma_j\delta_{e_j}+(1-\varphi)\Gamma\delta_{\pi^\ast}$
gives
$\varphi\sum_j\Gamma_j\sinh^2u_j+(1-\varphi)\,\Gamma\sinh^2\bar u$: a
share $\varphi$ of every unit standing apart and the rest standing as
one. For $m=2$ and equal amplitudes,
$\kappa/(1-\kappa)=\frac{\varphi}{1-\varphi}\cosh(u_2-u_1)$: the
distinctness is $\varphi$ at equal loads and tends to one as the loads
part.
\item \emph{Pooled units.} For a matrix $S$ with nonnegative entries and
rows summing to one and $a_i\ge0$, $\mu=\sum_ia_i\delta_{S_{i\cdot}}$
gives $\sum_ia_i\sinh^2(Su)_i$, units that each bear a blend of the
loads, with $\Gamma_j=\sum_ia_iS_{ij}$. It is the form of the labels of
Definition~\ref{def:labels} when content read by several of them bears
the mean of their loads and the unit of each label is taken at the mean
load of its content: then
$S_{ij}=m_i^{-1}\int_{A_i\cap A_j}n^{-1}\,d\nu$, $n(\omega)$ being the
number of labels that read $\omega$; $S_{ij}=\tfrac12\rho_{ij}$ for
$i\ne j$ where no content is read by three labels; for two labels the
difference of the loads read is $1-\tfrac12(\rho_{12}+\rho_{21})$ times
that of the loads borne; and for two labels of equal mass and amplitude
and overlap $\rho$, $\kappa=(1-\rho)^2$ at all loads.
\item \emph{The powerset form.} Let $\nu_Q\ge0$ for every nonempty set $Q$
of domains, and let $b_Q$ be the barycenter of the face of $\Delta$ that
$Q$ spans. Then $\mu=\sum_Q\nu_Q\delta_{b_Q}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:powerset}
\sum_{\varnothing\ne Q\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}}\nu_Q\,\sinh^2\bar u_Q,
\qquad\bar u_Q=\frac1{|Q|}\sum_{j\in Q}u_j,
\end{equation}
one term for every set of domains, at the mean load of the set. It is the
form of the labels of Definition~\ref{def:labels} when content read by
several of them bears the mean of their loads: $\nu_Q$ is the amplitude
of the content read by exactly the labels in $Q$, and with
$N_T=\int_{\bigcap_{j\in T}A_j}\gamma\,d\nu$ the amplitude of what the
labels in $T$ read in common,
$\nu_Q=\sum_{T\supseteq Q}(-1)^{|T|-|Q|}N_T$. For two labels
$\kappa=(\nu_{\{1\}}+\nu_{\{2\}})/(\nu_{\{1\}}+\nu_{\{2\}}+\nu_{\{1,2\}})$
at equal loads, the share of their content that one of them alone reads.
Where each point of the content is read by each label independently with
probability $p$, $\nu_Q$ is proportional to $p^{|Q|}(1-p)^{m-|Q|}$, every
overlap $\rho_{jk}$ is $p$, and \eqref{eq:powerset} is a binomial sum
over the sizes of the sets.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By substitution in \eqref{eq:sharing} and \eqref{eq:distinct}, with
\eqref{eq:hessian} below for the distinctness. In (iii) with two units of
amplitude $a$ and $\delta=u_2-u_1$, $\partial_d^2h_\mu=\tfrac12\varphi
a(\cosh2u_1+\cosh2u_2)=\varphi a\cosh2\bar u\cosh\delta$ and
$\partial_q^2h_\mu$ exceeds it by $(1-\varphi)\,a\cosh2\bar u$. In (iv)
the mean over $A_i$ of the load of the content is $(Su)_i$,
$(Su)_2-(Su)_1=(1-S_{12}-S_{21})(u_2-u_1)$, and with equal masses
$\partial_d(Su)_{1,2}=\mp\tfrac12(1-\rho)$ and
$\partial_q(Su)_{1,2}=\tfrac12$. In (v) $N_T=\sum_{Q\supseteq T}\nu_Q$,
and the formula is its inversion over the lattice of subsets; under
independent reading the content read by exactly $Q$ has mass
proportional to $p^{|Q|}(1-p)^{m-|Q|}$, and a point read by $j$ is read
by $k$ with probability $p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[What every sharing measure gives; {[P]}]\label{prop:sharing}
For every sharing measure $\mu$ and all loads $u\ge0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item $\Gamma\sinh^2\bar u\ \le\ h_\mu(u)\ \le\ \sum_j\Gamma_j\sinh^2u_j$.
The hazard lies between that of the merged unit and that of the separate
units of the same amplitudes.
\item $\partial_jh_\mu=\int\pi_j\sinh(2\langle\pi,u\rangle)\,d\mu\ge0$:
the hazard is nondecreasing in every load, hence nonincreasing in every
scalar.
\item \emph{One law along the common direction.} With
$D=\sum_j\partial_j$, $D^2h_\mu=4h_\mu+2\Gamma$, and
$h_\mu(u+t\mathbf 1)=(h_\mu(u)+\tfrac12\Gamma)\cosh2t+\tfrac12Dh_\mu(u)\sinh2t-\tfrac12\Gamma$:
all the loads raised together, the hazard moves as a single unit's does,
whatever the measure.
\item \emph{Sameness is in the second derivatives.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hessian}
\partial_i\partial_jh_\mu=2\int\pi_i\pi_j\cosh(2\langle\pi,u\rangle)\,d\mu,
\qquad
\partial_q^2h_\mu-\partial_d^2h_\mu=\partial_i\partial_jh_\mu.
\end{equation}
The matrix of second derivatives has nonnegative entries and is
nonnegative definite. Its entry for $i\ne j$ vanishes, at one $u$ or at
all, exactly when $\mu(\pi_i\pi_j>0)=0$, no content bearing both loads.
Where $\mu(\pi_i+\pi_j>0)>0$, $0\le\kappa_{ij}\le1$, with $\kappa_{ij}=1$
exactly when no content bears both loads and $\kappa_{ij}=0$ exactly when
$\pi_i=\pi_j$ $\mu$-almost everywhere.
\item \emph{Small loads.} $h_\mu(u)=u^{\!\top}Gu+O(|u|^4)$ with
$G_{ij}=\int\pi_i\pi_j\,d\mu\ge0$ and $\sum_jG_{ij}=\Gamma_i$: a quadratic
form in the loads, fixed by a matrix of pairs.
\item \emph{Large loads.} For $v\ge0$ with
$M(v):=\max\{\langle\pi,v\rangle:\pi\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\}>0$,
$\frac1{2t}\log h_\mu(tv)\to M(v)$ as $t\to\infty$: the hazard is carried
by the content whose blend is loaded most, $\max_jv_j$ for separate
units and $\langle\pi^\ast,v\rangle$ for the merged one.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\sinh^2$ is convex and $\pi\in\Delta$, so
$\sinh^2\langle\pi,u\rangle\le\sum_j\pi_j\sinh^2u_j$, and integrating
gives the upper bound of (i); the lower is Jensen's inequality for the
probability $\mu/\Gamma$, the mean of $\langle\pi,u\rangle$ under it
being $\bar u$. (ii) and \eqref{eq:hessian} are differentiation under the
integral, and
$\tfrac14[(\partial_i+\partial_j)^2-(\partial_j-\partial_i)^2]=\partial_i\partial_j$.
(iii) $D\langle\pi,u\rangle=\sum_j\pi_j=1$ and
$(\sinh^2)''=2\cosh2z=4\sinh^2z+2$; $g(t)=h_\mu(u+t\mathbf 1)$ then solves
$g''=4g+2\Gamma$. (iv) The matrix is the Gram matrix of the functions
$\pi_i$ in $L^2$ of the measure $2\cosh(2\langle\pi,u\rangle)\,d\mu$,
whose density is at least two. Further
$\partial_d^2h_\mu=\tfrac12\int(\pi_j-\pi_i)^2\cosh(2\langle\pi,u\rangle)\,d\mu$,
$\partial_q^2h_\mu$ is the same with $(\pi_i+\pi_j)^2$, and
$(\pi_j-\pi_i)^2\le(\pi_i+\pi_j)^2$ with equality exactly where
$\pi_i\pi_j=0$. (v) $\sinh^2z=z^2+O(z^4)$ and $\sum_j\pi_i\pi_j=\pi_i$.
(vi) $\sinh^2z\le\tfrac14e^{2z}$ gives
$h_\mu(tv)\le\tfrac14\Gamma e^{2tM(v)}$; and for $0<\varepsilon<M(v)$ the
set $\{\langle\pi,v\rangle>M(v)-\varepsilon\}$ is open and meets the
support, so it has a mass $c_\varepsilon>0$ and
$h_\mu(tv)\ge c_\varepsilon\sinh^2(t(M(v)-\varepsilon))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The powerset form under refinement; {[P]}]\label{prop:refine}
Let domain $j$ be split into $n_j$ parts, each carrying the load $u_j$.
The powerset form \eqref{eq:powerset} of the refined family is a sharing
measure of the original one, with an atom at the blend
$\pi=(k_j/\sum_ik_i)_j$ for each set that holds $k_j$ parts of $j$; every
point of $\Delta$ with rational coordinates is such a blend for some
refinement. Hence for every sharing measure $\mu$ and every $R>0$ there
are powerset forms of refinements whose hazards converge to $h_\mu$
uniformly on $\{0\le u_j\le R\}$. The powerset form is not a smaller
family than \eqref{eq:sharing}: it is that family at a finite resolution.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The mean load of a set holding $k_j$ parts of $j$ is
$\sum_jk_ju_j/\sum_jk_j$. For the convergence, cut $\Delta$ into finitely
many cells of diameter at most $\tfrac12\epsilon$ in the norm
$\sum_j|\pi_j|$ and move the mass of each cell to a rational point of
$\Delta$ within $\tfrac12\epsilon$ of the cell; the finitely many points
used are blends of one refinement. Where
$0\le u_j\le R$, $|\langle\pi,u\rangle-\langle\pi',u\rangle|\le
R\sum_j|\pi_j-\pi'_j|$ and $|(\sinh^2)'|\le\sinh2R$ on $[0,R]$, so the
hazard moves by at most $\Gamma R\sinh(2R)\,\epsilon$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A rule on the derivative along a difference; {[P]}]\label{prop:pairrule}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Two units.} Let two units have the amplitude $a$, and write
$\bar u=\tfrac12(u_1+u_2)$ and $\delta=u_2-u_1$. Apart they are
$2a\sinh^2\bar u+a\cosh(2\bar u)(\cosh\delta-1)$, with derivative
$a\cosh(2\bar u)\sinh\delta$ in $\delta$ at fixed $\bar u$. Let $f$ be
odd and continuous with $0\le f(s)\le s$ for $s\ge0$. The hazard that is
the merged unit at $\delta=0$ and whose derivative in $\delta$ is that of
the separate units taken at the imbalance $f(\delta)$,
$a\cosh(2\bar u)\sinh f(\delta)$, is
\[
H_f=2a\sinh^2\bar u+a\cosh(2\bar u)\int_0^\delta\sinh f(s)\,ds,
\]
and it lies between the merged unit and the separate units. $f(s)=s$
gives the separate units and $f=0$ the merged one;
$\sinh f=\varphi\sinh\delta$ gives the mixture of
Proposition~\ref{prop:forms}(iii);
$\sinh f=\varphi\sinh(\varphi\delta)$ gives two pooled units at the loads
$\bar u\pm\tfrac12\varphi\delta$; and $f=\varphi\delta$ gives
$2a\sinh^2\bar u+\varphi^{-1}a\cosh(2\bar u)(\cosh\varphi\delta-1)$, those
two pooled units taken $\varphi^{-1}$ times with the merged unit taken
$1-\varphi^{-1}$ times. With unequal amplitudes the imbalance has to be
taken at the pooled load fixed: at the plain sum fixed the rule with
$f=0$ keeps the term $\tfrac12(a_2-a_1)\sinh(u_1+u_2)\,(u_2-u_1)$, under
which the hazard is negative at the amplitudes $1$ and $3$ and the loads
$1$ and $0$.
\item \emph{Three or more.} Let $m\ge3$, and for a pair write
$v_{ij}=(\Gamma_i\partial_j-\Gamma_j\partial_i)/(\Gamma_i+\Gamma_j)$, the
derivative along the pair's difference at its pooled load fixed, which is
$\partial_d$ for equal amplitudes. Let $H$ be twice continuously
differentiable in the loads, and for every pair of a set of pairs that
links all the domains let
$v_{ij}H=\varphi_{ij}\,v_{ij}\bigl(\sum_k\Gamma_k\sinh^2u_k\bigr)$ with
a constant $\varphi_{ij}$. Then the $\varphi_{ij}$ are all equal, to
$\varphi$ say, and $H=\varphi\sum_k\Gamma_k\sinh^2u_k+G(\bar u)$ for a
function $G$; if $H$ is the merged unit where the loads are all equal,
$H$ is the mixture of Proposition~\ref{prop:forms}(iii). With equal
amplitudes $a$ the same holds if instead
$\partial_dH=a\cosh(u_i+u_j)\sinh f_{ij}(u_j-u_i)$ with $f_{ij}$ twice
differentiable and $f_{ij}(0)=0$: then $\sinh f_{ij}=\varphi\sinh$ for
one $\varphi$.
\end{enumerate}
A rule that fixes the response of each pair to its own imbalance from
that pair's loads alone thus gives, for two units, the whole family
between one unit and two, and for three or more leaves one number free
for the system. Under \eqref{eq:sharing},
$\partial_dh_\mu=\tfrac12\int(\pi_j-\pi_i)\sinh(2\langle\pi,u\rangle)\,d\mu$
depends on every load borne by content that bears $u_i$ or $u_j$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $a\sinh^2(\bar u-\tfrac12\delta)+a\sinh^2(\bar u+\tfrac12\delta)=a(\cosh2\bar u\cosh\delta-1)$.
Integrating the derivative from $\delta=0$ gives $H_f$, and
$0\le\int_0^\delta\sinh f\le\cosh\delta-1$. The cases named are
$\int_0^\delta\sinh f=\cosh\delta-1$, $0$, $\varphi(\cosh\delta-1)$,
$\cosh\varphi\delta-1$ and $\varphi^{-1}(\cosh\varphi\delta-1)$, and
$a\cosh(2\bar u)(\cosh\varphi\delta-1)$ is two units at
$\bar u\pm\tfrac12\varphi\delta$ less the merged unit, by the same
identity. For the last statement the derivative of the
separate units along $\tfrac12(\partial_2-\partial_1)$ is
$\tfrac12(a_2\sinh2u_2-a_1\sinh2u_1)$, which at $f=0$ is
$\tfrac12(a_2-a_1)\sinh(u_1+u_2)$. (ii) Write
$H^{\mathrm s}=\sum_k\Gamma_k\sinh^2u_k$. The $v_{ij}$ have constant
coefficients and commute. For two pairs $ij$ and $jk$ of the set,
$v_{jk}v_{ij}H=\varphi_{ij}\,v_{jk}v_{ij}H^{\mathrm s}$ and
$v_{ij}v_{jk}H=\varphi_{jk}\,v_{ij}v_{jk}H^{\mathrm s}$, and
$v_{ij}v_{jk}H^{\mathrm s}=-2\Gamma_i\Gamma_j\Gamma_k\cosh(2u_j)/(\Gamma_i+\Gamma_j)(\Gamma_j+\Gamma_k)\ne0$,
$H^{\mathrm s}$ having no mixed derivative and the two operators sharing
the variable $u_j$ alone; so $\varphi_{ij}=\varphi_{jk}$, and by the
linking all are equal. Then $v_{ij}(H-\varphi H^{\mathrm s})=0$ for pairs
whose directions $\Gamma_ie_j-\Gamma_je_i$ span the hyperplane orthogonal
to $(\Gamma_k)_k$, on whose translates $\bar u$ is constant. For the
second form put $g_{ij}=\sinh\circ f_{ij}$ and
$P_{ij}=a\cosh(u_i+u_j)\,g_{ij}(u_j-u_i)$. For pairs $ij$ and $jk$ the
equality of the mixed derivatives is
$-\partial_jP_{ij}(u_i,u_j)=\partial_jP_{jk}(u_j,u_k)$, so that each side
is a function of $u_j$ alone and $\partial_i\partial_jP_{ij}=0$. In
$q=u_i+u_j$ and $d=u_j-u_i$,
$\partial_i\partial_j=\partial^2/\partial q^2-\partial^2/\partial d^2$
and the condition is $\cosh q\,(g_{ij}-g_{ij}'')=0$: $g_{ij}=\varphi_{ij}\sinh$.
Then $\partial_jP_{ij}=a\varphi_{ij}\cosh2u_j$ and
$\partial_jP_{jk}=-a\varphi_{jk}\cosh2u_j$, the constants agree, and the
first form applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is modelled; {[A]}, and {[O]} for the leads]\label{rem:sharingscope}
That the content bears blends of the loads, and which measure says how,
are proposals about the system [A]; the propositions are what follows
for every measure. The blend is taken in the loads. With one strength the
content at the blend $\pi$ then has the scalar
$(\sum_j\pi_j/\tau_j)^{-1}$, a harmonic mean of the scalars, where the
listening law of Proposition~\ref{prop:listening} takes a geometric one;
the two agree to first order in the spread of the scalars. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharing}(ii) the survival weight is nondecreasing
in the path of every anatomy rate, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:pacedomains}(i) holds for \eqref{eq:sharing} with the
same proof: the filtering raises the technoanatomies jointly under any
sharing. The bounds on the leads, Theorem~\ref{thm:pacedomains}(ii),
(iii) and (v), are not established under sharing [O]: content at the
blend $\pi$ answers to $\pi_ju_j$ and not to $u_j$, and where no content
bears the load of $j$ alone the constants at least change. No run has
been made with shared loads.
\end{remark}

\section{Conditioning on survival}\label{sec:cond}

\begin{definition}[Survivorship interpolation; bias field]
\label{def:interp}
$\PP^{(t)}:=\PP(\cdot\mid T>t)$, likewise $\PP^{(t)}_\tau$ for the
hard wall; for $0\le s<t$ the bias field on $[0,s]$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bias}
\beta_t(s):=\frac{d\PP^{(t)}}{d\PP}\Big|_{\Fc_s}
=\one_{\{T>s\}}\,\frac{\PP_{X_s}(T>t-s)}{\PP_{X_0}(T>t)},
\qquad X_s=(\mu_s,D_s).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Survivorship tilts upward; {[P]}]\label{lem:monotone}
Coupling two initial data componentwise with the same $W$ shows both
$\PP_x(T>t)$ and $\PP_x(\tau>t)$ nondecreasing in $(\mu_0,D_0)$;
hence every bias field is, on the survival event, a nondecreasing
function of the state: the interpolation is a monotone flow of upward
tilts in rate and credit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[The critical limit is the pure Doob transform;
{[C]}, soft version {[A]}]\label{thm:Q}
Under the refined asymptotics of Theorem~\ref{thm:persist}, for every
$s\ge0$ and bounded $\Fc_s$-measurable $\Psi$,
$\E[\Psi\mid\tau>t]\to\E_Q[\Psi]$ as $t\to\infty$, where $Q$ is the
time-homogeneous Markov law with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qdensity}
\frac{dQ}{d\PP}\Big|_{\Fc_s}
=\one_{\{\tau>s\}}\,\frac{\hm(X_s)}{\hm(X_0)}:
\end{equation}
the pure Doob $\hm$-transform at eigenvalue one. Under
Ansatz~\ref{ans:ratio}, and conditionally on it, the soft
survivorship law $\PP(\cdot\mid T>t)$ converges to the Feynman--Kac
Doob transform
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qsoft}
\frac{dQ^{\mathrm{soft}}}{d\PP}\Big|_{\Fc_s}
=e^{-\int_0^sq(X_u)du}\,\frac{g(X_s)}{g(X_0)},
\end{equation}
which does not coincide with \eqref{eq:Qdensity} even when $g$ is proportional to $\hm$ after a credit translation: the hard density vanishes on paths crossing the wall while the soft density remains positive on finite paths with finite accumulated killing and positive $g$, so the two conditioned laws have different supports and are distinct laws. Ansatz~\ref{ans:soft}
alone yields neither the limit nor its existence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $f_t:=\one_{\{\tau>s\}}\PP_{X_s}(\tau>t-s)/\PP_{X_0}(\tau>t)$,
the Markov property gives $\E[f_t]=1$; factorizing,
$f_t=\one_{\{\tau>s\}}
\frac{(t-s)^{1/4}\PP_{X_s}(\tau>t-s)}{t^{1/4}\PP_{X_0}(\tau>t)}
(1-s/t)^{-1/4}\to f:=\one_{\{\tau>s\}}\hm(X_s)/\hm(X_0)$ a.s.; the
martingale property gives $\E[f]=1$, so $f_t\to f$ in $L^1$ by
Scheff\'e, and $\E[\Psi f_t]\to\E_Q[\Psi]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why survivorship has no clock here; {[P]}]
\label{rem:noclock}
For regularly varying tails the kinematic factor
$\PP_x(\tau>t-s)/\PP_x(\tau>t)\to1$ for fixed $s$: the age of the
conditioning drops out and the limit is an un-tilted $h$-transform.
For exponential tails the same factor converges to $e^{\theta s}$ and
survivorship acquires a clock. In the penalization taxonomy
\cite{RVY06} the survival weight lands in the clockless class
precisely because its expectation is regularly varying. Quantitatively
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
\beta_t(s)=\one_{\{\tau>s\}}\frac{\hm(X_s)}{\hm(X_0)}
\Bigl(1-\frac st\Bigr)^{-1/4}(1+o(1)),
\qquad
\Bigl(1-\frac st\Bigr)^{-1/4}=1+\frac{s}{4t}+O\Bigl(\frac{s^2}{t^2}\Bigr):
\end{equation}
the persistence exponent is the linear-response coefficient of
survivorship bias.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Survivorship drift; promotion to transience;
{[P]} given {[C]} inputs]\label{thm:drift}
Under $Q$ the state solves
$d\mu_t=\sigma^2\partial_\mu\log\hm(\mu_t,D_t)\,dt+\sigma\,dW^Q_t$,
$dD_t=\mu_t\,dt$: conditioning adds drift only in the noisy
coordinate, the drift is everywhere nonnegative, equals
$\sigma^2/2\mu$ near zero credit (the Bessel-$2$ form, the halving of
the classical exponent under one integration), and vanishes as
$\kappa:=\sigma^2D/\mu^3\to\infty$. Under $\PP$ the credit is oscillatory,
$\liminf D_s=-\infty$ and $\limsup D_s=+\infty$ (recurrence
classifications belong to the Markov pair, and $D$ alone is not
Markov); under $Q$ it is transient, $D_s\to\infty$ a.s.: the
P\'olya $2\mathrm D\to3\mathrm D$ transition with no literal change of
dimension anywhere --- survivorship alters the recurrence class of the
ensemble, not the state dynamics.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Locally ours, globally elsewhere; {[P/C]}]
\label{thm:singular}
$Q(\tau=\infty)=1$; $\hm(X_s)\to\infty$ and $D_s\to\infty$ $Q$-a.s.
\cite{GJW99}; $\liminf_sD_s=-\infty$ $\PP$-a.s. Hence
$Q|_{\Fc_s}\ll\PP|_{\Fc_s}$ for every finite $s$, with the explicit
density \eqref{eq:Qdensity} and mutual absolute continuity on the
survival event, while $Q\perp\PP$ on $\Fc_\infty$: the survivor's
law is locally equivalent to the unconditioned one on survivors and
globally disjoint from it, the exit from the measure class completed
only at $t=\infty$. Consequently no event of bounded duration perfectly separates the
conditioned law from the unconditioned one: none has probability one
under one law and zero under the other on their common survival
support. Mutually absolutely continuous laws can still be far apart
statistically, so this is a statement about certification and not
about discrimination.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[The limit law at a fixed age; {[P]} given {[C]} inputs]\label{prop:limitage}
In the driftless model with the hard wall, under the asymptotics of
Theorem~\ref{thm:persist}, let $Q$ be the limit law of
Theorem~\ref{thm:Q}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{The survivors to $s$, reweighted.} On $\Fc_s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limitage}
\frac{dQ}{d\PP^{(s)}_\tau}=\frac{\hm(X_s)}{\E[\hm(X_s)\mid\tau>s]},
\qquad
\E[\hm(X_s)\mid\tau>s]=\frac{\hm(X_0)}{\PP(\tau>s)}:
\end{equation}
the limit law at age $s$ is the law of the survivors to $s$, each
weighted by $\hm$ at its state, a weight nondecreasing in the rate and in
the credit (Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone}).
\item \emph{Scaling.} For $\lambda>0$,
$\hm(\lambda\mu,\lambda^3D)=\lambda^{1/2}\hm(\mu,D)$, and under $Q$
started at $(\mu_0,D_0)$ the process
$(\lambda^{-1}\mu_{\lambda^2s},\lambda^{-3}D_{\lambda^2s})_{s\ge0}$ has the
law $Q$ started at $(\mu_0/\lambda,D_0/\lambda^3)$. Hence
$(\mu_s/s^{1/2},D_s/s^{3/2})$ has the law of $(\mu_1,D_1)$ under $Q$
started at $(\mu_0/s^{1/2},D_0/s^{3/2})$, and for the process started at
the origin its law does not depend on $s$ \cite{GJW99}: under the limit
law the rate is of order $\sigma s^{1/2}$ and the credit of order
$\sigma s^{3/2}$.
\item \emph{The added drift.} For $\mu>0$ the drift of
Theorem~\ref{thm:drift} is $\sigma^2\mu^{-1}\psi(\sigma^2D/\mu^3)$, with
$\psi$ continuous and bounded, $\psi(0^+)=\tfrac12$ and
$\psi(\kappa)\to0$ as $\kappa\to\infty$. For the process started at the
origin the drift at age $s$ is $\sigma s^{-1/2}$ times a variable whose
law does not depend on $s$. Under $Q$, $\mu_t=\mu_0+\sigma W^Q_t+A_t$ with
$A$ nondecreasing: from a rate $m>0$ at any time, the rate keeps its sign
for a further time $u$ with probability at least
$\operatorname{erf}\bigl(m/\sigma\sqrt{2u}\bigr)$, its value for the
unconditioned rate.
\item \emph{Near the wall.} For the process started at the origin with
$\sigma=1$, and $k>0$, $0<\alpha<\tfrac32$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nearwall}
Q\bigl(D_s<ks^{\alpha}\bigr)\sim c_0\,k^{5/3}\,s^{5\alpha/3-5/2}
\qquad(s\to\infty)
\end{equation}
with a constant $c_0>0$, and the expected time the credit spends below
the curve $ks^{\alpha}$ is finite for $\alpha<\tfrac9{10}$ and infinite
for $\alpha\ge\tfrac9{10}$ \cite{GJW99}. With (ii),
$Q(D_s<\varepsilon s^{3/2})\sim c_0\varepsilon^{5/3}$ as
$\varepsilon\to0$, at every age.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) On $\Fc_s$, $dQ/d\PP=\one_{\{\tau>s\}}\hm(X_s)/\hm(X_0)$ by
\eqref{eq:Qdensity} and
$d\PP^{(s)}_\tau/d\PP=\one_{\{\tau>s\}}/\PP(\tau>s)$; the quotient has
$\PP^{(s)}_\tau$-expectation one. (ii) By Brownian scaling
$\PP_{(\lambda\mu,\lambda^3D)}(\tau>\lambda^2t)=\PP_{(\mu,D)}(\tau>t)$,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:persist} on each side gives
$\hm(\lambda\mu,\lambda^3D)\,\lambda^{-1/2}=\hm(\mu,D)$. The killed
transition kernel is covariant under the same scaling and the ratio
$\hm(X_s)/\hm(X_0)$ of \eqref{eq:Qdensity} is invariant under it, which
is the scaling of $Q$; take $\lambda=s^{1/2}$. (iii) By (ii),
$\mu\,\partial_\mu\log\hm$ is invariant under
$(\mu,D)\mapsto(\lambda\mu,\lambda^3D)$. It is also invariant under
$(\mu,D,\sigma)\mapsto(c\mu,cD,c\sigma)$, which carries the model of
volatility $\sigma$ to that of volatility $c\sigma$ with the same
survival. For $\mu>0$ it is therefore a function $\psi$ of
$\sigma^2D/\mu^3$, continuous, with the limits stated in
Theorem~\ref{thm:drift}, hence bounded; and
$\partial_\mu\log\hm(\mu,D)=s^{-1/2}\,\partial_\mu\log\hm(\mu/s^{1/2},D/s^{3/2})$,
which with (ii) gives the drift at age $s$. The drift being nonnegative,
$A_t=\int_0^t\sigma^2\partial_\mu\log\hm(X_r)\,dr$ is nondecreasing, so
that $\mu_{t+r}\ge m+\sigma(W^Q_{t+r}-W^Q_t)$ for $r\ge0$, and a Brownian
motion started at $m$ stays positive to time $u$ with probability
$\operatorname{erf}(m/\sigma\sqrt{2u})$. (iv) The two statements are
those of \cite{GJW99}; by (ii) the law of $D_s/s^{3/2}$ is that of $D_1$,
and \eqref{eq:nearwall} with $\varepsilon=ks^{\alpha-3/2}\to0$ is the
last.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Three canonical survivorship regimes; {[P/C/S]}]
\label{thm:modes}
Three tail classes of the driver produce three regimes of the
interpolation, canonical rather than exhaustive: stretched-exponential
and other subexponential tails, and regularly varying tails of
exponent other than $1/4$, fall under the same categories at the
level of the survival weight's behavior in the penalization taxonomy
but are not treated here. \emph{(I) Saturation} [P] (favorable:
$\PP(T=\infty)>0$): $\|\PP^{(t)}-\PP(\cdot\mid
T=\infty)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\to0$ with every bias bounded by
$1/\PP(T=\infty)$; no new measure class. \emph{(II) Transformation}
[C/A] (regularly varying tails that also admit a statewise ratio
asymptotic, $\PP_x(T>t)\sim g(x)L(t)t^{-\alpha}$; regular variation of
the scalar tail alone does not determine the state-dependent ratio):
the clockless Doob or Feynman--Kac transform by $g$, locally absolutely
continuous, globally singular, with its kinematic factor; the hard-wall
case $\alpha=1/4$, $g=\hm$, is the proved instance
(Theorem~\ref{thm:Q}) and the soft case is conditional on
Ansatz~\ref{ans:ratio}. \emph{(III) Concentration} [S]
(exponential tails, $\PP(T>t)\sim e^{-\theta t}$): on fixed windows
the eigen-tilted transform, the quasi-stationary regime with a clock;
for the linearly adverse driver $d\mu=\nu\,dt+\sigma\,dW$ with
$\nu<0$ the clock is $\theta=3\nu^2/8\sigma^2$ and, at macroscopic
scale, the conditioned path law collapses onto the deterministic
optimal profile $\mu_{tv}/t\to\tfrac{|\nu|}4v(2-3v)$, a law of large
numbers for the rare, a large-deviation computation for the linearly
adverse driver that is stated here at scaling level and not derived. Modes (II) and (III) share $\PP(\Imm)=0$ and are separated by
the interpolation --- by whether the survivor's measure class is
approached polynomially, without a clock, or exponentially, with one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Survivorship conditioning selects any linearly
growing hazard gap; {[P]}]\label{prop:select}
Let the histories split into two classes, $C_1$ of prior weight
$\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $C_0$ of weight $1-\alpha$, whose hazards are
realized on one probability space, with cumulative hazards
$\Lambda^{(1)},\Lambda^{(0)}$ and class survival functions
$S_j(t)=\E\bigl[e^{-\Lambda^{(j)}_t}\bigr]$ as in the Cox construction,
and suppose that for some $\Delta>0$ and $K<\infty$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
\Lambda^{(0)}_t-\Lambda^{(1)}_t\;\ge\;\Delta t-K
\qquad\text{for all }t\ge0,\ \text{almost surely}.
\end{equation}
Then the odds of $C_0$ against $C_1$ under the interpolation satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selectodds}
O(\eta):=\frac{\PP(C_0\mid T>\eta)}{\PP(C_1\mid T>\eta)}
\;\le\;\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\,e^{K-\Delta\eta},
\end{equation}
so that $O(\eta)<\beta$ for every $\eta>\eta_\ast:=\Delta^{-1}
\bigl[\log\tfrac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}+\log\tfrac1\beta+K\bigr]$, which is
finite for every $\alpha>0$, and $\PP(C_1\mid T>\eta)\to1$ as
$\eta\to\infty$. The bound holds with equality for constant hazards
differing by $\Delta$ with $K=0$. In particular \eqref{eq:gap} holds with
$\Delta=\delta h_{\min}$ when
$\Lambda^{(1)}\le(1-\delta)\Lambda^{(0)}+K$ for some $\delta\in(0,1]$
and the hazard of $C_0$ has a floor $h_{\min}>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:gap}, $e^{-\Lambda^{(0)}_t}\le e^{K-\Delta t}\,
e^{-\Lambda^{(1)}_t}$ on every path; taking expectations,
$S_0(t)\le e^{K-\Delta t}S_1(t)$, and by Bayes
$O(\eta)=(1-\alpha)S_0(\eta)/\bigl(\alpha S_1(\eta)\bigr)$. In the
fractional case $\Lambda^{(0)}_t-\Lambda^{(1)}_t\ge\delta\Lambda^{(0)}_t-K
\ge\delta h_{\min}t-K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the hypothesis needs; the two-channel instance

\begin{remark}[The conditioned law read as stated content]
\label{rem:qcontent}
Each horizon event $\{T>t\}$ has positive probability, and the limit of
Theorem~\ref{thm:Q} is the law that has absorbed every one of them. Read
not as a limit of laws but as the content of an information state, it
states survival through every horizon at once, every other attribute
blank: in set notation $\bigcap_t\{T>t\}=\{T=\infty\}$, null in the
critical regime, which is why it is reached through the interpolation
and never conditioned on as an event. The larger program in which these
results are interpreted, whose vocabulary this paper otherwise does not
use, calls that content the Golden Aethus at its primitive, and takes
as its Golden Axiom that it is stated in one's own information state:
an exposed postulate, carried in the downstream companions at the grade
of an open hypothesis {[O]} and nowhere asserted or used here.
Theorem~\ref{thm:singular} says what it would cost, since no event of
bounded duration separates the conditioned law from the unconditioned
one with certainty, and no finite record could therefore certify the
statedness; Proposition~\ref{prop:select} says what it would buy, since
under the conditioning any class whose hazard sits linearly below
another's takes all of the weight.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Which channel carries the hazard; {[P]}]\label{prop:carry}
In the two-channel model at a strength $x>0$, the background standing at
$B$, write $h^{\mathrm{ext}}=B\operatorname{sech}^2x$ and
$h_j=\Gamma_j\sinh^2(x/\tau_j)$, with loads $u_j=x/\tau_j$ and leads
$\ell_j=\log(\tau_j/x)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{The least margin.} $Be^{-2x}\le h^{\mathrm{ext}}<4Be^{-2x}$,
and for $\ell_j\ge0$,
$\Gamma_je^{-2\ell_j}\le h_j<2\Gamma_je^{-2\ell_j}$. With every lead
nonnegative the hazard therefore lies between $\mathsf M$ and
$4(1+m)\mathsf M$,
$\mathsf M=\max\{Be^{-2x},\Gamma_1e^{-2\ell_1},\dots,\Gamma_me^{-2\ell_m}\}$:
it is set by the least of the strength and the leads, each offset by half
the logarithm of its amplitude.
\item \emph{A race of lead against strength.} The unit carries more
hazard than the external channel if $\ell_j\le x+\tfrac12\log(\Gamma_j/4B)$
and less if $\ell_j\ge0$ and $\ell_j\ge x+\tfrac12\log(2\Gamma_j/B)$. The internal hazard
stays within the order of the external one only where the lead keeps
level with the strength itself, the scalar exceeding the strength by a
factor of order $e^{x}$.
\item \emph{What each improvement is worth.}
\[
\frac{\partial h}{\partial\log\tau_j}=-2u_j\coth(u_j)\,h_j<0,\qquad
\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}=-2\tanh(x)\,h^{\mathrm{ext}}+\frac1x\sum_j2u_j\coth(u_j)\,h_j .
\]
A longer technoanatomy always lowers the hazard, in proportion to the
hazard its unit carries. Strength lowers it, the scalars held, exactly
when $x\tanh(x)\,h^{\mathrm{ext}}>\sum_ju_j\coth(u_j)\,h_j$, which at small
loads reads $h^{\mathrm{int}}<x\tanh(x)\,h^{\mathrm{ext}}$: further strength
is worth having only while the internal hazard is below about $x$ times
the external one, and at the strength of least hazard for given scalars
the two stand in that ratio. In the lead form of
Corollary~\ref{cor:lead}, the leads held,
$\partial h/\partial x=-2\tanh(x)\,h^{\mathrm{ext}}$ and strength always
lowers the hazard.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\operatorname{sech}^2x=4e^{-2x}/(1+e^{-2x})^2$ and the divisor lies in
$(1,4]$; for $0<u\le1$, $u^2\le\sinh^2u\le(\sinh1)^2u^2<2u^2$, and
$u_j=e^{-\ell_j}$. The hazard is a sum of $1+m$ terms. (ii) In the first
case $h_j\ge\Gamma_ju_j^2=\Gamma_je^{-2\ell_j}\ge4Be^{-2x}>h^{\mathrm{ext}}$,
whatever the sign of the lead; in the second
$h_j<2\Gamma_je^{-2\ell_j}\le Be^{-2x}\le h^{\mathrm{ext}}$. (iii) $\partial_x\sinh^2(x/\tau)=2\sinh u\cosh u/\tau$
and $\partial_y\sinh^2(xe^{-y})=-2u\sinh u\cosh u$, with
$\sinh u\cosh u=\coth(u)\sinh^2u$; and
$\partial_x\operatorname{sech}^2x=-2\tanh(x)\operatorname{sech}^2x$. In the
lead form the units do not depend on $x$ at fixed leads.
\end{proof}
